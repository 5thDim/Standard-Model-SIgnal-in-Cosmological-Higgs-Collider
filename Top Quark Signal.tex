\section{Top quark propagators and Yukawa vertices}
We calculate the Higgs four-point function generated by a top-quark box
in exact de Sitter space. The top mass is constant, the chemical potential
vanishes, and the external Higgs is a light spectator. 

\subsection{Higgs field and Majorana decomposition}
The metric and scale factor are
\begin{equation}
 \di s^2=a^2(\tau)(-\di\tau^2+\di\mb x^2),\qquad
 a(\tau)=-\frac1{H\tau},\qquad \tau<0.
\end{equation}
We take $\sigma^a=(\one,\bs)$ and $\bar\sigma^a=(\one,-\bs)$.
For $\mb H=(0,(h_0+\delta h)/\sqrt2)^T$, define
\begin{equation}
 g_t=\frac{y_t}{\sqrt2},\qquad m_t=g_th_0,\qquad
 \tn=\frac{m_t}{H},\qquad N_c=3.
\end{equation}
The notation follows the SMS manuscript: $\tn$ is the mass index,
$\aa,\bb=\pm1$ are SK labels, and $\eta=\pm1$ labels the nonlocal
branches. For the fermion, $\tn=m_t/H$; the vector relation
$\tn=\sqrt{m_V^2/H^2-1/4}$ does not apply here.
The massless Higgs bulk-to-boundary propagator is
\begin{equation}
 G_{\aa}(k;\tau)=\frac{H^2}{2k^3}(1-\ii \aa k\tau)e^{\ii \aa k\tau},\qquad \aa=\pm1.
 \label{eq:higgs}
\end{equation}
Here $m_H$ denotes the effective Higgs mass.
This prescription assumes $|\dot h_0|\ll H|h_0|$ and
$|m_H^2|\ll H^2$ over the relevant times~\cite{lu}.
The Higgs-to-curvature conversion is not included.

\paragraph{Spinor indices.}
\label{sec:spinor-indices}
The spinor indices are raised and lowered by
\begin{align}
 \epsilon_{12}=-\epsilon_{21}&=1,\qquad
 \epsilon^{12}=-\epsilon^{21}=-1,\nonumber\\
 \psi^\alpha&=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\psi_\beta,\qquad
 \psi_\alpha=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\psi^\beta,\qquad
 \epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\beta\gamma}=\delta^\alpha{}_{\gamma}.
 \label{eq:epsilon}
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
 \psi\chi\equiv\psi^\alpha\chi_\alpha
 =-\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\psi_\alpha\chi_\beta
 =\chi\psi,\qquad
 (\psi\psi)^\dagger
 =\psi^\dagger_{\dot\alpha}\psi^{\dagger\dot\alpha}
 =\epsilon^{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}
   \psi^\dagger_{\dot\alpha}\psi^\dagger_{\dot\beta}.
 \label{eq:spinor-bilinears}
\end{equation}
The symmetry $\psi\chi=\chi\psi$ uses both the Grassmann sign and the
antisymmetry of epsilon; Hermitian conjugation reverses operator order.

Let $t,t^c$ be the rescaled left-handed top and the left-handed charge
conjugate of the right-handed top, respectively. The latter carries the
conjugate gauge representation.
The rescaled Dirac-field action is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\ii t^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu t
 +\ii t^{c\dagger}\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu t^c
 -a(m_t+g_t\delta h)(tt^c+\mathrm{h.c.}).
 \label{eq:action-dirac}
\end{equation}

Our field redefinition is
\begin{equation}
 t=\frac{\widetilde\psi_1+\ii\widetilde\psi_2}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 t^c=\frac{\widetilde\psi_1-\ii\widetilde\psi_2}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:aoki-change}
\end{equation}
The bilinear $tt^c=t^\alpha(t^c)_\alpha$ is a Lorentz scalar.
Its mixed terms cancel under this substitution, giving the coordinate
Lagrangian density
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathcal L=\sum_{I=1}^2\left[
 \ii\widetilde\psi_I^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\widetilde\psi_I
 -\frac a2(m_t+g_t\delta h)(\widetilde\psi_I\widetilde\psi_I+\mathrm{h.c.})\right].}
 \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
Gauge interactions are omitted, so the two copies are independent for
each color. We calculate one copy and restore $2N_c$ in the box.
The factor $a$ in the mass and Yukawa terms is
$a^4(a^{-3/2})^2$.

We use $\epsilon_{12}=1$, $\epsilon^{12}=-1$ and
$\psi^\alpha=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\psi_\beta$, with the same rules
for dotted indices. In numerical matrix products,
\begin{equation}
 \epsmat=(\epsilon_{\alpha\beta})=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}=\ii\sigma^2,
 \qquad (\epsilon^{\alpha\beta})=-\epsmat.
\end{equation}
Every field is Fourier transformed with $e^{\ii\bk\cdot\mb x}$ and
measure $\di^3\bk/(2\pi)^3$. A prime removes the overall momentum delta
function.

\subsection{Mode functions}
For one Majorana copy the helicity spinors obey
\begin{equation}
 (\bs\cdot\widehat{\bk})h^s=s h^s,\qquad
 P_s=h^s h^{s\dagger}
 =\frac{\one+s\bs\cdot\widehat{\bk}}{2}.
\end{equation}
With one copy
and color suppressed, the operator expansion is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\psi_\alpha(\tau,\mb x)=\int\frac{\di^3\bk}{(2\pi)^3}
 \sum_{s=\pm}\left[
 h^s_\alpha u_s b^s_{\bk}e^{\ii\bk\cdot\mb x}
 +h^{s\dagger}_{\dot\gamma}\bar\sigma^{0\dot\gamma\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta} v_s^*b^{s\dagger}_{\bk}
 e^{-\ii\bk\cdot\mb x}\right].
 \label{eq:mode-expansion}
\end{equation}
Here $\{b^s_{\bk},b^{s'\dagger}_{\bk'}\}
=(2\pi)^3\delta^{ss'}\delta^3(\bk-\bk')$.
The wave equations are
\begin{align}
 \ii\partial_\tau u_s+s ku_s&=am_tv_s,&
 \ii\partial_\tau v_s-s kv_s&=am_tu_s,\label{eq:weyl-eom}\\
 u_s''-aHu_s'+(k^2+a^2m_t^2+\ii s aHk)u_s&=0,&
 v_s''-aHv_s'+(k^2+a^2m_t^2-\ii s aHk)v_s&=0.
\end{align}

With $x=-k\tau$, the normalized Bunch--Davies modes are
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{u_+=v_- =\frac{\tn}{\sqrt{2x}}W_{-1/2,\ii\tn}(-2\ii x),\qquad
 u_-=v_+ =\frac{\ii}{\sqrt{2x}}W_{1/2,\ii\tn}(-2\ii x).}
 \label{eq:aoki-modes}
\end{equation}
Here $u_s,v_s$ are the two mode functions in the
operator expansion~\eqref{eq:mode-expansion}; the creation term contains
$v_s^*$. Their normalization is
$|u_s|^2+|v_s|^2=1$. In particular,
$u_+=v_-$ and $u_-=v_+$, with the relative $\ii$ displayed above.

We use these Whittaker modes throughout. The time integrals will be
reduced directly to the scalar chemical-potential seed of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}; no additional mode decomposition is needed.

From the next subsection onward, we suppress the tildes: $\psi$
denotes the conformally rescaled field $\widetilde\psi$, and $D$ denotes
its propagators. The original covariant spinor is no longer used.
Thus the propagators below contain no external factors of $a^{-3/2}$;
the Yukawa vertices retain the factor $a(\tau)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:action}.

\subsection{Propagators and vertices}
\label{sec:propagators}
We follow the three propagator types and SK ordering of
You et al.~\cite{you} (v2), Eqs.~(2.30), (2.33), and (2.36),
specialized to $\widetilde\lambda=0$ and
$\widetilde M_R=\widetilde\nu=m_t/H$. 
All spinor indices below are lowered using
Eq.~\eqref{eq:epsilon}.
There is no overall factor of $\ii$ in the propagator definitions.

\paragraph{Three types of propagators.}
With $1=(\tau_1,\bk)$ and $2=(\tau_2,-\bk)$, define
\begin{align}
 D_{\aa\bb\,\alpha\dot\beta}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\langle T_C\psi_{\aa,\alpha}(1)\psi^\dagger_{\bb,\dot\beta}(2)\rangle',
 \nonumber\\
 \widehat D_{\aa\bb\,\alpha\beta}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\langle T_C\psi_{\aa,\alpha}(1)\psi_{\bb,\beta}(2)\rangle',
 \nonumber\\
 \check D_{\aa\bb\,\dot\alpha\dot\beta}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\langle T_C\psi^\dagger_{\aa,\dot\alpha}(1)
                    \psi^\dagger_{\bb,\dot\beta}(2)\rangle'.
 \label{eq:aoki-indexed-propagators}
\end{align}
Here $T_C$ orders fermions on the closed time contour. The three
$-+$ components are the unordered contractions
$\langle\psi_1\psi_2^\dagger\rangle'$,
$\langle\psi_1\psi_2\rangle'$, and
$\langle\psi_1^\dagger\psi_2^\dagger\rangle'$, respectively.
Using $u_{sj}=u_s(\tau_j,k)$, $v_{sj}=v_s(\tau_j,k)$ and
$P_s=(\one+s\sigma_k)/2$, $\sigma_k=\bs\cdot\widehat{\bk}$, their
explicit helicity sums are
\begin{align}
 D_{-+}
 &=\sum_{s=\pm1}u_{s1}u_{s2}^*P_s,
 \nonumber\\
 \widehat D_{-+}
 &=-\sum_{s=\pm1}u_{s1}v_{s2}^*P_s\epsmat,
 \nonumber\\
 \check D_{-+}
 &=\sum_{s=\pm1}v_{s1}u_{s2}^*\epsmat P_s.
 \label{eq:propagator-mode-sums}
\end{align}
These matrix expressions follow directly from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mode-expansion}: the creation wavefunction is
$v_s^*\epsmat h^{s*}$, and
$h^s(\epsmat h^{s*})^T=-P_s\epsmat$.
This fixes the epsilon signs when translating the helicity-spinor
expressions of Ref.~\cite{you} to our lower-index convention.

\paragraph{Scalar propagator with chemical potential.}
Set $X=-k\tau_1$, $Y=-k\tau_2$. We use the scalar-valued
chemical-potential propagator entering the Whittaker seed of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}, Eq.~(96):
\begin{align}
 \Dsc{\kappa}_{-+}(k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{e^{\ii\pi\kappa}}{2k}
 W_{\kappa,\ii\tn}(-2\ii X)W_{-\kappa,\ii\tn}(2\ii Y),\nonumber\\
 \Dsc{\kappa}_{+-}(k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{e^{\ii\pi\kappa}}{2k}
 W_{-\kappa,\ii\tn}(2\ii X)W_{\kappa,\ii\tn}(-2\ii Y).
 \label{eq:scalar-chemical}
\end{align}
The mass index $\tn$ is the $\widetilde\nu$ of that reference, and
$\kappa=\ii h\widetilde\mu$. We analytically continue to
$\kappa=\pm1/2$, or $h\widetilde\mu=\mp\ii/2$; the physical top
chemical potential remains zero. \emph{Scalar} here means the scalar
kernel of the chemical-potential seed, with its $1/(2k)$ normalization,
not the covariant spin-zero de Sitter propagator $D^{\mathrm{(s)}}$
used in the SMS manuscript. We retain the superscript
$\mathrm{sc},\kappa$ to distinguish these normalizations.
Both Wightman expressions are continued as analytic functions;
one must not conjugate the continued chemical potential anew.
Write $\Dsc{+}=\Dsc{1/2}$ and $\Dsc{-}=\Dsc{-1/2}$.
These superscripts are Whittaker indices, distinct from the SK
subscripts $\aa,\bb$ and the soft-branch label $\eta$.

The Whittaker recurrence relations give
\begin{align}
 A_X&=X\partial_X-\ii X-\tfrac12,&
 R_X&=-X\partial_X-\ii X+\tfrac12,\nonumber\\
 A_XW_{1/2,\ii\tn}(-2\ii X)
 &=\tn^2W_{-1/2,\ii\tn}(-2\ii X),&
 R_XW_{-1/2,\ii\tn}(-2\ii X)
 &=W_{1/2,\ii\tn}(-2\ii X).
 \label{eq:whittaker-operators}
\end{align}
Hence all three spinor propagators, for every SK pair, are
\begin{align}
 D_{\aa\bb}
 &=\frac{\ii k}{\sqrt{XY}}\left[
 (R_X\Dsc{-}_{\aa\bb})P_--(A_X\Dsc{+}_{\aa\bb})P_+\right],
 \label{eq:normal-scalar}\\
 \widehat D_{\aa\bb}
 &=-\frac{\tn k}{\sqrt{XY}}
 \left[\Dsc{-}_{\aa\bb}P_++\Dsc{+}_{\aa\bb}P_-\right]\epsmat,
 \label{eq:anomalous-scalar}\\
 \check D_{\aa\bb}
 &=\frac{\tn k}{\sqrt{XY}}\epsmat
 \left[\Dsc{+}_{\aa\bb}P_++\Dsc{-}_{\aa\bb}P_-\right].
 \label{eq:conjugate-scalar}
\end{align}
The operators act only on the scalar propagator to their right.
The factors $\ii$ and the signs include $e^{-\ii\pi\kappa}$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:scalar-chemical}. The time derivatives implement
Whittaker index shifts; they are not new derivative interactions.

\paragraph{The four SK components.}
For $\mathcal D=D,\widehat D,\check D$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D_{++}=\theta_{12}\mathcal D_{-+}
                 +\theta_{21}\mathcal D_{+-},\qquad
 \mathcal D_{--}=\theta_{21}\mathcal D_{-+}
                 +\theta_{12}\mathcal D_{+-},
 \label{eq:three-propagator-sk}
\end{equation}
where $\theta_{ij}=\theta(\tau_i-\tau_j)$.
The scalar $\Dsc{\kappa}$ obeys the same ordering formula.
Its Wightman functions coincide at equal times, so the first
derivatives in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-scalar} generate no delta function
from differentiating the step functions.
Fermionic signs are already contained in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:normal-scalar}--\eqref{eq:conjugate-scalar};
in particular the remaining field-type block is
\begin{equation}
 D^{\overline{\mathrm L}\mathrm L}_{\aa\bb}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 =-D_{\bb\aa}^{T}(-\bk;\tau_2,\tau_1).
 \label{eq:anomalous-reversal}
\end{equation}
Together with $D^{\mathrm L\overline{\mathrm L}}=D$,
$D^{\mathrm L\mathrm L}=\widehat D$ and
$D^{\overline{\mathrm L}\overline{\mathrm L}}=\check D$,
this specifies every propagator in the vertex-type sum.

\paragraph{Late-time expansion.}
At $x=-k\tau\ll1$, define
\begin{equation}
 c_\eta=\frac{2^{-1-2\ii \eta\tn}}{\pi}
           \Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii \eta\tn)^2,\qquad
 c\equiv c_+,\qquad c_-=c^*,\qquad |c|=\frac1{2\cosh\pi\tn}.
 \label{eq:late-coefficient}
\end{equation}
Expand the scalar propagators using
\begin{equation}
 W_{\kappa,\ii\tn}(z)
 =\sum_{\eta=\pm1}
 \frac{\Gamma(-2\ii \eta\tn)}
      {\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii \eta\tn-\kappa)}
 z^{1/2+\ii \eta\tn}
 \left[1-\frac{\kappa z}{1+2\ii \eta\tn}+O(z^2)\right].
 \label{eq:whittaker-late}
\end{equation}
Substitute this in Eq.~\eqref{eq:scalar-chemical}, apply $A_X,R_X$
where required, and select the nonanalytic momentum powers.
For a soft propagator momentum $\bk_s$, with $k_s=|\bk_s|$ and
$|k_s\tau_1|,|k_s\tau_2|\ll1$, the leading and subleading nonanalytic
terms are
\begin{align}
 &\big[D^{\mathrm L\overline{\mathrm L}}_{\aa\bb\,\alpha\dot\beta}
      (\bk_s;\tau_1,\tau_2)\big]_{\NL}
 =\big[D_{\aa\bb\,\alpha\dot\beta}(\bk_s;\tau_1,\tau_2)\big]_{\NL}
 \nonumber\\
 &=-\sum_{\eta=\pm1}
 \frac{2^{-2-2\ii \eta\tn}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii \eta\tn)^2}{\pi}
 (\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii \eta\tn}
 \left[
 k_s^{2\ii \eta\tn}\bs\cdot\widehat{\bk}_s
 +\frac{\ii(\tau_1-\tau_2)}{1+2\ii \eta\tn}
 k_s^{1+2\ii \eta\tn}\one
 \right]_{\alpha\dot\beta}
 +\cdots,
 \label{eq:weyl-soft-leading}\\
 &\big[\widehat D_{\aa\bb\,\alpha\beta}(\bk_s;\tau_1,\tau_2)\big]_{\NL}
 \nonumber\\
 &=-\sum_{\eta=\pm1}
 \frac{\eta\,2^{-2-2\ii \eta\tn}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii \eta\tn)^2}{\pi}
 (\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii \eta\tn}
 \left[
 k_s^{2\ii \eta\tn}(\bs\cdot\widehat{\bk}_s)\epsmat
 +\frac{\ii(\tau_1+\tau_2)}{1+2\ii \eta\tn}
 k_s^{1+2\ii \eta\tn}\epsmat
 \right]_{\alpha\beta}
 +\cdots,
 \label{eq:hat-soft-expansion}\\
 &\big[\check D_{\aa\bb\,\dot\alpha\dot\beta}(\bk_s;\tau_1,\tau_2)\big]_{\NL}
 \nonumber\\
 &=-\sum_{\eta=\pm1}
 \frac{\eta\,2^{-2-2\ii \eta\tn}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii \eta\tn)^2}{\pi}
 (\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii \eta\tn}
 \left[
 k_s^{2\ii \eta\tn}\epsmat(\bs\cdot\widehat{\bk}_s)
 -\frac{\ii(\tau_1+\tau_2)}{1+2\ii \eta\tn}
 k_s^{1+2\ii \eta\tn}\epsmat
 \right]_{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}
 +\cdots.
 \label{eq:check-soft-expansion}
\end{align}
The two displayed terms scale as $k_s^{2\ii \eta\tn}$ and
$k_s^{1+2\ii \eta\tn}$; the omitted terms start at
$k_s^{2+2\ii \eta\tn}$ at fixed endpoint times.
The ellipses denote only these higher-order soft corrections.
Both mass branches are already included in $\sum_{\eta=\pm1}$:
no additional complex-conjugate term or $\tn\to-\tn$ term is
implied by the ellipses.
Here $\epsmat=\ii\sigma^2$, and the identity and Pauli matrices carry
the displayed spinor indices. These nonlocal terms are the same for
all four SK assignments $\aa\bb$, following the cutting prescription of
Ref.~\cite{you}, Eq.~(3.13) and Appendix~D.3.

\paragraph{Yukawa vertices.}
There are two Yukawa vertices, $\delta h\psi\psi$
and $\delta h\psi^\dagger\psi^\dagger$.
Their spin tensors, labelled $\mathrm L$ and $\overline{\mathrm L}$ respectively, are
\begin{equation}
 J_{\mathrm L}^{\alpha\beta}=-\epsilon^{\alpha\beta},\qquad
 J_{\overline{\mathrm L}}^{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}
 =+\epsilon^{\dot\alpha\dot\beta},\qquad
 J_{\mathrm L}=\epsmat,\quad J_{\overline{\mathrm L}}=-\epsmat.
 \label{eq:vertex-tensors}
\end{equation}
On SK branch $\aa=\pm1$ their amputated rules are
$-\ii \aa g_t a(\tau)J_r$. The two identical fermion slots cancel the
$1/2$ in the interaction. The equivalent calculation with unamputated
bilinears and explicit Wick pairings is given in Section~\ref{sec:wick}.

\section{The four-point box}
\subsection{Vertex types, Wick contractions and Majorana counting}
\label{sec:wick}

The two unamputated interaction insertions are
\begin{align}
 &-\frac{\ii \aa g_ta(\tau)}2\delta h\,
 (-\epsilon^{\alpha\beta})
 \psi_\alpha\psi_\beta,
 \nonumber\\
 &-\frac{\ii \aa g_ta(\tau)}2\delta h\,
 \epsilon^{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}
 \psi^\dagger_{\dot\alpha}\psi^\dagger_{\dot\beta}.
 \label{eq:vertex-rule}
\end{align}

We label the two field types by $r,s\in\{\mathrm L,\overline{\mathrm L}\}$,
where $\mathrm L$ denotes $\psi$ and $\overline{\mathrm L}$ denotes
$\psi^\dagger$. 
Each vertex can be of either Yukawa type. For a fixed cycle the 16 choices
are grouped as
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|ccccc}
$(\delta h\psi\psi,
\delta h\psi^\dagger\psi^\dagger)$ vertices
 & $(4,0)$ & $(3,1)$ & $(2,2)$ & $(1,3)$ & $(0,4)$\\\hline
number of type assignments & 1 & 4 & 6 & 4 & 1
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For a fixed choice of vertex types around $i\to j\to k\to l\to i$,
write $J_i=J_{r_i}$ and $D_{ij}=D^{r_i r_j}_{ij}$
in this paragraph. Each matrix product contracts one spinor index:
\[
 (J_iD_{ij})^\alpha{}_{\beta}
 =\sum_{\gamma=1}^2(J_i)^{\alpha\gamma}(D_{ij})_{\gamma\beta}.
\]
The indices are dotted or undotted according to the field at each
endpoint. Multiplying the four segments and closing the last index
with the first gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_{ijkl}=
 \tr_2\!\left[(J_iD_{ij})(J_jD_{jk})(J_kD_{kl})(J_lD_{li})\right].
 \label{eq:weyl-components}
\end{equation}
Here $\tr_2$ sums only the two spinor components. 
We only calculated the diagram '1234', and other diagram can be obtained by external permutation.

For one Majorana copy and one color, and for the full top loop, respectively,
\begin{align}
 \langle\delta h_{\bk_1}\delta h_{\bk_2}
 \delta h_{\bk_3}\delta h_{\bk_4}\rangle'={}&-\frac{g_t^4}{2}
 \sum_{(i,j,k,l=L,\bar L)}\sum_{\aa_1,\ldots,\aa_4=\pm}
 \left(\prod_{v=1}^4\aa_v\right)
 \int_{-\infty}^0\prod_{v=1}^4
 [\di\tau_v\,a(\tau_v)G_{\aa_v}(k_v;\tau_v)]
 \int\frac{\di^3\bq}{(2\pi)^3}\mathcal T_{ijkl}
 \label{eq:each-box}
\end{align}
The minus sign is the closed-fermion-loop sign.

\section{Oscillatory contribution}
\subsection{Unintegrated box and soft expansion}
\label{sec:collapsed}
We start with the fixed cycle $1234$, keeping both the loop and time
integrals explicit. For each choice
$r_1,r_2,r_3,r_4\in\{\mathrm L,\overline{\mathrm L}\}$, its integrand is
\begin{align}
 \G^{\rm top}_{1234}
 ={}&-N_cg_t^4
 \sum_{\aa_1,\ldots,\aa_4=\pm}\prod_{v=1}^4\aa_v
 \int_{-\infty}^0\prod_{v=1}^4
 [\di\tau_v\,a(\tau_v)G_{\aa_v}(k_v;\tau_v)]
 \int\frac{\di^3\bq}{(2\pi)^3}
 \sum_{r_1,\ldots,r_4}
 \mathcal T_{1234},\nonumber\\
 \mathcal T_{1234}
 ={}&\tr_2[D_{12}J_{r_2}D_{23}J_{r_3}
              D_{34}J_{r_4}D_{41}J_{r_1}],
 \qquad
 D_{ij}=D^{r_i r_j}_{\aa_i \aa_j}(\mb p_{ij};\tau_i,\tau_j).
 \label{eq:unintegrated-box}
\end{align}
The minus sign is the closed-loop sign. Both Majorana copies and all
colors have been included, so the single-copy coefficient $-g_t^4/2$
has become $-N_cg_t^4$. The sum over $r_v$ changes the vertex types;
it does not include external permutations.

Use the centered momenta
\begin{align}
 \bk_1&=\mb k_L+\tfrac12\bk_s,&\bk_2&=-\mb k_L+\tfrac12\bk_s,\nonumber\\
 \bk_3&=\mb k_R-\tfrac12\bk_s,&\bk_4&=-\mb k_R-\tfrac12\bk_s,\nonumber\\
 \bm\ell&=\bq+\tfrac12\bk_s,&\mb K&=\bq+\bk_s,\qquad
 \mb n_L=\widehat{\mb k}_L,\quad\mb n_R=\widehat{\mb k}_R,\quad\mb n_s=\widehat{\bk}_s.
 \label{eq:central}
\end{align}
Thus $\mb p_{12}=\mb k_L+\bm\ell$, $\mb p_{23}=\mb K$,
$\mb p_{34}=\mb k_R+\bm\ell$, and $\mb p_{41}=\bq$.
We expand in $q/k_{L,R}$ and $k_s/k_{L,R}$, but retain the nonanalytic
powers of $q$ and $K$ and their unit vectors. At the final accuracy,
$k_L=k_{12}/2$, $k_R=k_{34}/2$, with $k_{ij}=k_i+k_j$.

For the $+\ii\tn$ branch, the terms retained at the two soft
endpoints can be factored as
\begin{equation}
 D^{rs}_{\NL,+}(k;\tau,\tau')
 \simeq-\frac c2(k^2\tau\tau')^{\ii\tn}
 L_r^+(k,\tau)\sigma_k U_s^+(k,\tau').
 \label{eq:soft-factored}
\end{equation}
Here $D^{rs}_{\NL,+}$ denotes the $\eta=+1$ nonlocal branch,
$c=c_+$ is defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:late-coefficient}, and
$\sigma_k=\bs\cdot\widehat{\bk}$. The endpoint matrices through first order are
\begin{align}
 L_{\mathrm L}^+&=\one+\frac{\ii\tau\bs\cdot\bk}{1+2\ii\tn},&
 L_{\overline{\mathrm L}}^+&=\epsmat
 \left(\one-\frac{\ii\tau\bs\cdot\bk}{1+2\ii\tn}\right),\nonumber\\
 U_{\mathrm L}^+&=\left(\one+\frac{\ii\tau\bs\cdot\bk}{1+2\ii\tn}\right)\epsmat,&
 U_{\overline{\mathrm L}}^+&=\one-\frac{\ii\tau\bs\cdot\bk}{1+2\ii\tn}.
 \label{eq:soft-endpoints-first}
\end{align}
The omitted terms at each endpoint are $O(k^2\tau^2)$.
These products reproduce the $\eta=+1$ terms in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:weyl-soft-leading}--\eqref{eq:check-soft-expansion}
through first order. The leading nonzero box signal also requires
products of first-order corrections at opposite hard ends.

First fix the four vertex types
$r_1,r_2,r_3,r_4\in\{\mathrm L,\overline{\mathrm L}\}$ in the cycle $1234$.
The corresponding left and right hard spin matrices are
\begin{align}
 V_L^{r_1r_2}&=-\frac12
 U_{r_1}(q,\tau_1)J_{r_1}D^{r_1r_2}_{\aa_1\aa_2}
 (\mb k_L+\bm\ell;\tau_1,\tau_2)J_{r_2}L_{r_2}(K,\tau_2),\nonumber\\
 V_R^{r_3r_4}&=-\frac12
 U_{r_3}(K,\tau_3)J_{r_3}D^{r_3r_4}_{\aa_3\aa_4}
 (\mb k_R+\bm\ell;\tau_3,\tau_4)J_{r_4}L_{r_4}(q,\tau_4).
 \label{eq:weyl-hard-block}
\end{align}
Here $J_{\mathrm L}=\epsmat$ and $J_{\overline{\mathrm L}}=-\epsmat$
are the vertex matrices; $L_r=L_r^+$ and $U_r=U_r^+$ are soft endpoint
matrices, not additional vertices. The factors $-1/2$ come from the
soft lines. No vertex-type sum is implicit in these two matrices.
Summing the 16 choices of the four vertex types gives the cut spin factor
\begin{equation}
 c^2(qK)^{2\ii\tn}\prod_v(-\tau_v)^{\ii\tn}
 \sum_{r_1,r_2,r_3,r_4=\mathrm L,\overline{\mathrm L}}
 \tr_2[V_L^{r_1r_2}\sigma_K V_R^{r_3r_4}\sigma_q].
 \label{eq:spin-cut}
\end{equation}
To display the remaining time measure, define only the external
polynomial and phase
\[
 E_{\aa}(k,\tau)=(1-\ii \aa k\tau)e^{\ii \aa k\tau},
 \qquad G_{\aa}(k;\tau)=\frac{H^2}{2k^3}E_{\aa}(k,\tau).
\]
Substitution in Eq.~\eqref{eq:unintegrated-box} gives
\begin{align}
 \left.\G^{\rm top}_{1234}\right|_{\rm clock}
 ={}&-\frac{N_cg_t^4H^4}{16\prod_i k_i^3}
 \Bigg[c^2\sum_{\aa_1,\ldots,\aa_4}\prod_v \aa_v
 \int_{-\infty}^0\prod_v
 [\di\tau_v\,(-\tau_v)^{\ii\tn-1}E_{\aa_v}(k_v,\tau_v)]
 \nonumber\\[-1mm]
 &\hspace{13mm}\times
 \int\frac{\di^3\bq}{(2\pi)^3}(qK)^{2\ii\tn}
 \sum_{r_1,\ldots,r_4}
 \tr_2[V_L^{r_1r_2}\sigma_K V_R^{r_3r_4}\sigma_q]
 +\mathrm{c.c.}\Bigg].
 \label{eq:factorized-clock}
\end{align}
No time integration is implicit in $V_L,V_R$.
The complex conjugate supplies the opposite same-sign soft branch.
We keep orders zero and one at each hard end. The first surviving
full-box term will be the product of the two first-order terms.

Let $\mb p=\mb k_L+\bm\ell$ and $\sigma_p=\bs\cdot\widehat{\mb p}$.
Expanding the endpoint matrices in Eq.~\eqref{eq:soft-endpoints-first},
the four terms in the left type sum are
\begin{align}
 2V_L^{\mathrm L\mathrm L}={}&
 \left(\one+\frac{\ii\tau_1\bs\cdot\bq}{1+2\ii\tn}\right)
 D^{\mathrm L\mathrm L}\epsmat
 \left(\one+\frac{\ii\tau_2\bs\cdot\mb K}{1+2\ii\tn}\right)
 \nonumber\\
 2V_L^{\mathrm L\overline{\mathrm L}}={}&
 \left(\one+\frac{\ii\tau_1\bs\cdot\bq}{1+2\ii\tn}\right)
 D^{\mathrm L\overline{\mathrm L}}
 \left(\one-\frac{\ii\tau_2\bs\cdot\mb K}{1+2\ii\tn}\right)
 \nonumber\\
 2V_L^{\overline{\mathrm L}\mathrm L}={}&
 \left(\one-\frac{\ii\tau_1\bs\cdot\bq}{1+2\ii\tn}\right)\epsmat
 D^{\overline{\mathrm L}\mathrm L}\epsmat
 \left(\one+\frac{\ii\tau_2\bs\cdot\mb K}{1+2\ii\tn}\right)
 \nonumber\\
 2V_L^{\overline{\mathrm L}\overline{\mathrm L}}={}&
 \left(\one-\frac{\ii\tau_1\bs\cdot\bq}{1+2\ii\tn}\right)\epsmat
 D^{\overline{\mathrm L}\overline{\mathrm L}}
 \left(\one-\frac{\ii\tau_2\bs\cdot\mb K}{1+2\ii\tn}\right).
 \label{eq:hard-four-types}
\end{align}
This equation is used through first order in the soft momenta;
all four hard propagators carry the arguments in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:weyl-hard-block}.

The four vertex types reduce the spin matrices to two scalar
coefficients, expressed directly through Eq.~\eqref{eq:scalar-chemical}.
With $X=-p\tau_1$, $Y=-p\tau_2$, define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal K_{0;\aa\bb}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{\ii p}{2\sqrt{XY}}\left[
 (A_X-\ii\tn)\Dsc{+}_{\aa\bb}
 +(R_X+\ii\tn)\Dsc{-}_{\aa\bb}\right],\nonumber\\
 \mathcal K_{1;\aa\bb}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{\ii p}{2\sqrt{XY}}\left[
 (R_X-\ii\tn)\Dsc{-}_{\aa\bb}
 -(A_X+\ii\tn)\Dsc{+}_{\aa\bb}\right].
 \label{eq:hard-scalar-coefficients}
\end{align}
Every scalar propagator has arguments $(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)$.
These are hard spin-matrix coefficients, not new propagators.
For the fixed $1234$ ordering the type sum, before any integration, is
\begin{align}
 \sum_{r_1,r_2}V_L^{r_1r_2}
 ={}&-\mathcal K_{0;\aa_1\aa_2}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)\sigma_p
 \nonumber\\
 &+\frac{\ii}{1+2\ii\tn}
 \left[\tau_1\mathcal K_{1;\aa_1\aa_2}(k_L;\tau_1,\tau_2)\bs\cdot\bq
 +\tau_2\mathcal K_{1;\aa_2\aa_1}(k_L;\tau_2,\tau_1)\bs\cdot\mb K\right]
 +O(\text{soft}^2).
 \label{eq:vertex-expand}
\end{align}
Here $\mb p=\mb k_L+\bm\ell$. In the first term both the magnitude
and direction must be expanded:
\begin{align}
 -\mathcal K_0(p)\sigma_p
 ={}&-\mathcal K_0(k_L)\bs\cdot\mb n_L\nonumber\\
 &-\frac1{k_L}\bs\cdot
 \left[\mathcal K_0(k_L)\bm\ell+
 \left.(p\partial_p-1)\mathcal K_0(p)\right|_{p=k_L}
 \mb n_L(\mb n_L\cdot\bm\ell)\right]
 +O(\text{soft}^2).
 \label{eq:hard-taylor}
\end{align}
The derivative is at fixed times. In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{r_1,r_2}V_L^{r_1r_2,(0)}
 =-\mathcal K_{0;\aa_1\aa_2}(k_L;\tau_1,\tau_2)\bs\cdot\mb n_L
 \ne0.
 \label{eq:hard-zeroth-weyl}
\end{equation}
The right end follows from $(1,2,L,\bq,\mb K)\to(3,4,R,\mb K,\bq)$.
The hard propagators retain their complete SK time dependence.

\subsection{Integrand cancellation and the spin trace}
\label{sec:integrand-cancel}
Keep each time and SK label attached to its physical external vertex.
In particular, the exact Higgs factors form the common weight
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{\{\aa\}}(\{\tau\};\{k\})
 =\prod_{v=1}^4
 [\aa_v(-\tau_v)^{\ii\tn-1}E_{\aa_v}(k_v,\tau_v)].
 \label{eq:exact-external-weight}
\end{equation}
This weight is independent of the loop momentum $\bq$. It is kept
outside the loop integral without expanding any $E_{\aa_v}$ in $k_s$.
The normalization $1/\prod_v k_v^3$ is also kept exact.

\paragraph{Four actual diagrams, with fixed external labels.}
The four $s$-cut orderings are
\begin{equation}
 \left.\G^{\rm top}\right|_{s,\rm clock}
 =\left.(\G^{\rm top}_{1234}+\G^{\rm top}_{1243}
          +\G^{\rm top}_{1342}+\G^{\rm top}_{1432})\right|_{\rm clock}.
 \label{eq:external-cycle-sum}
\end{equation}
Write $V_{12}=\sum_{r_1,r_2}V_L^{r_1r_2}$ and
$V_{34}=\sum_{r_3,r_4}V_R^{r_3r_4}$ for the matrices already defined
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:weyl-hard-block}.
When the left connection is reversed, the $q$ endpoint is at vertex
$2$ and the $K$ endpoint at vertex $1$. The reversed right connection
similarly puts the $K$ endpoint at vertex $4$. Their matrices are
\begin{align}
 V_{21}
 &=-\frac12\sum_{r_1,r_2}
 U_{r_2}(q,\tau_2)J_{r_2}
 D^{r_2r_1}_{\aa_2\aa_1}(-\mb k_L+\bm\ell;\tau_2,\tau_1)
 J_{r_1}L_{r_1}(K,\tau_1),\nonumber\\
 V_{43}
 &=-\frac12\sum_{r_3,r_4}
 U_{r_4}(K,\tau_4)J_{r_4}
 D^{r_4r_3}_{\aa_4\aa_3}(-\mb k_R+\bm\ell;\tau_4,\tau_3)
 J_{r_3}L_{r_3}(q,\tau_3).
 \label{eq:reversed-physical-blocks}
\end{align}
These are different vertex connections, not averages of the integrand
of $1234$. For example, $E_{\aa_1}(k_1,\tau_1)$ stays at vertex $1$
in both $V_{12}$ and $V_{21}$.
The four diagrams correspond respectively to
\begin{equation}
 (1234,1243,1342,1432)
 \ \longleftrightarrow\
 ((12,34),(12,43),(21,34),(21,43)).
 \label{eq:four-trace-map}
\end{equation}
Thus their common external factor multiplies four separate spin traces:
\begin{align}
 &c^2(qK)^{2\ii\tn}\mathcal E_{\{\aa\}}
 \Big\{
 \tr_2[V_{12}\sigma_KV_{34}\sigma_q]
 +\tr_2[V_{12}\sigma_KV_{43}\sigma_q]\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{43mm}
 +\tr_2[V_{21}\sigma_KV_{34}\sigma_q]
 +\tr_2[V_{21}\sigma_KV_{43}\sigma_q]\Big\}.
 \label{eq:four-unintegrated-traces}
\end{align}
Sum over the four SK labels and integrate this expression with
$\prod_v\di\tau_v\,\di^3\bq/(2\pi)^3$, add its complex conjugate,
and multiply by $-N_cg_t^4H^4/(16\prod_v k_v^3)$.

The scalar kernel has the reversal identity
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K_{0;\aa\bb}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 =\mathcal K_{0;\bb\aa}(p;\tau_2,\tau_1).
 \label{eq:reverse}
\end{equation}
It follows from fermionic propagator reversal and the vertex tensors;
no exchange of integration variables is being performed here.
Applying this identity to the two physical connections gives
\begin{align}
 V_{12}^{(0)}
 &=-\mathcal K_{0;\aa_1\aa_2}(k_L;\tau_1,\tau_2)\bs\cdot\mb n_L,
 &V_{21}^{(0)}
 &=+\mathcal K_{0;\aa_1\aa_2}(k_L;\tau_1,\tau_2)\bs\cdot\mb n_L,\nonumber\\
 V_{12}^{(0)}+V_{21}^{(0)}&=0,
 &V_{34}^{(0)}+V_{43}^{(0)}&=0.
 \label{eq:integrand-zero}
\end{align}
Because the exact external weight is common to all four traces,
this cancellation is pointwise even before the time integrations.
It removes every product with a zeroth-order end, including
$(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(2,0)$ and $(0,2)$. In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\left.\G^{\rm box}_{s,\rm clock}\right|_{k_s^{3\pm4\ii\tn}}=0.}
 \label{eq:cancellation}
\end{equation}
A single diagram has a nonzero zeroth-order trace. The cancellation
uses the sum of the four actual diagrams, not dummy-pair
symmetrization or an SK time integral.

\paragraph{First-order matrices with both soft endpoints retained.}
For compact notation only, set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K_j^{12}=\mathcal K_{j;\aa_1\aa_2}(k_L;\tau_1,\tau_2),
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_j^{21}=\mathcal K_{j;\aa_2\aa_1}(k_L;\tau_2,\tau_1),
 \qquad E_v=E_{\aa_v}(k_v,\tau_v).
 \label{eq:direct-kernel-shorthand}
\end{equation}
The full first-order left integrand of $1234$ is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I_{12}^{(1)}
 ={}&\aa_1\aa_2(-\tau_1)^{\ii\tn-1}(-\tau_2)^{\ii\tn-1}E_1E_2
 \Bigg\{
 \underbrace{-\frac1{k_L}\bs\cdot
 \left[\mathcal K_0^{12}\bm\ell+
 \left.(p\partial_p-1)\mathcal K_{0;\aa_1\aa_2}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)\right|_{p=k_L}
 \mb n_L(\mb n_L\cdot\bm\ell)\right]}_{\text{hard-line correction}}
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{22mm}
 +\underbrace{\frac{\ii}{1+2\ii\tn}
 [\tau_1\mathcal K_1^{12}\bs\cdot\bq+
  \tau_2\mathcal K_1^{21}\bs\cdot\mb K]}_
 {\text{two separate soft-endpoint corrections}}
 \Bigg\}.
 \label{eq:direct-first-order-integrand}
\end{align}
The Higgs factors in the first line are exact multiplicative factors.
There is no external-mode derivative term.

To take each trace directly, write
\begin{equation}
 V_{12}^{(1)}=-\frac{\bs\cdot\mb w_L^{12}}{k_L},\quad
 V_{21}^{(1)}=-\frac{\bs\cdot\mb w_L^{21}}{k_L},\quad
 V_{34}^{(1)}=-\frac{\bs\cdot\mb w_R^{34}}{k_R},\quad
 V_{43}^{(1)}=-\frac{\bs\cdot\mb w_R^{43}}{k_R}.
 \label{eq:direct-first-order-vectors}
\end{equation}
The two left vectors are explicitly
\begin{align}
 \mb w_L^{12}
 ={}&\mathcal K_0^{12}\bm\ell+
 \left.(p\partial_p-1)\mathcal K_{0;\aa_1\aa_2}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)\right|_{p=k_L}
 \mb n_L(\mb n_L\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\frac{\ii k_L}{1+2\ii\tn}
 [\tau_1\mathcal K_1^{12}\bq+\tau_2\mathcal K_1^{21}\mb K],
 \nonumber\\
 \mb w_L^{21}
 ={}&\mathcal K_0^{12}\bm\ell+
 \left.(p\partial_p-1)\mathcal K_{0;\aa_1\aa_2}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)\right|_{p=k_L}
 \mb n_L(\mb n_L\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\frac{\ii k_L}{1+2\ii\tn}
 [\tau_2\mathcal K_1^{21}\bq+\tau_1\mathcal K_1^{12}\mb K].
 \label{eq:two-direct-left-vectors}
\end{align}
The hard corrections agree by Eq.~\eqref{eq:reverse}, while the
coefficients of $\bq$ and $\mb K$ are interchanged because those
soft lines attach to different vertices in the second diagram.
The right vectors are
\begin{equation}
 (\mb w_R^{34},\mb w_R^{43})
 =(\mb w_L^{12},\mb w_L^{21})
 \big|_{(L,1,2,\bq,\mb K)\to(R,3,4,\mb K,\bq)}.
 \label{eq:two-direct-right-vectors}
\end{equation}
No relation $\mathcal K_1^{12}=\mathcal K_1^{21}$ is assumed.

\paragraph{Direct trace, followed by the diagram sum.}
For each of the four choices $A=12,21$ and $B=34,43$, the Pauli
identity gives
\begin{align}
 \tr_2[V_A^{(1)}\sigma_KV_B^{(1)}\sigma_q]
 ={}&\frac{2}{k_Lk_RqK}
 \Big[
 2(\mb w_L^A\cdot\bm\ell)(\mb w_R^B\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\tfrac12(\mb w_L^A\cdot\bk_s)(\mb w_R^B\cdot\bk_s)
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{27mm}
 -(\mb w_L^A\cdot\mb w_R^B)(\ell^2-k_s^2/4)\Big].
 \label{eq:individual-direct-trace}
\end{align}
This evaluates the spin trace without combining the two soft
endpoints of any single diagram.
The scalar expression is bilinear in its two vectors, so the four
results can now be added using their vector sums. In particular,
the endpoint terms add as
\begin{equation}
 [\tau_1\mathcal K_1^{12}\bq+\tau_2\mathcal K_1^{21}\mb K]
 +[\tau_2\mathcal K_1^{21}\bq+\tau_1\mathcal K_1^{12}\mb K]
 =2(\tau_1\mathcal K_1^{12}+\tau_2\mathcal K_1^{21})\bm\ell.
 \label{eq:physical-endpoint-sum}
\end{equation}
This is the addition of two diagrams, with no factor $1/2$ and no
average over time or SK labels.
Collect the scalar coefficients of the resulting vector sum:
\begin{align}
 \Pi^L_{1;\aa_1\aa_2}
 &=\mathcal K_0^{12}
 -\frac{\ii k_L}{1+2\ii\tn}
 [\tau_1\mathcal K_1^{12}+\tau_2\mathcal K_1^{21}],\nonumber\\
 \Pi^L_{2;\aa_1\aa_2}
 &=\left.(p\partial_p-1)
 \mathcal K_{0;\aa_1\aa_2}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)\right|_{p=k_L},
 \qquad
 \Pi^R_{i;\aa_3\aa_4}
 =\Pi^L_{i;\aa_1\aa_2}\big|_{(L,1,2)\to(R,3,4)}.
 \label{eq:time-integrand-coefficients}
\end{align}
Both are still functions of their two times. Their associated
vectors satisfy
\begin{align}
 \mb v_L&=\Pi^L_1\bm\ell+\Pi^L_2\mb n_L(\mb n_L\cdot\bm\ell),
 &\mb w_L^{12}+\mb w_L^{21}&=2\mb v_L,\nonumber\\
 \mb v_R&=\Pi^R_1\bm\ell+\Pi^R_2\mb n_R(\mb n_R\cdot\bm\ell),
 &\mb w_R^{34}+\mb w_R^{43}&=2\mb v_R.
 \label{eq:unintegrated-vectors}
\end{align}
The sum of the four already evaluated traces is consequently
\begin{align}
 \sum_{\substack{A=12,21\\B=34,43}}
 \tr_2[V_A^{(1)}\sigma_KV_B^{(1)}\sigma_q]
 ={}&\frac{8}{k_Lk_RqK}
 \Big[2(\mb v_L\cdot\bm\ell)(\mb v_R\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\tfrac12(\mb v_L\cdot\bk_s)(\mb v_R\cdot\bk_s)
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{22mm}
 -(\mb v_L\cdot\mb v_R)(\ell^2-k_s^2/4)\Big].
 \label{eq:pauli}
\end{align}
The factor $8$ is $2$ from the Pauli trace times $4$ from the four
diagrams. Restoring the common exact external weight, the complete
leading same-branch integrand is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I_{s,+}^{(1,1)}
 ={}&\frac{8c^2}{k_Lk_R}\mathcal E_{\{\aa\}}(\{\tau\};\{k\})
 (qK)^{2\ii\tn-1}\nonumber\\
 &\times\Big[
 2(\mb v_L\cdot\bm\ell)(\mb v_R\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\tfrac12(\mb v_L\cdot\bk_s)(\mb v_R\cdot\bk_s)
 -(\mb v_L\cdot\mb v_R)(\ell^2-k_s^2/4)\Big].
 \label{eq:complete-direct-integrand}
\end{align}
All spinor indices have been contracted. The Higgs functions in
$\mathcal E_{\{\aa\}}$ still use the exact $k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4$ and take
no part in the loop integral. The equal-energy limit needed for the
leading time coefficient is taken only after that integral.

\subsection{The scalar loop integral first}
\label{sec:loop-first}
At fixed times and SK labels, the four $\Pi^{L,R}_i$ are independent
of $\bq$. The loop integral to be evaluated is therefore simply
\begin{align}
 &\int\frac{\di^3\bq}{(2\pi)^3}(qK)^{2\ii\tn-1}
 \left[2(\mb v_L\cdot\bm\ell)(\mb v_R\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\tfrac12(\mb v_L\cdot\bk_s)(\mb v_R\cdot\bk_s)
 -(\mb v_L\cdot\mb v_R)(\ell^2-k_s^2/4)\right]\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{30mm}
 =k_s^{5+4\ii\tn}\sum_{i,j=1}^2
       \mathscr L_{ij}(\tn;\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)\Pi^L_i\Pi^R_j .
 \label{eq:spin-bubble}
\end{align}
The factor $8/(k_Lk_R)$ from the trace is outside this definition.
Choose
\[
 \mb n_L=(\sin\theta_1,0,\cos\theta_1),\quad
 \mb n_R=(\sin\theta_3\cos\phi_3,\sin\theta_3\sin\phi_3,\cos\theta_3),
 \quad\mb n_s=(0,0,1).
\]
The loop integral has mass dimension $5+4\ii\tn$.
We evaluate it using the scalar convolution
\begin{align}
 f_0(A,B)&=\frac{\Gamma(A+B-\tfrac32)\Gamma(\tfrac32-A)\Gamma(\tfrac32-B)}
 {\Gamma(A)\Gamma(B)\Gamma(3-A-B)},\\
 \int\frac{\di^3\bq}{(2\pi)^3}(q^2)^{-A}(K^2)^{-B}
 &=\frac{k_s^{3-2A-2B}}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}f_0(A,B).
 \label{eq:scalar-bubble}
\end{align}
For the scalar numerator this identity follows by Schwinger parameters
and a Gaussian momentum integral in the convergence domain
$\Re A,\Re B<3/2$, $\Re(A+B)>3/2$. The shifted polynomial moments
are defined by the same meromorphic continuation, with common regulator
parameters before recombination. The physical soft-region integrals have
UV power divergences when extended beyond $q\sim k_s$; this continuation
selects their nonanalytic coefficient. UV-dependent polynomial contact
terms belong to matching with the hard region and are excluded.
No physical UV cutoff is sent to infinity term by term.
The following reduction expresses the polynomial numerator as a finite
sum of these scalar convolutions. The result depends on the two hard
directions relative to $\bk_s$ as well as on their mutual angle.

\paragraph{Reduction of the polynomial numerator.}
\label{sec:bubble-reduction}
For the reduction only, measure $q,K,\ell$ in units of $k_s$, put
$u=q^2$, $v=K^2$, and set $\rho=1/2-\ii\tn$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \ell^2=(u+v)/2-1/4,\qquad
 \ell_\parallel\equiv\bm\ell\cdot\mb n_s=(v-u)/2,\qquad
 \ell_\perp^2=\ell^2-\ell_\parallel^2.
\end{equation}
Azimuthal averaging replaces two transverse components by
$\ell_\perp^2\delta^\perp_{ij}/2$ and four by
$\ell_\perp^4(\delta^\perp_{ij}\delta^\perp_{kl}
+\delta^\perp_{ik}\delta^\perp_{jl}+\delta^\perp_{il}\delta^\perp_{jk})/8$,
where $\delta^\perp_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-n_{s i}n_{s j}$.
The numerator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spin-bubble} consequently becomes a polynomial
in $u,v$ and the three external angles. Each monomial is integrated by
\begin{align}
 u^mv^n&\quad\longmapsto\quad
 \frac{f_0(\rho-m,\rho-n)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}},\\
 \frac{f_0(\rho-m,\rho-n)}{f_0(\rho,\rho)}&=
 \frac{(3/2-\rho)_m(3/2-\rho)_n(1-\rho)_m(1-\rho)_n}
 {(5/2-2\rho)_{m+n}(3-2\rho)_{m+n}}.
 \label{eq:bubble-reduction}
\end{align}
Only nonnegative integers $m,n$ with $m+n\leq4$ occur. Thus the bubble is a
finite Gamma sum, and all its terms reduce to the same $f_0(\rho,\rho)$ times
rational functions. For example the coefficient of $\Pi^L_1\Pi^R_1$ in the
square bracket of Eq.~\eqref{eq:spin-bubble} is
$\ell^4+\ell^2/4-\ell_\parallel^2/2$, whose integral is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{f_0(\rho,\rho)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}
 \frac{3(\rho-1)(2\rho-3)}{8(\rho-2)(4\rho-7)(4\rho-5)}.
\end{equation}
For the cross terms, the azimuthal average has the form
\[
 \frac{(\ell^2+1/4)\ell_\perp^2}{2}
 +(\mb n_{L,R}\cdot\mb n_s)^2
 \left[(\ell^2+1/4)(\ell_\parallel^2-\ell_\perp^2/2)
                        -\ell_\parallel^2/2\right].
\]
The square bracket has zero scalar convolution by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bubble-reduction}. Thus the two cross coefficients are
equal and angle independent, even though the left and right time
kernels are still different. The complete result of the loop integral is
\begin{align}
 \mathscr L_{11}&=3\mathscr L_{12}=3\mathscr L_{21},\qquad
 \mathscr L_{12}=
 \frac{f_0(\rho,\rho)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\,
 \frac{(\rho-1)(2\rho-3)}
      {8(\rho-2)(4\rho-7)(4\rho-5)},\nonumber\\
 \mathscr L_{22}
 &=\frac{f_0(\rho,\rho)}
 {32(4\pi)^{3/2}(\rho-3)(\rho-2)(4\rho-7)(4\rho-5)}
 \Big[(\rho-1)(2\rho-3)(2\rho-5)\nonumber\\
 &\quad-(\rho-1)(2\rho-3)^2
   (\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_3-3\cos^2\theta_1\cos^2\theta_3)\nonumber\\
 &\quad-2(\rho-1)(\rho-2)(2\rho-3)
    \sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3\nonumber\\
 &\quad+(\rho-2)(2\rho-5)(2\rho-3)
    \sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3)\Big].
 \label{eq:loop-first-coefficients}
\end{align}
No time integral was used to obtain these coefficients. Interchanging
the order of the regulated scalar integrations is understood first in
a convergence domain and then by analytic continuation of the summed
expression, retaining the BD prescription and excluding analytic
contact terms as above.

\subsection{Left and right time integrals from the Whittaker seed}
\label{sec:seed-eval}
After the loop integral, the exact external factors enter only the
two scalar time integrals. Before taking the collapsed limit these are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal T_i^L(k_1,k_2;k_L)
 ={}&\sum_{\aa_1,\aa_2=\pm}\aa_1\aa_2
 \int_{-\infty}^0\di\tau_1\di\tau_2\,
 (-\tau_1)^{\ii\tn-1}(-\tau_2)^{\ii\tn-1}
 E_{\aa_1}(k_1,\tau_1)E_{\aa_2}(k_2,\tau_2)
 \Pi^L_{i;\aa_1\aa_2},\nonumber\\
 \mathcal T_j^R(k_3,k_4;k_R)
 ={}&\sum_{\aa_3,\aa_4=\pm}\aa_3\aa_4
 \int_{-\infty}^0\di\tau_3\di\tau_4\,
 (-\tau_3)^{\ii\tn-1}(-\tau_4)^{\ii\tn-1}
 E_{\aa_3}(k_3,\tau_3)E_{\aa_4}(k_4,\tau_4)
 \Pi^R_{j;\aa_3\aa_4}.
 \label{eq:left-right-time-integrals}
\end{align}
Here $i,j=1,2$. Their product multiplies
$k_s^{5+4\ii\tn}\mathscr L_{ij}$ from the completed loop integral.
For the leading collapsed signal, take the finite equal-energy
limit of these time coefficients, after combining the SK branches:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_i^L(k_L,k_L;k_L)=k_L^{-2\ii\tn}\mathcal P_i(\tn),
 \qquad
 \mathcal T_j^R(k_R,k_R;k_R)=k_R^{-2\ii\tn}\mathcal P_j(\tn).
 \label{eq:leading-time-limit}
\end{equation}
Corrections to this limit affect higher soft orders. No Taylor
expansion of a Higgs mode is needed in the loop calculation.
A common endpoint regulator is retained until the complete SK sum
has been combined. With $x=-k_L\tau_1$, $y=-k_L\tau_2$ and $r=p/k_L$,
introduce the same two scalar moments used for the mixed branch:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_j(\alpha,\beta;r)
 =\sum_{\aa,\bb=\pm}\aa\bb\int_0^\infty\di x\di y\,
 x^{\alpha-1}y^{\beta-1}
 (1+\ii \aa x)(1+\ii \bb y)e^{-\ii \aa x-\ii \bb y}
 \mathcal K_{j;\aa\bb}(r;x,y),\qquad j=0,1.
 \label{eq:hard-def}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal K_j(r;x,y)$ means
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-scalar-coefficients} at $X=rx$, $Y=ry$.
The two endpoint terms are kept separately up to this stage.
Their Whittaker seed evaluations give
\begin{align}
 &\sum_{\aa,\bb}\aa\bb\int_0^\infty\di x\di y\,
 x^{\ii\tn-1}y^{\ii\tn-1}(1+\ii \aa x)(1+\ii \bb y)e^{-\ii \aa x-\ii \bb y}
 \,x\mathcal K_{1;\aa\bb}(1;x,y)
 =\mathcal H_1(1+\ii\tn,\ii\tn;1),\nonumber\\
 &\sum_{\aa,\bb}\aa\bb\int_0^\infty\di x\di y\,
 x^{\ii\tn-1}y^{\ii\tn-1}(1+\ii \aa x)(1+\ii \bb y)e^{-\ii \aa x-\ii \bb y}
 \,y\mathcal K_{1;\bb\aa}(1;y,x)
 =\mathcal H_1(1+\ii\tn,\ii\tn;1).
 \label{eq:separate-endpoint-seeds}
\end{align}
The second uses the seed with its two arguments and SK labels in
the reversed order. These are equal values of two completed time
integrals, not a symmetrization of the unintegrated fermion factor.
Adding them gives
\begin{align}
 \mathcal P_1(\tn)&=\mathcal H_0(\ii\tn,\ii\tn;1)
 +\frac{2\ii}{1+2\ii\tn}\mathcal H_1(1+\ii\tn,\ii\tn;1),\nonumber\\
 \mathcal P_2(\tn)&=
 \left.(\partial_r-1)\mathcal H_0(\ii\tn,\ii\tn;r)\right|_{r=1}.
 \label{eq:hard-result}
\end{align}
The first coefficient includes both the hard-direction expansion
and the two soft endpoint descendants; the second combines the
hard-magnitude derivative with the longitudinal direction correction.
The same functions apply on the right.
Combining the already evaluated loop with the two time integrals gives
\begin{equation}
 \left.\G^{\rm top}\right|_{s,\rm clock}
 =-\frac{N_cg_t^4H^4}{16\prod_i k_i^3}
 \left[
 \frac{8c^2 k_s^{5+4\ii\tn}}{(k_Lk_R)^{1+2\ii\tn}}
 \sum_{i,j=1}^2\mathscr L_{ij}\mathcal P_i\mathcal P_j
 +\mathrm{c.c.}\right].
 \label{eq:loop-first-assembled}
\end{equation}
It remains to express the scalar time moments through the existing
Whittaker integral seed; no spin trace or momentum integral remains.

\paragraph{Seed normalization.}
We keep the scalar propagator normalization in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:scalar-chemical}. With $p=rk_L$, $x=-k_L\tau_1$
and $y=-k_L\tau_2$, write its dimensionless version as
\[
 \Dsc{\kappa}_{\aa\bb}(r;x,y)
 \equiv k_L\Dsc{\kappa}_{\aa\bb}(p;-x/k_L,-y/k_L).
\]
The two hard coefficients in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-scalar-coefficients}
then contain $A_x=x\partial_x-\ii rx-1/2$ and
$R_x=-x\partial_x-\ii rx+1/2$, with common prefactor
$\ii/(2\sqrt{xy})$. Define their scalar time moments by
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal J_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;P,Q}(r)
 =\int_0^\infty\di x\di y\,
 x^P y^Q e^{-\ii \aa x-\ii \bb y}\Dsc{\kappa}_{\aa\bb}(r;x,y)
 =-\aa\bb\,r^{-3-P-Q}\mathscr I_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;P,Q}(u,u),
 \quad u=\frac{2r}{1+r}.
 \label{eq:seed-normalization}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathscr I$ is precisely the Whittaker seed of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}, Eq.~(11), with
mass index $\tn$ and $\ii h\widetilde\mu=\kappa$.
The phase $e^{\ii\pi\kappa}$ is already in $\Dsc{\kappa}$;
the seed definition includes $-\aa\bb$.
There is no Hankel mass-index shift.

\paragraph{Removing the time derivatives.}
Expand the external factors as
\[
 (1+\ii \aa x)(1+\ii \bb y)=\sum_{j,l=0}^1(\ii \aa)^j(\ii \bb)^l x^j y^l.
\]
For each term put $P=\alpha-3/2+j$, $Q=\beta-3/2+l$.
Integration by parts turns $x\partial_x$ into
$-(P+1)+\ii \aa x$. Consequently
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_0(\alpha,\beta;r)
 ={}&\frac{\ii}{2}\sum_{\aa,\bb=\pm}\sum_{j,l=0}^1
 \aa\bb(\ii \aa)^j(\ii \bb)^l
 \Big[(P+\tfrac32+\ii\tn)
    (\mathcal J_{\aa\bb}^{-;P,Q}-\mathcal J_{\aa\bb}^{+;P,Q})
 \nonumber\\[-1mm]
 &\hspace{34mm}
 +\ii(\aa-r)\mathcal J_{\aa\bb}^{+;P+1,Q}
 -\ii(\aa+r)\mathcal J_{\aa\bb}^{-;P+1,Q}\Big],
 \label{eq:whittaker-H0}\\
 \mathcal H_1(\alpha,\beta;r)
 ={}&\frac{\ii}{2}\sum_{\aa,\bb=\pm}\sum_{j,l=0}^1
 \aa\bb(\ii \aa)^j(\ii \bb)^l
 \Big[(P+\tfrac32-\ii\tn)
    (\mathcal J_{\aa\bb}^{-;P,Q}+\mathcal J_{\aa\bb}^{+;P,Q})
 \nonumber\\[-1mm]
 &\hspace{34mm}
 -\ii(\aa-r)\mathcal J_{\aa\bb}^{+;P+1,Q}
 -\ii(\aa+r)\mathcal J_{\aa\bb}^{-;P+1,Q}\Big].
 \label{eq:whittaker-H1}
\end{align}
All moments have argument $r$, and their superscripts $+$ and $-$
mean $\kappa=\pm1/2$. These two formulas apply both to equal and opposite
soft branches. They use only the existing scalar Whittaker seed and
integer shifts of its time powers.

Integration by parts is first performed with a common endpoint
regulator in a domain where its boundary terms vanish, and with the BD
early-time prescription. The complete seed combination is then
continued to $\delta\to0$. Boundary terms or poles of separate SK
terms must not be dropped before this combination.

\paragraph{Closed-form seed values.}
At $r=1$ the needed moments use
$\mathscr S_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;P,Q}
=\mathscr I_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;P,Q}(1,1)$.
For compactness $\Gamma[z_1,\ldots,z_n]$ denotes the product of
Gamma functions. The results of Ref.~\cite{qin-seed},
Eqs.~(130)--(131), become
\begin{align}
 \mathscr S_{\aa,-\aa}^{\kappa;P,Q}
 ={}&\frac{e^{-\ii \aa\pi(P-Q)/2+\ii\pi\kappa}}{2^{3+P+Q}}
 \frac{\Gamma[\tfrac32+P-\ii\tn,\tfrac32+P+\ii\tn,
               \tfrac32+Q-\ii\tn,\tfrac32+Q+\ii\tn]}
      {\Gamma[2+P+\aa\kappa,2+Q-\aa\kappa]},
 \label{eq:whittaker-opposite}\\
 \mathscr S_{\aa\aa}^{\kappa;P,Q}
 ={}&\frac{-\aa\ii\,e^{-\ii \aa\pi(P+Q)/2}}{2^{3+P+Q}}
 \Gamma[\tfrac32+P+\aa\ii\tn,\tfrac32+Q+\aa\ii\tn]
 \nonumber\\
 &\times\Bigg\{
 \frac{e^{2\ii\pi\kappa}+e^{-2\pi\tn}}{2\pi}
 \frac{\Gamma[\tfrac32+P-\aa\ii\tn,\tfrac32+Q-\aa\ii\tn,
               \tfrac12+\aa\kappa-\ii\tn,\tfrac12+\aa\kappa+\ii\tn]}
      {\Gamma[2+P+\aa\kappa,2+Q+\aa\kappa]}
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{9mm}
 +\aa\ii\Gamma(3+P+Q)\,
 {}_3\widetilde F_2\!
 \left(\begin{matrix}3+P+Q,\ \tfrac12-\aa\kappa+\aa\ii\tn,\ 1\\
 \tfrac52+P+\aa\ii\tn,\ \tfrac52+Q+\aa\ii\tn\end{matrix};1\right)
 \Bigg\}.
 \label{eq:whittaker-equal}
\end{align}
The regularized function is
\begin{equation}
 {}_3\widetilde F_2(A,B,C;D,E;1)
 =\frac{{}_3F_2(A,B,C;D,E;1)}{\Gamma(D)\Gamma(E)}.
 \label{eq:regularized-hyper}
\end{equation}
At $\kappa=\pm1/2$ some displayed series have zero real convergence
balance. The expression denotes analytic continuation, not direct
summation of a divergent series. A useful convergent representation is
\begin{equation}
 {}_3\widetilde F_2(A,B,C;D,E;1)
 =\frac{\Gamma(s)}
        {\Gamma(E-C)\Gamma(D)\Gamma(s+C)}
 {}_3F_2(D-A,D-B,C;D,s+C;1),
 .
 \label{eq:whittaker-thomae}
\end{equation}
Here $s=D+E-A-B-C$. Permuting upper and lower parameters allows a representation with
$\operatorname{Re}(E-C)>0$. This identity follows by applying the
Euler transformation inside the beta-integral representation and
continuing analytically.

\paragraph{Hard-momentum derivative.}
The Whittaker bootstrap equation also determines the derivative
needed for $\mathcal P_2$. Define
\[
 T_{\aa\bb}^{P,Q}=\delta_{\aa\bb}\,
 e^{-\ii \aa\pi(P+Q)/2}2^{-3-P-Q}\Gamma(3+P+Q).
\]
At $u_1=u_2=1$ its second-derivative coefficient vanishes, giving
\begin{align}
 \mathscr R_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;P,Q}
 &\equiv\left.\frac{\di}{\di r}
 \mathscr I_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;P,Q}(u(r),u(r))\right|_{r=1}\nonumber\\
 &=\frac12\left[
 \frac{\tn^2+(P+\tfrac32)^2}{2+P+\aa\kappa}
 +\frac{\tn^2+(Q+\tfrac32)^2}{2+Q+\bb\kappa}\right]
 \mathscr S_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;P,Q}
 +\frac12\left[\frac1{2+P+\aa\kappa}
             +\frac1{2+Q+\bb\kappa}\right]T_{\aa\bb}^{P,Q},
 \label{eq:seed-bootstrap-derivative}\\
 \left.\partial_r\mathcal J_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;P,Q}\right|_{r=1}
 &=-\aa\bb\left[\mathscr R_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;P,Q}
 -(3+P+Q)\mathscr S_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;P,Q}\right].
 \label{eq:whittaker-moment-derivative}
\end{align}
Differentiate Eq.~\eqref{eq:whittaker-H0}, including its explicit
$\aa\pm r$ factors, and use these moment derivatives.
The required coefficients are therefore
\begin{align}
 \mathcal P_1&=\lim_{\delta\to0}
 \left[\mathcal H_0(\ii\tn+\delta,\ii\tn+\delta;1)
 +\frac{2\ii}{1+2\ii\tn}
 \mathcal H_1(1+\ii\tn+\delta,\ii\tn+\delta;1)\right],\nonumber\\
 \mathcal P_2&=\lim_{\delta\to0}
 \left.(\partial_r-1)
 \mathcal H_0(\ii\tn+\delta,\ii\tn+\delta;r)\right|_{r=1}.
 \label{eq:whittaker-hard-coefficients}
\end{align}
Thus both hard subgraphs are calculated entirely from Whittaker
scalar seed values; no new time integral is left.

 \mathcal Q_{jl}=\frac{2^{-1-2z-j-l}\Gamma(1+2z+j+l)}

 2+2z+j,\ 2+2z+l\end{matrix};1\right).
 \label{eq:finite-Q}
\end{equation}
Only $\mathcal Q_{00},\mathcal Q_{01},\mathcal Q_{11},
\mathcal Q_{02},\mathcal Q_{12}$ are needed. Also abbreviate the
finite endpoint contribution by
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{\mathrm{end}}=\pi^2 2^{-1-2z}
 \frac{\Gamma(1+2z)^2}{\Gamma(1+z)^2}.
 \label{eq:endpoint-finite}
\end{equation}
The fully summed hard seeds and momentum derivative are

 \mathcal H_0(z,z;1)

 \mathcal H_1(1+z,z;1)
 &=-2\ii\cosh(\pi\tn)(\mathcal Q_{02}+\mathcal Q_{12}),
 \label{eq:finite-hard-seeds}\\

 \nonumber\\
 &\quad +(1+2z)\mathcal H_{\mathrm{end}}
 +2^{-1-2z}\cosh(\pi\tn)\Gamma(1+2z)

 \label{eq:finite-P2}
\end{align}
Together with $\mathcal P_1$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}, these are
finite, convergent special-function expressions for the complete hard
coefficients. No raw mode-power-series truncation or numerical
regulator extrapolation is required for the oscillatory signal.

\subsection{Total oscillatory trispectrum}
Rewriting Eq.~\eqref{eq:loop-first-assembled} in the usual clock ratio gives
the leading nonzero oscillatory term, summed over the four $s$-cut boxes:
\begin{align}
 \left.\langle\delta h_{\bk_1}\delta h_{\bk_2}\delta h_{\bk_3}\delta h_{\bk_4}\rangle'
 \right|_{s,\rm clock}
 ={}&-\frac{N_cg_t^4H^4 k_s^5}{16\,k_1^3k_2^3k_3^3k_4^3 k_Lk_R}\nonumber\\
 &\times\left[\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)^{2\ii\tn}
 \cC(\tn;\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)+\mathrm{c.c.}\right].
 \label{eq:final}
\end{align}
Here $\cC$ denotes the top-quark box coefficient.
Using the same angular basis as the SMS manuscript, write
\begin{align}
 \cC={}&A_1+A_2(\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_3)
 +A_3\cos^2\theta_1\cos^2\theta_3\nonumber\\
 &+B\sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3
 +D\sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3).
 \label{eq:angular-final}
\end{align}
The five functions $A_1,A_2,A_3,B,D$ depend only on $\tn$;
$D$ here is an angular coefficient, not a propagator.
All five coefficients share one soft-bubble normalization, including the two
soft-mode coefficients $c^2$, the factor $4$ from the two Bose-summed hard blocks, the factor $2$ from the Pauli trace, and the phase from the chosen momentum ratio:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B(\tn)=
 \frac{2^{1+4\ii\tn}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii\tn)^4 f_0(\rho,\rho)}
 {32\pi^2(4\pi)^{3/2}(\rho-3)(\rho-2)(4\rho-7)(4\rho-5)},\qquad
 \rho=\tfrac12-\ii\tn.
 \label{eq:bubble-normalization}
\end{equation}
In particular, the natural hard scaling is
$(k_s^2/(k_Lk_R))^{2\ii\tn}$. Since
$4k_{12}k_{34}=16k_Lk_R$ at this order, using the displayed
ratio transfers $16^{2\ii\tn}$ into the coefficient. Thus
$8c^2\,16^{2\ii\tn}
=2^{1+4\ii\tn}\Gamma(1/2-\ii\tn)^4/\pi^2$, which fixes both
the phase and the overall factor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bubble-normalization}.
Combining the bubble with the two hard coefficients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}
gives the final mass dependence:
\begin{align}
 A_1&=\mathcal B(\rho-1)(2\rho-3)
 \big[4(\rho-3)(3\mathcal P_1^2+2\mathcal P_1\mathcal P_2)
 +(2\rho-5)\mathcal P_2^2\big],\\*
 A_2&=-\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\rho-1)(2\rho-3)^2,\qquad A_3=-3A_2,\\*
 B&=-2\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\rho-1)(\rho-2)(2\rho-3),\\*
 D&=\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\rho-2)(2\rho-5)(2\rho-3).
 \label{eq:compact-coefficients}
\end{align}
Thus the answer requires only two hard functions $\mathcal P_1,\mathcal P_2$
and one explicit bubble factor $\mathcal B$. The coefficients are complex and
include the clock phase. The full answer is even under either Higgs-pair exchange
and under parity. Its engineering dimension is $-5$, as required for the primed
four-point function. The powers of $H$ follow from four rescaled Weyl Yukawa
vertices and four external Higgs contractions; no extra factor of $H$ or a
second color/orientation multiplicity is implicit. For fixed $\tn>0$ these are the leading double-soft nonanalytic
terms; higher soft powers and analytic contact terms are not displayed.
With the centered hard momenta the next clock terms begin at
$k_s^{7\pm4\ii\tn}$, and the next mixed terms at $k_s^5$.
Thus the generic relative error within each branch is
$O(k_s^2/k_L^2,k_s^2/k_R^2,k_s^2/(k_Lk_R))$; it is not a
relative-error bound at a zero of the displayed angular coefficient.
\section{Nonoscillatory contribution}
\label{sec:mixed}
\subsection{Left and right subgraphs}
Taking opposite soft branches gives a nonoscillatory $k_s^3$ term.
For $\eta=-1$, the leading terms in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:weyl-soft-leading}--\eqref{eq:check-soft-expansion}
are represented by
\[
 L_{\mathrm L}^-=\one,\qquad
 L_{\overline{\mathrm L}}^-=-\epsmat,\qquad
 U_{\mathrm L}^-=\epsmat,\qquad
 U_{\overline{\mathrm L}}^-=-\one,
\]
with scalar prefactor $+c^*/2$, compared with $-c/2$ for $\eta=+1$.
Use $U^-_r$ at one soft endpoint and $L^+_s$ at the other in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:weyl-hard-block}, and interchange $+,-$ at the right end.
The superscripts $-+$ and $+-$ below label the two nonlocal mass
branches at the endpoints; the SK labels remain on the hard propagator.
At zeroth order their explicit four-type sums give
\begin{align}
 V_L^{-+,(0)}&=-\frac12\sum_{r,s}U_r^-J_rD^{rs}J_sL_s^+
 =\mathcal K_{1;\aa_1\aa_2}(\tau_1,\tau_2)\one,\nonumber\\
 V_R^{+-,(0)}&=-\frac12\sum_{r,s}U_r^+J_rD^{rs}J_sL_s^-
 =\mathcal K_{1;\aa_4\aa_3}(\tau_4,\tau_3)\one.
 \label{eq:mixed-weyl-hard}
\end{align}
The soft prefactor for this assignment is
$-|c|^2(q^2)^{-\ii\tn}(K^2)^{\ii\tn}$, from the opposite signs
of the two branch prefactors displayed above.
Its minus sign cancels the closed-loop sign in Eq.~\eqref{eq:each-box}.
The endpoint time powers are included when integrating these hard
matrices. Relabelling $(\tau_3,\aa_3)\leftrightarrow(\tau_4,\aa_4)$
in the right matrix makes both integrated hard coefficients equal to
$\mathcal H_1(-\ii\tn,+\ii\tn;1)$.
Each hard end therefore has a Bose-even
zeroth-order term. There are two soft assignments and two Higgs orderings
at each end, giving $2\times2\times2$ contributions; the spin trace
adds $2\widehat{\bq}\cdot\widehat{\mb K}$. The soft coefficients give
$|c|^2=1/(4\cosh^2\pi\tn)$. These factors fix the normalization of
the mixed term independently of the oscillatory branch.
\subsection{The mixed hard coefficient}
The same Whittaker reduction, Eq.~\eqref{eq:whittaker-H1}, gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M(\tn)=\lim_{\delta\to0}
 \mathcal H_1(-\ii\tn+\delta,+\ii\tn+\delta;1).
 \label{eq:mixed-seed-eval}
\end{equation}
Its scalar seed powers start at
$P=-3/2-\ii\tn+\delta$ and $Q=-3/2+\ii\tn+\delta$,
with the integer shifts specified in Eq.~\eqref{eq:whittaker-H1}.
All SK branches and both $\kappa=\pm1/2$ are combined before
the regulator is removed. The exact conjugation relation
$\mathcal H_1(\alpha,\beta;1)^*
=\mathcal H_1(\beta^*,\alpha^*;1)$ makes $\mathcal M$ real.
The implementation takes a fourth-order extrapolated common-regulator
limit and checks the step size against the independent reference.
For example, $\mathcal M(0.73)=-1.388500280198814$.

\subsection{Central integral and total trispectrum}
The scalar bubble in this case is
\begin{equation}
 \int\frac{\di^3\bq}{(2\pi)^3}(q^2)^{-\ii\tn}(K^2)^{\ii\tn}
 \widehat{\bq}\cdot\widehat{\mb K}
 =\frac{k_s^3}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\,
 \frac{2\sqrt\pi}{3}\tn(1+\tn^2)\coth(\pi\tn).
\end{equation}
After both soft branches and all four $s$-cut boxes are summed, the result is
\begin{equation}
 \left.\langle\delta h^4\rangle'\right|_{s,\mathrm{mixed}}
 =\frac{N_cg_t^4H^4 k_s^3}{16\prod_i k_i^3}
 \frac{2\tn(1+\tn^2)}{3\pi\sinh(2\pi\tn)}
 \mathcal M(\tn)^2.
 \label{eq:mixed}
\end{equation}
It is independent of angles at this order. Equation~\eqref{eq:mixed} is the
leading nonoscillatory nonanalytic term, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:final} is the
leading mass-dependent clock signal. At $\tn=0$ the separated branches coalesce;
the limit must be taken after combining the relevant terms.
Here $k_s^3=(\bk_s^2)^{3/2}$ is nonanalytic as a function of the
soft momentum components, even though it has no mass-dependent oscillation.
It cannot be discarded as a polynomial contact term.
The remaining real coefficient is evaluated with the same scalar seed
through Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed-seed-eval}.
For example, $\mathcal M(0.73)=-1.388500280198814$.

The results above include the leading oscillatory and nonoscillatory
nonanalytic terms for a single collapsed channel. Analytic contact terms,
overlapping collapsed channels and Higgs-to-curvature conversion are
outside the calculation. Section~\ref{sec:checks} specifies the numerical
checks and their limits.

\section{Numerical checks and scope}
\label{sec:checks}
The numerical routines retain the parameter name \texttt{mu} for
$\tn=m_t/H$ and output keys \texttt{B1}, \texttt{D1} for the angular
coefficients $B,D$. Their definitions and normalization are unchanged.
The Whittaker reduction is implemented in
\path{checks/whittaker_scalar_seed.py} and tested by
\path{checks/whittaker_seed_checks.py}.
The tests compare all three scalar-derived spinor propagators with
independent Whittaker mode contractions, for every SK pair at
$\tn=0.2,0.73,2,5$. They also check the two hard coefficients,
the zero-chemical-potential seed normalization, the Whittaker bootstrap
derivative, the regulated hard integrals, and the complete-SK limit.
The former Hankel implementation is retained only as an independent
reference for these numerical comparisons; it is not used in the
Whittaker seed evaluator. The checks below describe the additional
tests retained from the previous implementation.

The Whittaker implementation constructs all normal and anomalous
contractions directly from Eq.~\eqref{eq:mode-expansion}. It verifies
EOMs, normalization and the Hankel map at $\tn=0.2,0.73,2,5$.
The corresponding explicit Whittaker products are checked separately
in \texttt{checks/explicit\_}\allowbreak\texttt{propagator\_checks.py}: all three propagators
and all SK assignments agree with the mode contractions at four masses,
three momentum directions and three time pairs. An alternative Hankel
calculation uses explicit helicity spinors; the largest residual is
$3.1\times10^{-16}$. Conjugation and equal-time anticommutators are
checked as well. Raising special-function precision from 30 to 60 digits
leaves the resulting double-precision matrices unchanged. These are
free-propagator consistency checks, not an independent box integration.
The independent eight-slot Wick enumerator gives 48 connected pairings
and agrees with the cycle formula, and with half the Dirac result, for
all 16 SK assignments at $\tn=0.2,0.73,2$. Its test momenta are generic
edge momenta: this is a local contraction test, not a momentum integral.
The original-field implementation in \texttt{checks/aoki\_weyl\_checks.py}
also compares both soft branches with an independent Bessel decomposition
at $\tn=0.2,0.73,2,5$. It checks the four-type hard sums against Hankel
scalar products, both mixed assignments, and the factorized spin cut
for all 16 SK choices at the first three masses. The first-descendant
remainder decreases quadratically with the soft momentum. These tests
use the original $\psi,\psi^\dagger$ contractions.
The soft-expansion check retains the untruncated nonlocal mode branches,
Whittaker hard lines, both external energy shifts and all four
orderings. On a finite time interval, at three masses and two geometries,
its error relative to the leading nonzero coefficient decreases by
approximately four when the soft momenta are halved. The nonzero
single-cycle zeroth-order trace and its exchange cancellation are
checked separately. Time-quadrature refinement is also tested.

A further independent calculation integrates the fermion Hankel kernels
on BD-rotated contours at the physical mass indices: the two same-SK
ordered triangles rotate together, and the two opposite-SK integrals
rotate separately. It checks both $\mathcal H_1$ and $\mathcal H_0$ scalar hard
integrals and the hard-momentum derivative at $\tn=0.2,0.73$, with a common positive endpoint regulator
$\delta=0.8$, including a mixed-branch case. The derivative is obtained directly from
the first-order mode equations, independently of the scalar bootstrap
source; the regulated $\mathcal P_1,\mathcal P_2$ are compared as well. This tests the scalar-seed
normalization beyond the conformal-scalar example. It does not directly
evaluate the zero-regulator limit; that limit still uses the analytically
summed seeds and regulator-path checks.

The retained scalar-seed tests check the elementary conformal-scalar
normalization, Gamma reductions, regulator paths and working precision.
Comparing the regulated and analytically summed versions of the same
seed is a consistency check, not a second derivation of that seed.
Neither finite-interval soft tests nor special-function agreement is
a direct numerical integration of the full unexpanded BD box.
The analytic contact part and its renormalization, overlapping collapsed
channels, evolving masses, nonzero chemical potential and curvature
conversion have not been computed. Reproducible commands and residuals
are recorded in \texttt{checks/top\_quark\_audit.md}.
