\section{Two-Weyl setup and Feynman rules}
\subsection{Lagrangian, basis and mode functions}
We start with the top sector of the Standard Model before choosing a
coordinate system. In the usual left/right notation, let
$Q_{3L}=(t_L,b_L)^T$ and
$\widetilde\Phi=(\Phi^{2*},-\Phi^{1*})^T$. Its relevant curved-space
action is, in four-component chiral notation,
\begin{align*}
 S_{\rm top}=\int\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\big[
 &\ii\bar Q_{3L}\gamma^a e_a{}^\mu D_\mu Q_{3L}
 +\ii\bar t_R\gamma^a e_a{}^\mu D_\mu t_R\\
 &-y_t\big(\bar Q_{3L}\widetilde\Phi\,t_R
              +\bar t_R\widetilde\Phi^\dagger Q_{3L}\big)\big].
\end{align*}
To express everything with left-handed two-component spinors, identify
each chiral field with its nonzero Weyl component and replace the
right-handed $t_R$ by its charge conjugate:
\begin{equation*}
 Q_3=(t_{\rm cov},b_{\rm cov})^T,\qquad
 t_{\rm cov}=t_L,\qquad
 (t_R)^C=C\bar t_R^{\,T},\qquad
 t^c_{\rm cov}=\big[(t_R)^C\big]_L.
\end{equation*}
Here $C$ is the charge-conjugation matrix. Equivalently, for the
two-component right-handed spinor,
$t^c_{\rm cov}=\ii\sigma^2 t_R^*$, with $\sigma^2$ acting on spinor
indices. Thus $t^c$ is the same right-handed top degree of freedom
written as a left-handed charge-conjugate field. Its SM gauge charges
are conjugated, so its covariant derivative acts in the conjugate
representation. In particular,
\begin{align*}
 \bar t_R\widetilde\Phi^\dagger Q_{3L}
 &\longleftrightarrow
 (\Phi^2t_{\rm cov}-\Phi^1b_{\rm cov})t^c_{\rm cov}
 =\epsilon_{ij}Q_3^i\Phi^j t^c_{\rm cov},\\
 \ii\bar t_R\gamma^a e_a{}^\mu D_\mu t_R
 &\longleftrightarrow
 \ii t_{\rm cov}^{c\dagger}\bar\sigma^a e_a{}^\mu
 D_\mu t^c_{\rm cov},
\end{align*}
where $\epsilon_{12}=+1$ and the kinetic identity holds up to a total
derivative. We use the
phase of $t^c_{\rm cov}$ for which the Yukawa term has the sign below.
The action therefore becomes
\begin{align}
 S_{\rm top}=\int\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\big[
 &\ii Q_3^\dagger\bar\sigma^a e_a{}^\mu D_\mu Q_3
 +\ii t_{\rm cov}^{c\dagger}\bar\sigma^a e_a{}^\mu D_\mu t^c_{\rm cov}
 \nonumber\\
 &-y_t\big(\epsilon_{ij}Q_3^i\Phi^j t^c_{\rm cov}
       +\mathrm{h.c.}\big)\big].
 \label{eq:covariant-top}
\end{align}
Here $\Phi$ is the SM Higgs doublet, $D_\mu$ contains the spin and
SM gauge connections, and $\sigma^a=(\mathbf1_2,\bs)$,
$\bar\sigma^a=(\mathbf1_2,-\bs)$ with
$\eta_{ab}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$. Gauge backgrounds are set
to zero in the Yukawa box considered below. Choosing the neutral Higgs
background
$\Phi=(0,(h_0+\delta h)/\sqrt2)^T$ selects the top component of
$Q_3$. We assume $h_0$ is constant, and write
\begin{equation}
 g_t=\frac{y_t}{\sqrt2},\qquad m_t=g_t h_0,\qquad
 \mu=\frac{m_t}{H},\qquad N_c=3.
\end{equation}
The two-component contraction $t_{\rm cov}t^c_{\rm cov}$ includes its
antisymmetric spinor epsilon tensor. Since the fields are distinct,
the Yukawa term has no factor of $1/2$.

In conformal de Sitter coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \dd s^2=a(\tau)^2(\dd\tau^2-\dd\bm x^2),\qquad
 a(\tau)=-\frac1{H\tau},\quad \tau<0,\qquad
 e_a{}^\mu=a^{-1}\delta_a{}^\mu .
\end{equation}
Rescale the two Weyl fields by their conformal weight,
\begin{equation}
 t=a^{3/2}t_{\rm cov},\qquad t^c=a^{3/2}t^c_{\rm cov}.
 \label{eq:weyl-rescaling}
\end{equation}
The tetrad and spin connection give
$\ii a^3t_{\rm cov}^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu
(\partial_\mu+3a'\delta_\mu^0/2a)t_{\rm cov}$, and likewise for
$t^c_{\rm cov}$. The extra $3a'/2a$ cancels the derivative of
$a^{-3/2}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:weyl-rescaling}. The Yukawa bilinear
instead retains one factor of $a^4(a^{-3/2})^2=a$. Thus the action for
the top sector becomes
\begin{align}
 S=\int\dd\tau\dd^3x\big\{
 &\ii t^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu t
 +\ii t^{c\dagger}\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu t^c
 \nonumber\\*[-1mm]
 &-a(\tau)(m_t+g_t\delta h)
       (tt^c+t^\dagger t^{c\dagger})\big\}.
 \label{eq:action}
\end{align}
All fermion fields from here onward are the rescaled fields. The
background mass $m_t$ belongs to the free equation; each
$\delta h$ insertion supplies one Yukawa vertex.

To keep the two Weyl equations in one matrix equation, introduce the
four-component Dirac column
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_W=\binom{t_\alpha}{t^{c\dagger\dot\alpha}},\qquad
 \beta_W=\begin{pmatrix}0&\one\\ \one&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The epsilon contractions in Eq.~\eqref{eq:action} are understood in
this column notation, so its mass bilinear is
$-a m_t\Psi_W^\dagger\beta_W\Psi_W$ in our index convention.
Under top number, $t\mapsto e^{\ii\vartheta}t$ and
$t^c\mapsto e^{-\ii\vartheta}t^c$. Both entries of $\Psi_W$ therefore
have the same charge; no Majorana reality condition is imposed.

The mass matrix is diagonal in a simple orthonormal component basis.
Rather than naming its two basis columns separately, define
\begin{equation}
 U_B=\frac1{\sqrt2}
 \begin{pmatrix}\one&-\one\\ \one&\one\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Psi=U_B^\dagger\Psi_W,\qquad
 \beta=U_B^\dagger\beta_WU_B
      =\begin{pmatrix}\one&0\\0&-\one\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:basis-change}
\end{equation}
This unitary change acts only on spinor components. It preserves the
top charge, canonical anticommutators, and traces.

Use the Fourier convention
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_W(\tau,\bm x)
 =\int\frac{\dd^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\,
   e^{\ii\bm k\cdot\bm x}\Psi_W(\tau,\bm k),
 \qquad \partial_i\longmapsto\ii k_i .
 \label{eq:fermion-fourier}
\end{equation}
Varying the quadratic action and then applying $U_B$ gives, with the
same momentum on both sides,
\begin{align}
 \ii\partial_\tau\Psi_W(\tau,\bm k)
 &=\begin{pmatrix}
   -\bs\cdot\bm k&a m_t\one\\
   a m_t\one&\bs\cdot\bm k
   \end{pmatrix}\Psi_W(\tau,\bm k),
 \nonumber\\
 \ii\partial_\tau\Psi(\tau,\bm k)
 &=\begin{pmatrix}
   a m_t\one&\bs\cdot\bm k\\
   \bs\cdot\bm k&-a m_t\one
   \end{pmatrix}\Psi(\tau,\bm k).
 \label{eq:weyl-eom}
\end{align}
The matrix in the second line is Hermitian; at fixed time its square
is $(k^2+a^2m_t^2)\mathbf1_4$.

For nonzero momentum, write $k=|\bm k|>0$ and
$\bm k=k(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)$.
We use the following explicit normalized helicity basis:
\begin{equation}
 \xi_+(\bm k)=
 \binom{e^{-\ii\phi/2}\cos(\theta/2)}
       {e^{\ii\phi/2}\sin(\theta/2)},\qquad
 \xi_-(\bm k)=
 \binom{-e^{-\ii\phi/2}\sin(\theta/2)}
       {e^{\ii\phi/2}\cos(\theta/2)}.
 \label{eq:helicity-explicit}
\end{equation}
They obey
\begin{equation}
 (\bs\cdot\hat{\bm k})\xi_h=h\xi_h,\qquad
 \xi_h^\dagger\xi_{h'}=\delta_{hh'},\qquad
 \sum_{h=\pm1}\xi_h\xi_h^\dagger=\one.
 \label{eq:helicity-basis}
\end{equation}
The phases in Eq.~\eqref{eq:helicity-explicit} are a convention;
the subsequent propagators depend only on the helicity projectors.
For fixed nonzero momentum, ``positive frequency'' means a mode
approaching $e^{-\ii k\tau}$ in the far past $\tau\to-\infty$;
``negative frequency'' means one approaching $e^{+\ii k\tau}$.
Indeed, $\ii\partial_\tau e^{\mp\ii k\tau}
=\pm k e^{\mp\ii k\tau}$.
The superscript $(+)$ or $(-)$ on a mode denotes this frequency,
while $h=\pm1$ denotes helicity. Neither sign is an SK branch label.
We first solve for the positive-frequency mode:
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_h^{(+)}(\tau,\bm k)
 =\binom{F(x)\xi_h}{hG(x)\xi_h},
 \qquad x=-k\tau>0.
 \label{eq:positive-mode}
\end{equation}
The factor $h$ in the lower entry makes the same two radial functions
work for both helicities.
Since $a m_t=\mu k/x$ and $\partial_\tau=-k\partial_x$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:weyl-eom} reduces to
\begin{align}
 -\ii F'&=\frac{\mu}{x}F+G,&
 -\ii G'&=F-\frac{\mu}{x}G,
 \label{eq:radial-first-order}\\
 F''+\left[1+\frac{\mu^2+\ii\mu}{x^2}\right]F&=0,&
 G''+\left[1+\frac{\mu^2-\ii\mu}{x^2}\right]G&=0.
 \label{eq:radial-second-order}
\end{align}
The second line follows by eliminating one component from the first.
These are Bessel equations with orders $1/2-\ii\mu$ and
$1/2+\ii\mu$. The positive-frequency BD condition is
$e^{-\ii k\tau}=e^{\ii x}$, so we choose Hankel functions of the
first kind. Normalizing the positive-frequency column to unit norm
fixes
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{F(x)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi x}}2e^{\pi\mu/2}
 H^{(1)}_{1/2-\ii\mu}(x),\qquad
 G(x)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi x}}2e^{-\pi\mu/2}
 H^{(1)}_{1/2+\ii\mu}(x).}
 \label{eq:modes}
\end{equation}
The relative coefficients in this pair follow directly from
$G=-\ii F'-\mu F/x$ and the Hankel identities
$H_{\nu-1}^{(1)}=H_\nu^{(1)\prime}+\nu H_\nu^{(1)}/x$ and
$H_{-1/2-\ii\mu}^{(1)}=\ii e^{-\pi\mu}H_{1/2+\ii\mu}^{(1)}$.
Both functions approach $-\ii e^{\ii x}/\sqrt2$ as $x\to\infty$.
The first-order equations imply
$\partial_\tau(|F|^2+|G|^2)=0$, so this asymptotic normalization gives
$|F|^2+|G|^2=1$ at every time. The common phase is conventional; the
relative phase between $F$ and $G$ is fixed by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:radial-first-order}.
The independent negative-frequency mode at the same Fourier momentum
is then
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_h^{(-)}(\tau,\bm k)
 =\binom{G(x)^*\xi_h}{-hF(x)^*\xi_h}
 \ \propto e^{+\ii k\tau}\quad(\tau\to-\infty).
 \label{eq:negative-mode}
\end{equation}
Here $^*$ means complex conjugation of the time-dependent functions;
the relative minus sign makes this column solve the same field
equation at momentum $\bm k$.

\subsection{SK propagators}
Here $A,B=1,\ldots,4$ label components of the four-component spinor
$\Psi$; they are not SK indices. The SK branch labels will be
$s,s'\in\{+,-\}$. All propagators in this subsection are first
computed in the rotated $\Psi$ basis. Outer products of the modes
give the two Wightman functions, and contour ordering then gives the
four SK propagators.
The positive- and negative-frequency solutions can be collected into
two $4\times2$ matrices. Their two-dimensional column space represents
the two spin states; multiplying either matrix by $\xi_h$ selects
the helicity-$h$ solution:
\begin{equation}
 u(\bm k,\tau)=
 \binom{F(x)\one}{G(x)\bs\cdot\hat{\bm k}},\qquad
 v(\bm k,\tau)=
 \binom{G(x)^*\one}{-F(x)^*\bs\cdot\hat{\bm k}}.
 \label{eq:uv}
\end{equation}
Thus $u\xi_h=\Psi_h^{(+)}$ and $v\xi_h=\Psi_h^{(-)}$.
At fixed Fourier momentum the field operator expands as
\begin{align}
 \Psi(\tau,\bm k)
 ={}&\sum_{h=\pm1}
 \binom{F(x)\xi_h(\bm k)}{hG(x)\xi_h(\bm k)}
 b_h(\bm k)\nonumber\\
 &+\sum_{h=\pm1}
 \binom{G(x)^*\xi_h(\bm k)}{-hF(x)^*\xi_h(\bm k)}
 d_h^\dagger(-\bm k).
 \label{eq:mode-expansion}
\end{align}
For each $h$, $b_h(\bm k)$ annihilates a particle and
$d_h^\dagger(-\bm k)$ creates an antiparticle. The label $h$ on the
creation operator refers to the basis spinor $\xi_h(\bm k)$ used in
this Fourier expansion. The $-\bm k$ argument follows from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fermion-fourier}; color labels are suppressed.
The compact form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mode-expansion} is
$\Psi=ub+vd^\dagger$, where
$b(\bm k)=\sum_h\xi_h(\bm k)b_h(\bm k)$ and
$d^\dagger(-\bm k)=\sum_h\xi_h(\bm k)d_h^\dagger(-\bm k)$
are two-entry operator columns.
The dagger on an operator denotes its adjoint, whereas $^*$ on $F,G$
means complex conjugation and $u^\dagger,v^\dagger$ mean Hermitian
conjugation of matrices. The canonical
anticommutators, together with the mode normalization, give
$u^\dagger u=v^\dagger v=\one$, $u^\dagger v=0$, and
$uu^\dagger+vv^\dagger=\mathbf1_4$. The modes in the original Weyl
basis are $U_Bu$ and $U_Bv$.

Before choosing an SK branch, $R^>$ and $R^<$ denote the two
possible field orderings; the symbols $>$ and $<$ do not compare
$\tau$ with $\tau'$. Matrix multiplication sums over the two spin
states. We define these contractions without an extra prefactor of
$\ii$, and suppress the momentum-conserving delta function. With
the first argument at the field end of the ordered line,
\begin{align}
 R^>_{AB}(\bm k;\tau,\tau')
 &=\langle\Psi_A(\tau,\bm k)\Psi_B^\dagger(\tau',\bm k)\rangle
   =[u(\tau)u^\dagger(\tau')]_{AB},
 \nonumber\\
 R^<_{AB}(\bm k;\tau,\tau')
 &=-\langle\Psi_B^\dagger(\tau',\bm k)\Psi_A(\tau,\bm k)\rangle
   =-[v(\tau)v^\dagger(\tau')]_{AB}.
 \label{eq:wightman}
\end{align}
Here is the vacuum contraction that gives the second equality in each
line. Let $|0\rangle$ be the BD vacuum, so
$b_h(\bm k)|0\rangle=d_h(\bm k)|0\rangle=0$.
Taking the adjoint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mode-expansion} gives
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_B^\dagger(\tau',\bm k')=
 \sum_{h'=\pm1}\left[
 b_{h'}^\dagger(\bm k')\Psi_{h',B}^{(+)*}(\tau',\bm k')
 +d_{h'}(-\bm k')\Psi_{h',B}^{(-)*}(\tau',\bm k')
 \right].
 \label{eq:adjoint-mode-expansion}
\end{equation}
The only nonzero operator products between these vacuum states are
\begin{equation}
 \langle0|b_h(\bm k)b_{h'}^\dagger(\bm k')|0\rangle
 =\langle0|d_h(-\bm k)d_{h'}^\dagger(-\bm k')|0\rangle
 =(2\pi)^3\delta_{hh'}\delta^3(\bm k-\bm k').
 \label{eq:vacuum-operators}
\end{equation}
Substituting Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mode-expansion} and
\eqref{eq:adjoint-mode-expansion} into the two expectation values
leaves one operator product in each ordering. Suppressing the
arguments of the modes and operators for this intermediate step,
\begin{align}
 \langle0|\Psi_A\Psi_B^\dagger|0\rangle
 &=\sum_{h,h'}\Psi_{h,A}^{(+)}\Psi_{h',B}^{(+)*}
   \langle0|b_h b_{h'}^\dagger|0\rangle,\nonumber\\
 -\langle0|\Psi_B^\dagger\Psi_A|0\rangle
 &=-\sum_{h,h'}\Psi_{h',B}^{(-)*}\Psi_{h,A}^{(-)}
   \langle0|d_{h'} d_h^\dagger|0\rangle.
 \label{eq:vacuum-intermediate}
\end{align}
Terms with $b^\dagger b$, $d^\dagger d$, or a mixed $b,d$ product
vanish in the vacuum. Restoring the arguments and using
Eq.~\eqref{eq:vacuum-operators} gives
\begin{align}
 \langle0|\Psi_A(\tau,\bm k)\Psi_B^\dagger(\tau',\bm k')|0\rangle
 &=(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\bm k-\bm k')\nonumber\\
 &\quad\times\sum_h\Psi_{h,A}^{(+)}(\tau,\bm k)
             \Psi_{h,B}^{(+)*}(\tau',\bm k),\nonumber\\
 -\langle0|\Psi_B^\dagger(\tau',\bm k')\Psi_A(\tau,\bm k)|0\rangle
 &=-(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\bm k-\bm k')\nonumber\\
 &\quad\times\sum_h\Psi_{h,A}^{(-)}(\tau,\bm k)
             \Psi_{h,B}^{(-)*}(\tau',\bm k).
 \label{eq:vacuum-wightman}
\end{align}
Finally, Eq.~\eqref{eq:helicity-basis} converts the helicity sums
into matrix products. The common momentum $\bm k$ is suppressed in
this display:
\begin{align}
 \sum_h\Psi_h^{(+)}(\tau)\Psi_h^{(+)\dagger}(\tau')
 &=u(\tau)\left(\sum_h\xi_h\xi_h^\dagger\right)u^\dagger(\tau')
 =u(\tau)u^\dagger(\tau'),\nonumber\\*
 \sum_h\Psi_h^{(-)}(\tau)\Psi_h^{(-)\dagger}(\tau')
 &=v(\tau)\left(\sum_h\xi_h\xi_h^\dagger\right)v^\dagger(\tau')
 =v(\tau)v^\dagger(\tau').
 \label{eq:helicity-sums}
\end{align}
These are precisely the products in Eq.~\eqref{eq:wightman} after
removing the common momentum delta function. Both nonzero vacuum
products in Eq.~\eqref{eq:vacuum-operators} are positive; the minus
sign in $R^<$ comes from the fermionic ordering convention in its
definition, not from the $d d^\dagger$ vacuum product.

Write
$F_1=F(-k\tau)$, $F_2=F(-k\tau')$, and likewise for $G$; also write
$\sigma_k=\bs\cdot\hat{\bm k}$. Multiplication of the matrices in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uv} then gives
\begin{align}
 R^>&=\begin{pmatrix}
 F_1F_2^*\one&F_1G_2^*\sigma_k\\
 G_1F_2^*\sigma_k&G_1G_2^*\one
 \end{pmatrix},
 &
 R^<&=\begin{pmatrix}
 -G_1^*G_2\one&G_1^*F_2\sigma_k\\
 F_1^*G_2\sigma_k&-F_1^*F_2\one
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:Rblocks}
\end{align}
For example, the upper-right block of $R^<$ is positive because
$-\bigl(G_1^*\one\bigr)
 \bigl(-F_2^*\sigma_k\bigr)^\dagger
 =G_1^*F_2\sigma_k$.
This is a useful check on the relative sign of the two components
of $v$.

Let $+$ denote the forward SK branch and $-$ the backward branch.
In $R_{ss'}(\bm k;\tau,\tau')$, $s$ belongs to the first time
$\tau$ and $s'$ to the second time $\tau'$. Each $R_{ss'}$ remains a
$4\times4$ spinor matrix with entries $R_{ss';AB}$, and $\theta$ is
the step function.
Contour ordering gives
\begin{align}
 R_{-+}&=R^>,&
 R_{+-}&=R^<,\nonumber\\
 R_{++}&=\theta(\tau-\tau')R^>
          +\theta(\tau'-\tau)R^<,&
 R_{--}&=\theta(\tau'-\tau)R^>
          +\theta(\tau-\tau')R^<.
 \label{eq:sk}
\end{align}
Thus $R_{++}$ is time ordered and $R_{--}$ is reverse time
ordered. A line in the original Weyl basis uses
$R_W=U_BR U_B^\dagger$. Each displayed entry of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Rblocks} is a $2\times2$ block, and its diagonal blocks
are not $\langle tt\rangle$ or $\langle t^ct^c\rangle$.
In the original Weyl basis, the diagonal blocks of $R_W$ contain
$\langle tt^\dagger\rangle$ and
$\langle t^{c\dagger}t^c\rangle$; the off-diagonal blocks contain
$\langle tt^c\rangle$ and its conjugate, with the ordering signs
specified by $R^>$ or $R^<$.
The Fourier convention in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fermion-fourier} fixes the momentum of a reversed
line to $-\bm k$.

\subsection{Feynman rules}

The external Higgs fluctuation is treated as massless. Its
bulk-to-boundary contraction on SK branch $s=\pm1$ is
\begin{equation}
 G_s(k;\tau)=\frac{H^2}{2k^3}(1-\ii s k\tau)e^{\ii s k\tau}.
 \label{eq:higgs}
\end{equation}
All external momenta are incoming, $\sum_i\bk_i=0$, and a prime on a
correlator removes the overall
$(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\sum_i\bk_i)$. We work in exact de Sitter with
constant $m_t$ and zero chemical potential, and keep the leading
collapsed expansion.
This is a spectator-background approximation: over the times sampled by
the correlator we require $|\dot h_0|\ll H|h_0|$ and
$|m_{h,\mathrm{eff}}^2|\ll H^2$, with the renormalized Higgs effective
mass including potential and curvature contributions. These conditions
do not follow from a nonzero $h_0$ alone. The calculation gives the
connected $\delta h$ four-point function at order $g_t^4$; converting it
to a curvature trispectrum requires a specified Higgs-to-curvature
transfer mechanism. Since the Yukawa coupling is linear in $\delta h$,
there is no elementary $\delta h^2\bar t t$ vertex and hence no Yukawa
bubble or triangle at this order with four external Higgs legs.


The following rules refer to the conformally rescaled fermions in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:action}. SK contractions contain no extra factor of $\ii$.
The branch sign at a vertex will be denoted by $s=\pm1$ here to distinguish it
from the scale factor $a(\tau)$.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{External Higgs line.} Attach $G_s(k;\tau)$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:higgs} to a vertex on branch $s$. External Higgs fields are
labelled before summing permutations.

\item \textbf{Yukawa vertex.} From
$S_{\rm int}=-g_t\int\dd\tau\dd^3x\,a(\tau)\delta h\,
\Psi_W^\dagger\beta_W\Psi_W$, the vertex matrix is
\begin{equation}
 -\ii s\,g_t a(\tau)(\beta_W)_{AB}\,\delta_{cc'}
 =-\ii s\,g_t a(\tau)
 \begin{pmatrix}0&\one\\\one&0\end{pmatrix}_{AB}\delta_{cc'}.
 \label{eq:vertex-rule}
\end{equation}
Here $A$ labels the $\Psi_W^\dagger$ slot, $B$ the $\Psi_W$ slot, and
$c,c'$ are color indices. The two off-diagonal blocks are precisely the
$\delta h\,tt^c$ and $\delta h\,t^\dagger t^{c\dagger}$ vertices with the
index positions specified below. Each has coefficient $-\ii s g_t a(\tau)$;
there is no $1/2$ since the two Weyl fields are distinct. In the mode basis,
replace $\beta_W$ by $\beta=\operatorname{diag}(\one,-\one)$.
There is only one power of the scale factor at a vertex. The background mass
is already included in the propagator, so it is not inserted again.

\item \textbf{Fermion line.} A line with field end $(A,\tau,s)$ and dagger
end $(B,\tau',s')$ contributes
\begin{equation}
 [R_{W,ss'}(\bm k;\tau,\tau')]_{AB}\delta_{cc'}.
\end{equation}
where $R_W=U_BR U_B^\dagger$ and $R$ is given in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Rblocks}--\eqref{eq:sk}. The block dictionary is
\begin{equation}
 R_{W,ss'}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \langle t_\alpha\,t^\dagger_{\dot\beta}\rangle_{ss'}&
 \langle t_\alpha\,t^{c\beta}\rangle_{ss'}\\
 \langle t^{c\dagger\dot\alpha}\,t^\dagger_{\dot\beta}\rangle_{ss'}&
 \langle t^{c\dagger\dot\alpha}\,t^{c\beta}\rangle_{ss'}
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:weyl-line-rule}
\end{equation}
Thus the epsilon tensors needed to raise the $t^c$ indices are already included;
one must not add another epsilon tensor to the vertex matrix. This fixes the
normal and anomalous Weyl contractions in one convention. The charge arrow runs
from the dagger end to the field end, and the Fourier factor of this ordered
line is $e^{\ii\bm k\cdot(\bm x-\bm x')}$.
If its endpoints are exchanged, use the coordinate-space Grassmann identity
\begin{equation}
 \langle T_C\Psi^\dagger_B(\tau,\bm x)\Psi_A(\tau',\bm x')\rangle_{ss'}
 =-[R_{s's}(\tau',\bm x';\tau,\bm x)]_{AB}.
 \label{eq:reverse-line-rule}
\end{equation}
The coefficient of $e^{\ii\bm k\cdot(\bm x-\bm x')}$ on the right is
$-[R_{s's}(-\bm k;\tau',\tau)]_{AB}$. This distinguishes the Fourier
transform of the adjoint field from the adjoint of a Fourier coefficient,
whose momentum convention was used in Eq.~\eqref{eq:wightman}.
In particular the minus sign of $R^<$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:wightman} is part of
the line rule, not an optional extra sign. Charge-forbidden contractions such
as $\langle tt\rangle$ and $\langle t^ct^c\rangle$ vanish.

\item \textbf{Integrals and closed loop.} Integrate every vertex over
$\dd\tau\in(-\infty,0)$, impose spatial momentum conservation at each vertex,
and integrate an independent loop momentum with $\dd^3q/(2\pi)^3$.
Use the BD $\ii\epsilon$ prescription for the early-time endpoint, and sum all vertex branch labels. A closed fermion loop gives $-1$, a spin trace,
and a color trace $N_c=3$. No spin average or additional factor of two is
included. Each labelled directed box below has symmetry factor one.
\end{enumerate}
Multiplying these rules gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:each-box}; its overall sign is
$(-1)_{\rm loop}(-\ii)^4=-1$, with the product of the four branch signs kept
inside the SK sum.

The calculation now follows the same order as the gauge-boson box note:
identify the two soft lines, keep the other two lines as exact SK hard
subgraphs, perform the spin or polarization contraction, and reduce the
remaining loop integral to scalar $F_0$ convolutions. For fermions the
polarization tensors are replaced by the matrices $\beta R$ and a Pauli
trace. The symmetric exchange of the external Higgs legs removes the
leading equal-branch spin contraction, so the hard subgraphs must be
expanded one order further before the scalar convolution is taken.

\section{Box diagrams and spin contraction}
\subsection{Diagram counting and four-point integrals}
For four labelled external Higgs fields there are $(4-1)!=6$ directed fermion
cycles, or three undirected boxes with two orientations each. Each carries
$-N_c$, and its symmetry factor is one. Expanding the Dirac determinant gives the
same answer: the $1/4$ cancels cyclic rotations of the $4!$ labelled insertions.
There is no extra Majorana factor, and color is counted once.

For $\pi=(i,j,k,l)$ define
\begin{equation}
 p_{ij}=q+k_i,\qquad p_{jk}=q+k_i+k_j,\qquad
 p_{kl}=q-k_l,\qquad p_{li}=q,
 \label{eq:routing}
\end{equation}
where this equation is a vector equation. For the four lines of this
cycle, abbreviate
$R_{W,ij}=R_{W,a_i a_j}(p_{ij};\tau_i,\tau_j)$, and similarly for
the other three pairs. The Feynman rules of Sec.~1.3 were written in
the original $\Psi_W$ basis, so their spinor contraction starts as the
$4\times4$ trace
\begin{equation}
 \tr_4[\beta_W R_{W,ij}\,\beta_W R_{W,jk}\,
       \beta_W R_{W,kl}\,\beta_W R_{W,li}].
 \label{eq:weyl-box-trace}
\end{equation}
Insert $R_{W,uv}=U_B R_{uv}U_B^\dagger$ and
$\beta_W=U_B\beta U_B^\dagger$ into every factor. Adjacent
$U_B^\dagger U_B$ cancel, and cyclicity of the trace removes the
remaining pair at the two ends:
\begin{align}
 &\tr_4[\beta_W R_{W,ij}\,\beta_W R_{W,jk}\,
          \beta_W R_{W,kl}\,\beta_W R_{W,li}]\nonumber\\
 &\quad=\tr_4\!\left[U_B(\beta R_{ij}\,\beta R_{jk}\,
              \beta R_{kl}\,\beta R_{li})U_B^\dagger\right]
 =\tr_4[\beta R_{ij}\,\beta R_{jk}\,
         \beta R_{kl}\,\beta R_{li}].
 \label{eq:box-basis-invariance}
\end{align}
Both sides remain $4\times4$ traces. We use the rotated form on the
right because the mode functions give $R$ directly. Restoring the
branch labels, momenta, time integrals, and external Higgs lines,
the exact individual contribution is
\begin{align}
 \G_{ijkl}={}&-N_cg_t^4\sum_{a_i,a_j,a_k,a_l=\pm}a_i a_j a_k a_l
 \int_{-\infty}^0\prod_{v=i,j,k,l}\big[\dd\tau_v\,a(\tau_v)G_{a_v}(k_v;\tau_v)\big]
 \int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\\[-2mm]
 &\hspace{5mm}\times\tr_4\left[
 \beta R_{a_i a_j}(p_{ij};\tau_i,\tau_j)
 \beta R_{a_j a_k}(p_{jk};\tau_j,\tau_k)
 \beta R_{a_k a_l}(p_{kl};\tau_k,\tau_l)
 \beta R_{a_l a_i}(q;\tau_l,\tau_i)\right].
 \label{eq:each-box}
\end{align}
Here $(-\ii)^4=1$ from the four Yukawa vertices.

We can also unpack the same trace into the original two-Weyl
components. Let $M_{uv}=\beta_W R_{W,uv}$. Each $M_{uv}$ is a
$4\times4$ matrix made of four $2\times2$ blocks $M_{uv}^{rs}$,
where $r,s\in\{1,2\}$ select the upper or lower Weyl block. For two
such matrices, ordinary block multiplication and the definition of
the trace give the simpler example
\begin{equation}
 \tr_4(M_{ij}M_{jk})
 =\sum_{r,s=1}^2\tr_2(M_{ij}^{rs}M_{jk}^{sr}).
 \label{eq:two-block-trace}
\end{equation}
For the four factors of the box, the same rule gives
\begin{align}
 \tr_4(M_{ij}M_{jk}M_{kl}M_{li})
 &=\sum_{w_i,w_j,w_k,w_l\in\{1,2\}}\mathcal S^w_{ijkl},\nonumber\\*
 \mathcal S^w_{ijkl}
 &=\tr_2[M_{ij}^{w_iw_j}M_{jk}^{w_jw_k}
              M_{kl}^{w_kw_l}M_{li}^{w_lw_i}].
 \label{eq:weyl-components}
\end{align}
Each $w$ label selects an upper or lower block index at one vertex;
adjacent labels select the block of the connecting $M$ factor. They
are neither SK branch labels nor helicities. For example,
$w_iw_jw_kw_l=1112$
selects blocks $11,11,12,21$. There are $2^4=16$ choices. The
$\tr_2$ still sums the two spinor components inside each selected
block; summing all 16 results restores the original $\tr_4$.
The table lists blocks of $M=\beta_W R_W$, not blocks of $R_W$.
Because $\beta_W$ exchanges the two block rows, for example
$M_{ij}^{11}=(R_{W,ij})^{21}$. Each term uses exactly the time
integrals, SK signs, color factor, and line momenta of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:each-box}. The complete block list is

\subsection{Collapsed limit and Bose-symmetry cancellation}
The collapsed kinematics are best organized symmetrically:
\begin{align}
 \bk_1&=\bm k_L+\tfrac12\bk_s,&\bk_2&=-\bm k_L+\tfrac12\bk_s,\\
 \bk_3&=\bm k_R-\tfrac12\bk_s,&\bk_4&=-\bm k_R-\tfrac12\bk_s,\\
 \bm\ell&=\bq+\tfrac12\bk_s,&\bm K&=\bq+\bk_s,\qquad
 \bm n_L=\hat{\bm k}_L,\quad\bm n_R=\hat{\bm k}_R,\quad\bm n_s=\hat{\bk}_s.
 \label{eq:central}
\end{align}
Write $k_L=|\bm k_L|$, $k_R=|\bm k_R|$; at the accuracy of the final answer
$k_L=k_{12}/2$, $k_R=k_{34}/2$, where $k_{ij}=k_i+k_j$ is a sum of magnitudes.

For the $+$ clock branch in $1234$, the two projected hard matrices are
\begin{align}
 V_L&=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\ii\tau_1\bs\cdot\bq}{1+2\ii\mu}&\one\end{pmatrix}\beta R_{12}(\bm k_L+\bm\ell;\tau_1,\tau_2)\begin{pmatrix}\one\\\dfrac{\ii\tau_2\bs\cdot\bm K}{1+2\ii\mu}\end{pmatrix},\\
 V_R&=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\ii\tau_3\bs\cdot\bm K}{1+2\ii\mu}&\one\end{pmatrix}\beta R_{34}(\bm k_R+\bm\ell;\tau_3,\tau_4)\begin{pmatrix}\one\\\dfrac{\ii\tau_4\bs\cdot\bq}{1+2\ii\mu}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
After the Weyl-component sum the entire spin contraction is
\begin{equation}
 c^2(qK)^{2\ii\mu}\prod_v(-\tau_v)^{\ii\mu}
 \tr_2[V_L\sigma_K V_R\sigma_q].
 \label{eq:spin-cut}
\end{equation}
Thus, already before integration,
\begin{equation}
 \tr[(\bs\cdot\bm A)\sigma_K(\bs\cdot\bm B)\sigma_q]
 =2[(\bm A\cdot\hat K)(\bm B\cdot\hat q)
 +(\bm A\cdot\hat q)(\bm B\cdot\hat K)
 -(\bm A\cdot\bm B)(\hat K\cdot\hat q)].
 \label{eq:pauli}
\end{equation}
At zeroth order $V_L=-R_{21}(k_L)\propto\bs\cdot\bm n_L$ and
$V_R\propto\bs\cdot\bm n_R$. Their spin trace is odd under either
$\bm n_L\to-\bm n_L$ or $\bm n_R\to-\bm n_R$. The sum of the four cut cycles is precisely
the independent exchange of the two left and the two right Higgs fields. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\left.\G^{\rm box}_{s,\rm clock}\right|_{k_s^{3\pm4\ii\mu}}=0.}
 \label{eq:cancellation}
\end{equation}
The cancellation follows before any scalar integration: every zeroth-order
term contains one factor of each hard direction and is odd under either
Higgs-pair exchange. We must therefore keep the first momentum correction at
both ends of the cut.

\section{Scalar integrals for the hard lines and the soft bubble}
\subsection{Left and right hard subgraphs}
We now calculate the first order of each Bose-symmetrized hard block.
Write $x=-k_L\tau_1$, $y=-k_L\tau_2$ and $r=p/k_L$, where
$p=|\bm p|$ denotes the hard-line momentum magnitude in this section.
Also write $\sigma_p=\bs\cdot\hat{\bm p}$. We need only two
SK-summed scalar seeds, corresponding to the $21$ and $11$ blocks of $R$:
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{IJ}(\alpha,\beta;r)={}&\sum_{a,b=\pm}ab\int_0^\infty\dd x\dd y\,
 x^{\alpha-1}y^{\beta-1}(1+\ii ax)(1+\ii by)e^{-\ii ax-\ii by}
 [R_{IJ}(r;x,y)]^{\rm radial}_{ab},\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{65mm} IJ=21,11.
 \label{eq:hard-def}
\end{align}
``Radial'' means the coefficient of $\sigma_p$ in $R_{21}$ or of $\one$ in
$R_{11}$; their mode arguments are $rx,ry$. Hence these are scalar integrals,
with the spin structure already removed. Their finite evaluation is given
immediately below in Sec.~\ref{sec:seed-eval}.

The useful exact reversal identities are
\begin{equation}
 R_{21;ab}(\tau_1,\tau_2)=R_{21;ba}(\tau_2,\tau_1),\qquad
 R_{11;ab}(\tau_1,\tau_2)=-R_{22;ba}(\tau_2,\tau_1).
 \label{eq:reverse}
\end{equation}
For one left ordering, expanding the endpoint projectors gives
\begin{equation}
 V_L=-R_{21}+\frac{\ii\tau_1}{1+2\ii\mu}\bs\cdot\bq\,R_{11}
                  -\frac{\ii\tau_2}{1+2\ii\mu}R_{22}\bs\cdot\bm K+O(q^2,k_s^2).
 \label{eq:vertex-expand}
\end{equation}
All entries here are unapproximated hard SK contractions before expansion of their
momentum $\bm p=\bm k_L+\bm\ell$. The other ordering has
$\bm p=-\bm k_L+\bm\ell$. The derivative of the hard line and the descendants
of both soft endpoints must both be kept. Eq.~\eqref{eq:reverse} shows that the
first-order difference between the two external magnitudes gives zero in the
sum: the integrated $R_{21}$ block is symmetric in those energies. Also $\int\tau_2R_{22}=-\int\tau_1R_{11}$
at equal external magnitudes.

The intermediate momentum expansion makes the two terms in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result} explicit. Write
$h(r)=\mathcal H_{21}(\ii\mu,\ii\mu;r)$ and
$L=\bm n_L\cdot\bm\ell$. For the ordering $\varepsilon=\pm1$,
\begin{align}
 |\varepsilon k_L\bm n_L+\bm\ell|
 &=k_L+\varepsilon L+O(\ell^2/k_L),\\
 \bs\cdot\widehat{\varepsilon k_L\bm n_L+\bm\ell}
 &=\varepsilon\bs\cdot\bm n_L
 +\frac{\bs\cdot(\bm\ell-\bm n_LL)}{k_L}
 +O(\ell^2/k_L^2),\\
 h\!\left(\frac{|\varepsilon k_L\bm n_L+\bm\ell|}{k_L}\right)
 &=h(1)+\frac{\varepsilon L}{k_L}h'(1)
 +O(\ell^2/k_L^2).
 \label{eq:hard-taylor}
\end{align}
The $-R_{21}$ terms of the two orderings therefore add to
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{2}{k_L}\bs\cdot\big[
 h(1)\bm\ell+(h'(1)-h(1))\bm n_LL\big]
 +O(\ell^2/k_L^2).
 \label{eq:hard-derivative-step}
\end{equation}
The $R_{11}$ and $R_{22}$ descendants in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:vertex-expand} contribute
$-4\ii\mathcal H_{11}(1+\ii\mu,\ii\mu;1)
\bs\cdot\bm\ell/[k_L(1+2\ii\mu)]$.
The common factor $k_L^{-2\ii\mu}$ comes from
$(-\tau_1)^{\ii\mu}(-\tau_2)^{\ii\mu}$ after
$x=-k_L\tau_1$, $y=-k_L\tau_2$.
Finally, the external magnitudes are
$|\bm k_L\pm\bk_s/2|=k_L\pm\bm n_L\cdot\bk_s/2+O(k_s^2/k_L)$.
Their first-order shifts cancel under the two orderings by the
time-reversal identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:reverse}; equal external energies
are imposed only after this sum.

Strip $H^2/(4k_1^3k_2^3)$ from the left two external lines and vertices. Its
integrated projected matrix, summed over its two Higgs orderings, is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal V_L&=-2k_L^{-1-2\ii\mu}\bs\cdot
 \big[\mathcal P_1(\mu)\bm\ell+
 \mathcal P_2(\mu)\bm n_L(\bm n_L\cdot\bm\ell)\big],\\
 \mathcal P_1(\mu)&=\mathcal H_{21}(\ii\mu,\ii\mu;1)
 +\frac{2\ii}{1+2\ii\mu}\mathcal H_{11}(1+\ii\mu,\ii\mu;1),\\
 \mathcal P_2(\mu)&=\left.(\partial_r-1)\mathcal H_{21}(\ii\mu,\ii\mu;r)\right|_{r=1}.
 \label{eq:hard-result}
\end{align}
The two coefficients $\mathcal P_1,\mathcal P_2$ multiply the independent
spin structures $\bs\cdot\bm\ell$ and
$(\bs\cdot\bm n_L)(\bm n_L\cdot\bm\ell)$.
The $\mathcal H_{11}$ term comes from the soft endpoint descendants;
the $r$ derivative comes from the hard propagator's momentum dependence.
The right result is the same with $(k_L,\bm n_L)\to(k_R,\bm n_R)$ and external
prefactor $H^2/(4k_3^3k_4^3)$. In particular the four-cycle sum is the product
of these two already symmetrized blocks; no further factor of two or four is
allowed. Formula~\eqref{eq:hard-result} retains all terms at this order.
The absence of the constant term in each block makes the signal a spatial
$P$-wave fermion-pair effect. It adds two powers of $k_s$ to the bubble scaling.

\subsection{Evaluation of the scalar hard seeds}
\label{sec:seed-eval}
\paragraph{Relation to the published Hankel scalar seed.}
The hard time integrals can also be reduced to the scalar seed of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}, provided its mass index is analytically continued.
This gives a direct route to the literature's single- and double-folded
formulae. Set
\begin{equation}
 \lambda=\mu-\frac{\ii}{2},\qquad \rho=\ii\lambda=\frac12+\ii\mu,
 \qquad x=-k_L\tau_1,\quad y=-k_L\tau_2,\quad r=p/k_L.
\end{equation}
Define a dimensionless scalar SK kernel $S_{ab}^{(\lambda)}$ from the two
Wightman functions
\begin{align}
 S^{>}(r;x,y)&=\frac\pi4 e^{-\pi\lambda}(xy)^{3/2}
 H^{(1)}_{\rho}(rx)H^{(2)}_{-\rho}(ry),\\
 S^{<}(r;x,y)&=\frac\pi4 e^{-\pi\lambda}(xy)^{3/2}
 H^{(2)}_{-\rho}(rx)H^{(1)}_{\rho}(ry),
 \label{eq:scalar-seed-kernel}
\end{align}
with the same SK theta-function ordering as Eq.~\eqref{eq:sk}.
Using $H_{-\rho}^{(2)}=e^{-\ii\pi\rho}H_\rho^{(2)}$ and
$N_FN_G=\pi/4$ for the mode prefactors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:modes}, one finds
the \emph{exact} radial identities, for every branch pair,
\begin{equation}
 [R_{21}(r;x,y)]^{\rm radial}_{ab}
 =\frac{r}{xy}S_{ab}^{(\lambda)}(r;x,y),\qquad
 [R_{11}(r;x,y)]^{\rm radial}_{ab}
 =\frac1r\left(-\ii\partial_x+\frac\mu x\right)
 [R_{21}(r;x,y)]^{\rm radial}_{ab}.
 \label{eq:fermion-scalar-seed-map}
\end{equation}
The $1/r$ in the second identity follows from differentiating a mode
with argument $rx$. Differentiating the SK theta functions introduces
no delta-function term: the two radial $R_{21}$ Wightman functions agree
at $x=y$. Although $\lambda$ is complex, Eq.~\eqref{eq:scalar-seed-kernel}
is an analytic scalar kernel; no physical scalar with complex mass is
being introduced.

To see precisely which published seeds are needed, let
\begin{equation}
 J^{p,q}_{ab}(r)=\int_0^\infty\dd x\dd y\,
 x^p y^q e^{-\ii ax-\ii by}S_{ab}^{(\lambda)}(r;x,y).
\end{equation}
In the units $H=1$ of Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}, its branch-resolved Hankel
seed $\mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}$ obeys the exact normalization map
\begin{equation}
 J^{p,q}_{ab}(r)=-ab\,r^{-5-p-q}
 \mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}(u,u),\qquad
 u=\frac{2r}{1+r}.
 \label{eq:seed-normalization}
\end{equation}
The reference includes $-ab$ in the seed definition; neither that sign
nor the power of $r$ may be omitted. For
$p=\alpha-2$, $q=\beta-2$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-def} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{21}(\alpha,\beta;r)
 =r\sum_{a,b=\pm}ab\big[
 J^{p,q}_{ab}+\ii aJ^{p+1,q}_{ab}
 +\ii bJ^{p,q+1}_{ab}-abJ^{p+1,q+1}_{ab}\big].
 \label{eq:published-seed-map}
\end{equation}
The four terms correspond to the integer time-power shifts generated by
$(1+\ii ax)(1+\ii by)$. The SK indices stay explicit until their
$a,b$ weights are combined.

The remaining hard coefficient can also be expressed without a new
time integral. Put $\mathscr S_{ab}^{p,q}=\mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}(1,1)$,
$p_0=-2+\ii\mu+\delta$, and use one common endpoint regulator
$\delta$ in all seed powers. Equation~\eqref{eq:published-seed-map} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{21}(\ii\mu+\delta,\ii\mu+\delta;1)
 =-\sum_{a,b=\pm}\sum_{j,l=0}^1
 (\ii a)^j(\ii b)^l\mathscr S_{ab}^{p_0+j,p_0+l}.
 \label{eq:H21-literature}
\end{equation}
Integrating the second relation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fermion-scalar-seed-map} by parts yields
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{11}(1+\ii\mu+\delta,\ii\mu+\delta;1)
 ={}&\ii\delta\,
 \mathcal H_{21}(\ii\mu+\delta,\ii\mu+\delta;1)\nonumber\\
 &-\ii\sum_{a,b=\pm}\left[
 \mathscr S_{ab}^{\ii\mu+\delta,p_0}
 +\ii b\,\mathscr S_{ab}^{\ii\mu+\delta,p_0+1}\right].
 \label{eq:H11-literature}
\end{align}
The $\ii\delta\mathcal H_{21}$ term is retained at finite regulator;
the complete $\mathcal H_{21}$ will be finite, so this term vanishes
in the final limit. Individual SK terms do have regulator poles.
The integration-by-parts boundary term at
$x=0$ cancels only in the complete SK sum, since for $x<y$ the two
branches with the same $b$ have the same Wightman function.

The hard-momentum derivative in $\mathcal P_2$ follows from the
published scalar bootstrap equation without differentiating a time
integral. At $u_1=u_2=1$, the coefficient of its second derivative
vanishes. Define
\begin{align}
 T_{ab}^{p,q}&=\delta_{ab}\,
 e^{-\ii a\pi(p+q)/2}2^{-5-p-q}\Gamma(5+p+q),\\
 \mathscr D_{ab}^{p,q}&=\frac12\sum_{w=p,q}
 \frac{\lambda^2+(w+5/2)^2}{w+3}\,\mathscr S_{ab}^{p,q}
 -\frac12\left(\frac1{p+3}+\frac1{q+3}\right)T_{ab}^{p,q}.
 \label{eq:seed-bootstrap-derivative}
\end{align}
Since $\dd u/\dd r|_{r=1}=1/2$, $\mathscr D_{ab}^{p,q}$ is precisely
$\dd\mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}(u(r),u(r))/\dd r|_{r=1}$.
Combining this with the explicit $r^{-5-p-q}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed-normalization} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_2=-\FP_{\delta\to0}
 \sum_{a,b=\pm}\sum_{j,l=0}^1(\ii a)^j(\ii b)^l
 \left[\mathscr D_{ab}^{p_0+j,p_0+l}
 -(5+2p_0+j+l)\mathscr S_{ab}^{p_0+j,p_0+l}\right].
 \label{eq:P2-literature}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:H21-literature}--\eqref{eq:P2-literature}, with
$\mathcal P_1$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}, complete both hard
subgraphs in terms of the scalar seed values of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}; the right subgraph uses the same two functions.
\paragraph{Regularization and a finite evaluation.}
There is an essential normalization in Eq.~(92) of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}: its hypergeometric function is regularized,
\begin{equation}
 {}_3\widetilde F_2(a,b,c;d,e;1)
 =\frac{{}_3F_2(a,b,c;d,e;1)}{\Gamma(d)\Gamma(e)}.
 \label{eq:regularized-hyper}
\end{equation}
The earlier numerical implementation omitted these two Gamma factors.
Consequently its apparent regulator-path instability was not evidence
for an obstruction to the scalar-seed map. A real-index conjugation
check alone cannot detect this error. As an independent normalization
check, the conformal scalar $\lambda=-\ii/2$ has
$S^>(1;x,y)=xy\,e^{\ii(x-y)}/2$. Direct elementary time integration gives
$\mathscr S_{++}^{0,0}=\mathscr S_{--}^{0,0}=-3/16$ and
$\mathscr S_{+-}^{0,0}=\mathscr S_{-+}^{0,0}=1/32$; the corrected
implementation reproduces these values and their unequal-power generalization.

The scalar kernel is invariant under $\lambda\to-\lambda$, by the
Hankel order-reflection identities. In each equal-SK seed choose the
sign of $\lambda$ so that $a\ii\lambda=1/2+\ii\mu$. Its ordinary
${}_3F_2(1)$ then has convergence balance $1+\ii\mu$, whose real
part is positive. This is an exact change of representation and needs
no additional mass-index regulator.

For this subsection only set $z=\ii\mu$, and define the single symmetric
family
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Q_{jl}=\frac{2^{-1-2z-j-l}\Gamma(1+2z+j+l)}
 {(1+2z+j)(1+2z+l)}
 {}_3F_2\!\left(\begin{matrix}1+2z+j+l,\ 1+z,\ 1\\
 2+2z+j,\ 2+2z+l\end{matrix};1\right).
 \label{eq:finite-Q}
\end{equation}
Only $\mathcal Q_{00},\mathcal Q_{01},\mathcal Q_{11},
\mathcal Q_{02},\mathcal Q_{12}$ are needed. Also abbreviate the
finite endpoint contribution by
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E=\pi^2 2^{-1-2z}
 \frac{\Gamma(1+2z)^2}{\Gamma(1+z)^2}.
 \label{eq:endpoint-finite}
\end{equation}
The fully summed hard seeds and momentum derivative are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{21}(z,z;1)
 &=2\cosh(\pi\mu)(\mathcal Q_{00}+2\mathcal Q_{01}+\mathcal Q_{11})
 -\mathcal E,\nonumber\\
 \mathcal H_{11}(1+z,z;1)
 &=-2\ii\cosh(\pi\mu)(\mathcal Q_{02}+\mathcal Q_{12}),
 \label{eq:finite-hard-seeds}\\
 \mathcal P_2
 &=2\cosh(\pi\mu)\sum_{j,l=0}^1
 \left[\frac{j(1+2z+j)}{2(1+z+j)}
 +\frac{l(1+2z+l)}{2(1+z+l)}-(1+2z+j+l)\right]\mathcal Q_{jl}
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad +(1+2z)\mathcal E
 +2^{-1-2z}\cosh(\pi\mu)\Gamma(1+2z)
 \frac{4+5z+2z^2}{1+z}.
 \label{eq:finite-P2}
\end{align}
Together with $\mathcal P_1$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}, these are
finite, convergent special-function expressions for the complete hard
coefficients. No raw mode-power-series truncation or numerical
regulator extrapolation is required for the oscillatory signal.

Here is the endpoint cancellation leading to these expressions.
For $p_j=-2+z+\delta+j$, write the Gamma product in the opposite-SK
seed as
\begin{equation}
 C_{jl}(\delta)=
 \frac{\Gamma(\delta+j)\Gamma(1+2z+\delta+j)
       \Gamma(\delta+l)\Gamma(1+2z+\delta+l)}
 {\Gamma(1+z+\delta+j)\Gamma(1+z+\delta+l)}.
\end{equation}
After the external-mode factors $(\ii a)^j(\ii b)^l$ and the SK
branches are summed, its contribution to $\mathcal H_{21}$ is
$2\,2^{-1-2z-2\delta-j-l}[\cos(\pi\delta)-1]C_{jl}(\delta)$.
Only $j=l=0$ has a double pole; therefore
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\delta\to0}2[\cos(\pi\delta)-1]C_{00}(\delta)
 =-\pi^2\frac{\Gamma(1+2z)^2}{\Gamma(1+z)^2}.
\end{equation}
This is precisely $-\mathcal E$ after restoring its power of two.
Discarding the poles branch by branch would lose this finite term.
The remaining regularized-hypergeometric pieces give
$2\cosh(\pi\mu)\mathcal Q_{jl}$.
The integration-by-parts insertion in Eq.~\eqref{eq:H11-literature}
replaces $j=0,1$ by $j=2$ and contributes $-\ii$ times the same
pieces; it has no double-pole remainder.
Finally, substituting $p_j$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed-bootstrap-derivative} gives the bracket in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-P2}. The last term in that equation is the
summed bootstrap source, and $(1+2z)\mathcal E$ is the derivative's
endpoint remainder. These steps preserve all factors of $r$.

The late-time cancellation can also be seen before integration.
With $E_a(x)=(1+\ii ax)e^{-\ii ax}$, the SK sum for $x<y$ is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{a,b}ab E_a(x)E_b(y)R_{ab}(x,y)
 =[E_+(x)-E_-(x)]
 [E_+(y)R^>(x,y)-E_-(y)R^<(x,y)].
 \label{eq:sk-endpoint-cancel}
\end{equation}
Since $E_+(x)-E_-(x)=-2\ii x^3/3+O(x^5)$, the common late-time
endpoint is integrable in the hard blocks used here. The early-time
BD prescription is still necessary; this identity does not justify
integrating undamped oscillations numerically on a truncated interval.

\subsection{Soft bubble integral}
The spin trace of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pauli} leaves a polynomial numerator divided
by $qK$. To display it, write the two vectors appearing in the hard blocks as
\begin{equation}
 \bm v_L=\mathcal P_1\bm\ell+\mathcal P_2\bm n_L(\bm n_L\cdot\bm\ell),\qquad
 \bm v_R=\mathcal P_1\bm\ell+\mathcal P_2\bm n_R(\bm n_R\cdot\bm\ell).
\end{equation}
Since $\bm K=\bm\ell+\bk_s/2$ and $\bq=\bm\ell-\bk_s/2$, the scalar loop
integral after the spin contraction is
\begin{align}
 &2\int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3}(qK)^{2\ii\mu-1}
 \big[2(\bm v_L\cdot\bm\ell)(\bm v_R\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\tfrac12(\bm v_L\cdot\bk_s)(\bm v_R\cdot\bk_s)\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{55mm}-(\bm v_L\cdot\bm v_R)(\ell^2-k_s^2/4)\big].
 \label{eq:spin-bubble}
\end{align}
Its mass dimension is $5+4\ii\mu$. We now evaluate it by scalar convolutions,
following the same procedure as for the vector box. The only required scalar
formula is
\begin{align}
 F_0(A,B)&=\frac{\Gamma(A+B-\tfrac32)\Gamma(\tfrac32-A)\Gamma(\tfrac32-B)}
 {\Gamma(A)\Gamma(B)\Gamma(3-A-B)},\\
 \int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3}(q^2)^{-A}(K^2)^{-B}
 &=\frac{k_s^{3-2A-2B}}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}F_0(A,B).
 \label{eq:scalar-bubble}
\end{align}
The right-hand side is the analytic continuation of the nonanalytic part;
UV-dependent polynomial contact terms are excluded.

For the reduction only, measure $q,K,\ell$ in units of $k_s$, put
$u=q^2$, $v=K^2$, and set $\nu=1/2-\ii\mu$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \ell^2=(u+v)/2-1/4,\qquad
 \ell_\parallel\equiv\bm\ell\cdot\bm n_s=(v-u)/2,\qquad
 \ell_\perp^2=\ell^2-\ell_\parallel^2.
\end{equation}
Azimuthal averaging replaces two transverse components by
$\ell_\perp^2\delta^\perp_{ij}/2$ and four by
$\ell_\perp^4(\delta^\perp_{ij}\delta^\perp_{kl}
+\delta^\perp_{ik}\delta^\perp_{jl}+\delta^\perp_{il}\delta^\perp_{jk})/8$,
where $\delta^\perp_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-n_{s i}n_{s j}$.
The numerator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spin-bubble} consequently becomes a polynomial
in $u,v$ and the three external angles. Each monomial is integrated by
\begin{align}
 u^mv^n&\quad\longmapsto\quad
 \frac{F_0(\nu-m,\nu-n)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}},\\
 \frac{F_0(\nu-m,\nu-n)}{F_0(\nu,\nu)}&=
 \frac{(3/2-\nu)_m(3/2-\nu)_n(1-\nu)_m(1-\nu)_n}
 {(5/2-2\nu)_{m+n}(3-2\nu)_{m+n}}.
 \label{eq:bubble-reduction}
\end{align}
Only nonnegative integers $m,n$ with $m+n\leq4$ occur. Thus the bubble is a
finite Gamma sum, and all its terms reduce to the same $F_0(\nu,\nu)$ times
rational functions. For example the coefficient of $\mathcal P_1^2$ in the
square bracket of Eq.~\eqref{eq:spin-bubble} is
$\ell^4+\ell^2/4-\ell_\parallel^2/2$, whose integral is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{F_0(\nu,\nu)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}
 \frac{3(\nu-1)(2\nu-3)}{8(\nu-2)(4\nu-7)(4\nu-5)}.
\end{equation}
The $\mathcal P_1\mathcal P_2$ and $\mathcal P_2^2$ terms are evaluated by
the same two steps. Their fully reduced coefficients are displayed directly
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:compact-coefficients}, without introducing separate symbols
for each tensor moment.
The azimuthal average introduces only the five angle monomials
$1$, $c_1^2+c_3^2$, $c_1^2c_3^2$,
$\sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3$, and
$\sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3)$, where
$c_i=\cos\theta_i$. Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:bubble-reduction} to their
coefficients reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:compact-coefficients}.

\subsection{Oscillatory signal and angular coefficients}
Choose the angle convention
\begin{align}
 \bm n_L&=(\sin\theta_1,0,\cos\theta_1),&
 \bm n_R&=(\sin\theta_3\cos\phi_3,\sin\theta_3\sin\phi_3,\cos\theta_3),&
 \bm n_s&=(0,0,1).
\end{align}
The leading nonzero oscillatory term, summed over the four $s$-cut boxes, is
\begin{align}
 \left.\langle\delta h_{\bk_1}\delta h_{\bk_2}\delta h_{\bk_3}\delta h_{\bk_4}\rangle'
 \right|_{s,\rm clock}
 ={}&-\frac{N_cg_t^4H^4 k_s^5}{16\,k_1^3k_2^3k_3^3k_4^3 k_Lk_R}\nonumber\\
 &\times\left[\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)^{2\ii\mu}
 \cC(\mu;\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)+\mathrm{c.c.}\right].
 \label{eq:final}
\end{align}
The coefficient is given directly in the angular basis of the vector-box note:
\begin{align}
 \cC={}&A_1+A_2(\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_3)
 +A_3\cos^2\theta_1\cos^2\theta_3\nonumber\\
 &+B_1\sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3
 +D_1\sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3).
 \label{eq:angular-final}
\end{align}
All five coefficients share one soft-bubble normalization, including the two
soft-mode coefficients $c^2$, the factor $4$ from the two Bose-summed hard blocks, the factor $2$ from the Pauli trace, and the phase from the chosen momentum ratio:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B(\mu)=
 \frac{2^{1+4\ii\mu}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii\mu)^4 F_0(\nu,\nu)}
 {32\pi^2(4\pi)^{3/2}(\nu-3)(\nu-2)(4\nu-7)(4\nu-5)},\qquad
 \nu=\tfrac12-\ii\mu.
 \label{eq:bubble-normalization}
\end{equation}
In particular, the natural hard scaling is
$(k_s^2/(k_Lk_R))^{2\ii\mu}$. Since
$4k_{12}k_{34}=16k_Lk_R$ at this order, using the vector note's
ratio transfers $16^{2\ii\mu}$ into the coefficient. Thus
$8c^2\,16^{2\ii\mu}
=2^{1+4\ii\mu}\Gamma(1/2-\ii\mu)^4/\pi^2$, which fixes both
the phase and the overall factor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bubble-normalization}.
Combining the bubble with the two hard coefficients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}
gives the final mass dependence:
\begin{align}
 A_1&=\mathcal B(\nu-1)(2\nu-3)
 \big[4(\nu-3)(3\mathcal P_1^2+2\mathcal P_1\mathcal P_2)
 +(2\nu-5)\mathcal P_2^2\big],\\
 A_2&=-\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\nu-1)(2\nu-3)^2,\qquad A_3=-3A_2,\\
 B_1&=-2\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\nu-1)(\nu-2)(2\nu-3),\\
 D_1&=\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\nu-2)(2\nu-5)(2\nu-3).
 \label{eq:compact-coefficients}
\end{align}
Thus the answer requires only two hard functions $\mathcal P_1,\mathcal P_2$
and one explicit bubble factor $\mathcal B$. The coefficients are complex and
include the clock phase. The full answer is even under either Higgs-pair exchange
and under parity. Its engineering dimension is $-5$, as required for the primed
four-point function. The powers of $H$ follow from four rescaled Weyl Yukawa
vertices and four external Higgs contractions; no extra factor of $H$ or a
second color/orientation multiplicity is implicit. Higher soft powers and
analytic contact terms are not displayed.

\subsection{Nonoscillatory contribution}
Taking opposite soft branches gives a nonoscillatory $k_s^3$ term. The hard
blocks are $R_{11}$ and $-R_{22}$; Eq.~\eqref{eq:reverse} makes their integrated
values equal to the real seed $\mathcal H_{11}(-\ii\mu,+\ii\mu;1)$.
The leading soft matrices are now opposite triangular blocks, so they
project the diagonal hard blocks. Each hard end therefore has a Bose-even
zeroth-order term. There are two soft assignments and two Higgs orderings
at each end, giving $2\times2\times2$ contributions; the spin trace
adds $2\hat q\cdot\hat K$. The soft coefficients give
$|c|^2=1/(4\cosh^2\pi\mu)$. These factors fix the normalization of
the mixed term independently of the oscillatory branch.
The scalar bubble in this case is
\begin{equation}
 \int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3}(q^2)^{-\ii\mu}(K^2)^{\ii\mu}
 \hat q\cdot\hat K
 =\frac{k_s^3}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\,
 \frac{2\sqrt\pi}{3}\mu(1+\mu^2)\coth(\pi\mu).
\end{equation}
After both soft branches and all four $s$-cut boxes are summed, the result is
\begin{equation}
 \left.\langle\delta h^4\rangle'\right|_{s,\mathrm{mixed}}
 =\frac{N_cg_t^4H^4 k_s^3}{16\prod_i k_i^3}
 \frac{2\mu(1+\mu^2)}{3\pi\sinh(2\pi\mu)}
 \big[\mathcal H_{11}(-\ii\mu,+\ii\mu;1)\big]^2.
 \label{eq:mixed}
\end{equation}
It is independent of angles at this order. Equation~\eqref{eq:mixed} is the
leading nonoscillatory nonanalytic term, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:final} is the
leading mass-dependent clock signal. At $\mu=0$ the separated branches coalesce;
the limit must be taken after combining the relevant terms.
Here $k_s^3=(\bk_s^2)^{3/2}$ is nonanalytic as a function of the
soft momentum components, even though it has no mass-dependent oscillation.
It cannot be discarded as a polynomial contact term.

For evaluation of the remaining mixed seed, the same radial identity
and integration by parts give the general formula
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{11}(\alpha,\beta;1)
 ={}&[\ii(\alpha-1)+\mu]\mathcal H_{21}(\alpha-1,\beta;1)
 \nonumber\\
 &-\ii\sum_{a,b=\pm}\left[
 \mathscr S_{ab}^{\alpha-1,\beta-2}
 +\ii b\,\mathscr S_{ab}^{\alpha-1,\beta-1}\right].
 \label{eq:mixed-seed-eval}
\end{align}
Set $\alpha=-\ii\mu+\delta$, $\beta=\ii\mu+\delta$, perform the
complete sum, and then take $\delta\to0$ using the corrected
regularized seeds. The conjugation relation
$\mathcal H_{11}(\alpha,\beta;1)^*
=\mathcal H_{11}(\beta^*,\alpha^*;1)$ proves the mixed seed is real.
The code evaluates this limit by fourth-order extrapolation, with
step-size and precision checks; the clock coefficients above instead
use the analytic limit.
For example,
$\mathcal H_{11}(-0.73\ii,+0.73\ii;1)=-1.388500280198814$;
halving the extrapolation step changes this value by less than
$2\times10^{-15}$.

\paragraph{Checks and scope.}
The accompanying scripts check the mode equations, normalization, signed SK
contractions, soft descendants, spin traces, scalar Gamma reductions, and hard
momentum expansion. The hard-seed checks include independent conformal-scalar
time integrals, the exact finite SK sum, regulator convergence, and precision
stability. The former hard-seed normalization error is corrected by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:regularized-hyper}; the scaling, diagram multiplicities and
five angular coefficients retain their displayed form.
These checks concern the leading collapsed nonanalytic terms of the
constant-mass, zero-chemical-potential Yukawa box in de Sitter with a light
external Higgs. They are not a numerical integration of the full unexpanded
box, a computation of its renormalized analytic contact terms, or a
Higgs-to-curvature conversion. The gauge-boson note supplies the
factorization workflow and angular basis. Its zeroth-order hard-line
replacement is insufficient for this fermion clock term: the two first
descendants and the hard momentum derivative are essential. The loop
factorization follows Ref.~\cite{qin}.
