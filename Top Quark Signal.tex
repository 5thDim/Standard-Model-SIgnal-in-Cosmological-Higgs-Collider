\section{Setup and conventions}
We compute the connected Higgs four-point function generated by the top
Yukawa box in the cosmological Higgs-collider setup~\cite{lu}, in exact
de Sitter space with constant $m_t$ and zero chemical potential. We use the free two-component construction of Aoki et al.~\cite{aoki}, Eqs.~(2.5)--(2.29), but derive the four-vertex contractions
and their collapsed expansion separately. Their bubble correlator, and
in particular its Yukawa bispectrum cancellation, is not a box amplitude.
The organization follows the gauge-boson note: setup, lines and vertices,
box contraction, soft expansion, scalar integrals, and signal.

\subsection{Geometry, indices and the Majorana decomposition}
Our metric is $\eta_{ab}=(+,-,-,-)$ and
\begin{equation}
 ds^2=a^2(d\tau^2-d\bm x^2),\qquad a=-\frac1{H\tau},\qquad
 \sigma^a=(\one,\bs),\quad\bar\sigma^a=(\one,-\bs),\quad\tau<0.
\end{equation}
For undotted and dotted indices alike take
\begin{equation}
 E=(\epsilon_{\alpha\beta})=\ii\sigma^2
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 (\epsilon^{\alpha\beta})=-E,\quad
 \psi^\alpha=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\psi_\beta,\quad
 \psi_\alpha=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\psi^\beta.
 \label{eq:epsilon}
\end{equation}
In particular $\psi\chi=\psi^\alpha\chi_\alpha
=\psi^T E\chi=\chi\psi$ for Grassmann fields, and, for the column
$d=(\psi^\dagger_{\dot1},\psi^\dagger_{\dot2})^T$,
$(\psi\psi)^\dagger=-d^TEd$. A dagger reverses operator order.
We Fourier transform \emph{every field}, including $d$, with
$e^{\ii\bk\cdot\bm x}$ and measure $d^3k/(2\pi)^3$;
thus two-field contractions carry $\delta^3(\bk+\bk')$.
This differs from taking the adjoint of a Fourier coefficient, which
reverses its momentum. A prime on a correlator removes the overall
$(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\sum\bk_i)$.

Let $t_{\rm cov}$ be the left-handed top, and $t^c_{\rm cov}$ the
left-handed charge conjugate of the right-handed top:
$t^c_{\rm cov}=[(t_R)^C]_L$, with two-component form
$\ii\sigma^2t_R^*$ up to the Yukawa phase convention. It carries the
conjugate gauge representation; it is not a second right-handed field.
For $\Phi=(0,(h_0+\delta h)/\sqrt2)^T$, define
\begin{equation}
 g_t=y_t/\sqrt2,\qquad m_t=g_th_0,\qquad \mu=m_t/H,\qquad N_c=3.
\end{equation}
The spin connection disappears on setting
$t=a^{3/2}t_{\rm cov}$ and $t^c=a^{3/2}t^c_{\rm cov}$.
The fermion density, with the coordinate measure $d\tau d^3x$, becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\ii t^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu t
 +\ii t^{c\dagger}\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu t^c
 -a(m_t+g_t\delta h)(tt^c+\mathrm{h.c.}).
 \label{eq:action-dirac}
\end{equation}
The remaining $a$ is $a^4(a^{-3/2})^2$, not $a^3$ or $a^4$.
Use Aoki's change of variables
\begin{equation}
 t=\frac{\psi_1+\ii\psi_2}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 t^c=\frac{\psi_1-\ii\psi_2}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:aoki-change}
\end{equation}
The cross terms cancel because $\psi_1\psi_2=\psi_2\psi_1$;
the kinetic cross terms cancel between the two original fields.
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathcal L=\sum_{I=1}^2\left[
 \ii\psi_I^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi_I
 -\frac a2(m_t+g_t\delta h)(\psi_I\psi_I+\mathrm{h.c.})\right].}
 \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
The two copies are identical and independent. This decomposition applies
color by color after gauge interactions are omitted. It is not a
splitting into two separately gauge-covariant colored Majorana particles:
gauge transformations mix the two real components of the original
charged Dirac representation. A connected loop belongs to one copy
throughout, so we calculate one copy and restore $2N_c$ once.

\subsection{External Higgs fields}
The existing light spectator-Higgs prescription is retained:
\begin{equation}
 G_s(k;\tau)=\frac{H^2}{2k^3}(1-\ii s k\tau)e^{\ii s k\tau},\qquad s=\pm1.
 \label{eq:higgs}
\end{equation}
We require $|\dot h_0|\ll H|h_0|$ and
$|m_{h,\mathrm{eff}}^2|\ll H^2$ over the relevant times. A nonzero
constant $h_0$ does not itself guarantee a light fluctuation; its
renormalized potential and curvature mass are part of this assumption.
We compute $\langle\delta h^4\rangle'_c$ at order $g_t^4$, without
specifying a Higgs-to-curvature conversion. A linear Yukawa interaction
has no elementary $\delta h^2\psi\psi$ vertex.

\section{Two-component modes, propagators and vertices}
\subsection{Helicity modes}
For one copy, suppress $I$ and color. Use Aoki's normalized spinors
\begin{equation}
 h_+(\hat k)=\binom{\cos(\theta/2)}{e^{\ii\phi}\sin(\theta/2)},\qquad
 h_-(\hat k)=\binom{-e^{-\ii\phi}\sin(\theta/2)}{\cos(\theta/2)},\qquad
 P_\lambda=h_\lambda h_\lambda^\dagger
 =\frac{\one+\lambda\bs\cdot\hat k}{2}.
\end{equation}
The operator expansion, with $\{b_\lambda(\bk),b_{\lambda'}^\dagger(\bk')\}
=(2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta^3(\bk-\bk')$, is
\begin{equation}
 \psi_\alpha(\tau,\bm x)=\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}
 \sum_{\lambda=\pm}\left[
 h_{\lambda\alpha}u_\lambda b_\lambda(\bk)e^{\ii\bk\cdot\bm x}
 +(Eh_\lambda^*)_\alpha v_\lambda^*b_\lambda^\dagger(\bk)
 e^{-\ii\bk\cdot\bm x}\right].
 \label{eq:mode-expansion}
\end{equation}
There is one set of $b,b^\dagger$ per Majorana copy.
Both $u_\lambda$ and $v_\lambda$ below are positive-frequency
\emph{radial} functions. In particular $v_\lambda$ is not the
negative-frequency four-component mode called $v$ in the old note.
The field equation yields
\begin{align}
 \ii\partial_\tau u_\lambda+\lambda ku_\lambda&=am_tv_\lambda,&
 \ii\partial_\tau v_\lambda-\lambda kv_\lambda&=am_tu_\lambda,\label{eq:weyl-eom}\\
 u_\lambda''-aHu_\lambda'+(k^2+a^2m_t^2+\ii\lambda aHk)u_\lambda&=0,&
 v_\lambda''-aHv_\lambda'+(k^2+a^2m_t^2-\ii\lambda aHk)v_\lambda&=0.
\end{align}
With $x=-k\tau$, the normalized BD solutions are
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{u_+=v_- =\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{2x}}W_{-1/2,\ii\mu}(-2\ii x),\qquad
 u_-=v_+ =\frac{\ii}{\sqrt{2x}}W_{1/2,\ii\mu}(-2\ii x).}
 \label{eq:aoki-modes}
\end{equation}
Take the boundary value from the BD continuation. At large $x$ the
nonvanishing massless-helicity amplitudes tend to
$e^{\ii\pi/4}e^{\ii x}$, and the other amplitudes are $O(\mu/x)$.
The first-order equations conserve $|u_\lambda|^2+|v_\lambda|^2$,
which is one by this boundary condition. Together with
$EP_\lambda^T(-\hat k)E^{-1}=P_\lambda(\hat k)$ this gives the canonical
anticommutators, including $\{\psi,\psi\}=0$.

It is useful to introduce only two auxiliary radial functions
\begin{equation}
 F=\frac{\sqrt{\pi x}}2e^{\pi\mu/2}H^{(1)}_{1/2-\ii\mu}(x),\qquad
 G=\frac{\sqrt{\pi x}}2e^{-\pi\mu/2}H^{(1)}_{1/2+\ii\mu}(x).
 \label{eq:modes}
\end{equation}
Aoki's Whittaker--Hankel identities (2.19) give the exact dictionary
\begin{equation}
 u_\lambda=e^{3\ii\pi/4}\frac{F-\lambda G}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 v_\lambda=e^{3\ii\pi/4}\frac{F+\lambda G}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 -\ii F'=\frac\mu x F+G,\quad-\ii G'=F-\frac\mu xG.
 \label{eq:mode-dictionary}
\end{equation}
The primes in the last two relations mean $d/dx$.
This is a change of radial variables, not the introduction of an
additional fermion species. The fixed common phase cancels from all
contractions. The $\mu=0$ modes are defined by continuity.

\subsection{All lower-index contractions and SK components}
Let $\zeta_+=\psi$ and $\zeta_-=d$ denote \emph{vertex types}, not
SK branches, and write $C^{rs,>}_{\alpha\beta}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
=\langle\zeta_{r\alpha}(\tau_1,\bk)
\zeta_{s\beta}(\tau_2,-\bk)\rangle'$.
An index on a type-$-$ slot is dotted; numerical matrix notation
identifies the two bases using $\sigma^0=\one$.
For $u_j=u_\lambda(k,\tau_j)$ and $v_j=v_\lambda(k,\tau_j)$, all four
$2\times2$ contractions follow directly from the one nonzero vacuum
product $\langle b b^\dagger\rangle$:
\begin{align}
 C^{++,>}&=-\sum_\lambda u_1v_2^*P_\lambda E,
 &C^{+-,>}&=\sum_\lambda u_1u_2^*P_\lambda,\nonumber\\
 C^{-+,>}&=-\sum_\lambda v_1v_2^*EP_\lambda E,
 &C^{--,>}&=\sum_\lambda v_1u_2^*EP_\lambda.
 \label{eq:two-contractions}
\end{align}
For example
$\langle\psi_\alpha\psi_\beta\rangle'
=\sum_\lambda u_1v_2^*h_{\lambda\alpha}
\epsilon_{\beta\gamma}h_{\lambda\gamma}^*$;
the minus sign in $-P_\lambda E$ is $E^T=-E$.
These anomalous contractions do not vanish for one Majorana copy.
Their cancellation in $\langle tt\rangle$ follows instead from the
relative $\ii^2$ between the two independent copies in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:aoki-change}.

The signed lesser contraction and the four SK components are
\begin{align}
 C^{rs,<}(\bk;1,2)&=-[C^{sr,>}(-\bk;2,1)]^T,\label{eq:reverse-line-rule}\\
 C^{rs}_{-+}&=C^{rs,>},&C^{rs}_{+-}&=C^{rs,<},\nonumber\\
 C^{rs}_{++}&=\theta(\tau_1-\tau_2)C^{rs,>}
             +\theta(\tau_2-\tau_1)C^{rs,<},&
 C^{rs}_{--}&=\theta(\tau_2-\tau_1)C^{rs,>}
             +\theta(\tau_1-\tau_2)C^{rs,<}.
 \label{eq:sk}
\end{align}
Here the two subscripts are always SK labels; superscripts $r,s$ select
$\psi$ or $d$. Reversal transposes spin indices, exchanges types and
endpoints, reverses momentum, and supplies a Grassmann minus sign.
For lower indices the canonical discontinuity is
$C^>-C^<=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\one\\\one&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
at equal times, in type-block notation. Conjugation provides an
additional check: $C^{--,>}(k;1,2)=[C^{++,>}(k;2,1)]^\dagger$.
No line includes a prefactor of $\ii$ or a power of $a$.

\subsection{Vertices and explicit index contraction}
Write $J_+=E$, $J_-=-E$. The two interaction insertions at a vertex
on SK branch $a$ are
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{\ii a g_ta(\tau)}2\delta h\,
 (J_r)^{\alpha\beta}\zeta_{r\alpha}\zeta_{r\beta},\qquad r=+,-.
 \label{eq:vertex-rule}
\end{equation}
In explicit raised-index notation $J_+^{\alpha\beta}=-\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}$
and $J_-^{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}=+\epsilon^{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}$.
Their numerical matrices are thus $E$ and $-E$, respectively.
Differentiating with respect to the two identical slots cancels $2!$
against $1/2$, giving the amputated rule
$-\ii a g_ta(\tau)J_r$. Alternatively retain $1/2$ and sum all Wick
slot assignments. These alternatives must not be combined.
An external Higgs line supplies Eq.~\eqref{eq:higgs}; integrate each
vertex over $(-\infty,0)$ with the BD prescription, and the independent
loop momentum over $d^3q/(2\pi)^3$.

For a cycle $i\to j\to k\to l\to i$ and fixed types,
the explicit spin contraction is
\begin{align}
 &(J_{r_i})^{\alpha_i\beta_i}C^{r_i r_j}_{ij,\beta_i\alpha_j}
 (J_{r_j})^{\alpha_j\beta_j}C^{r_j r_k}_{jk,\beta_j\alpha_k}
 (J_{r_k})^{\alpha_k\beta_k}C^{r_k r_l}_{kl,\beta_k\alpha_l}
 (J_{r_l})^{\alpha_l\beta_l}C^{r_l r_i}_{li,\beta_l\alpha_i}
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{8mm}=\tr_2[J_{r_i}C^{r_i r_j}_{ij}
 J_{r_j}C^{r_j r_k}_{jk}J_{r_k}C^{r_k r_l}_{kl}
 J_{r_l}C^{r_l r_i}_{li}].
 \label{eq:weyl-components}
\end{align}
This formula includes normal and anomalous lines with their correct
index tensors, including words such as $++++$ and $+-+-$. It is a
sum of $2\times2$ traces, not an assignment of a conserved Dirac arrow
to a Majorana line.

\section{Connected box and its multiplicity}
\subsection{SK expansion and Majorana Wick counting}
At fourth order expand
$\exp[-\ii g_t\sum_a a\int a(\tau)\delta h\,
(\psi\psi+\mathrm{h.c.})/2]$.
The $4!$ attachments of the labelled external Higgs fields cancel
$1/4!$. Retain the four bilinear $1/2$ factors for the following count.
Of the $7!!=105$ pairings of eight fermion slots, precisely 48 are
connected without self-contractions. They form three undirected cycles,
with $2^4$ choices of which slot at each vertex connects to its next
neighbor. Swapping these slots changes the Wick sign and the
antisymmetric $J$ sign together, so all 16 terms for a given cycle
are equal. The remaining closed-cycle Wick permutation gives a minus
sign. Thus $2^4/2^4=1$ for each \emph{undirected} Majorana cycle,
or $1/2$ for each of the six written orientations. This count is made
before referring to a Dirac trace. A Pfaffian gives the same factor
through $\log\operatorname{Pf}=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\log$.

Define the type-summed cycle tensor
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_{ijkl}=\sum_{r_i,r_j,r_k,r_l=\pm}
 \tr_2[J_{r_i}C^{r_i r_j}_{ij}J_{r_j}C^{r_j r_k}_{jk}
 J_{r_k}C^{r_k r_l}_{kl}J_{r_l}C^{r_l r_i}_{li}].
 \label{eq:weyl-box-trace}
\end{equation}
Take all external momenta incoming and choose
\begin{equation}
 p_{ij}=q+k_i,\quad p_{jk}=q+k_i+k_j,\quad
 p_{kl}=q-k_l,\quad p_{li}=q,
 \label{eq:routing}
\end{equation}
where this equation is vectorial and each $C_{uv}$ carries
$(p_{uv};\tau_u,\tau_v;a_u,a_v)$. For one Majorana copy and one color,
\begin{align}
 \G^{(1)}={}&-\frac{g_t^4}{2}
 \sum_{(1,j,k,l)}\sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_4=\pm}
 \left(\prod_{v=1}^4a_v\right)
 \int_{-\infty}^0\prod_{v=1}^4
 [d\tau_v\,a(\tau_v)G_{a_v}(k_v;\tau_v)]
 \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\mathcal T_{1jkl},\nonumber\\
 \G^{\rm top}&=2N_c\G^{(1)}.
 \label{eq:each-box}
\end{align}
The first sum is over permutations of $(j,k,l)=(2,3,4)$; the six cycles
are $1234$, $1243$, $1324$, $1342$, $1423$ and $1432$.
The loop sign and $(-\ii)^4=1$ are displayed separately from the SK
weights. Equation~\eqref{eq:each-box} is the exact box expression.
Appendix~\ref{app:dirac} proves its equality to the Dirac expression.
Explicit eight-slot Wick enumeration in the code verifies that equality
for all SK choices, rather than fixing the multiplicity by matching.

\subsection{Soft region and two-component mass projections}
The double-soft factorization~\cite{qin} selects four $s$-channel cycles
as $\bk_s=\bk_1+\bk_2\to0$:
$1234,1243$ near $q\sim k_s$, and $1342,1432$ near
$q+k_1\sim k_s$. The other two cycles have no double-soft $s$ cut
for generic hard momenta. In $1234$, the soft lines are $q$ and
$K=q+k_s$, while the right hard momentum is exactly
$q-k_4=q+k_s+k_3$. Dropping $k_s$ from that line spoils the first
nonzero coefficient. Overlapping collapsed channels are not assumed.

For reducing the type sum it is economical to form linear combinations
of the $2\times2$ contractions. Define
\begin{equation}
 N^{11}=C^{+-},\quad N^{12}=C^{++}E,\quad
 N^{21}=-EC^{--},\quad N^{22}=-EC^{-+}E,\qquad
 b=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
These $N^{rs}$ have one raised conjugate endpoint. Define the two-sector
projections and their vertex weights by
\begin{equation}
 R^{rs}=\sum_{u,v=1}^2 b_{ur}N^{uv}b_{vs},\qquad
 M^{rs}=\eta_r R^{rs},\qquad \eta_1=1,\quad\eta_2=-1.
 \label{eq:mass-projection}
\end{equation}
Here $r,s=1,2$ are projection labels, not the Majorana-copy label $I$.
Every entry remains a $2\times2$ matrix. In these variables
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_{ijkl}=\sum_{r_i,r_j,r_k,r_l=1}^2
 \tr_2[M^{r_i r_j}_{ij}M^{r_j r_k}_{jk}
 M^{r_k r_l}_{kl}M^{r_l r_i}_{li}].
\end{equation}
The radial dictionary needed below follows by substituting the
Whittaker modes, or equivalently Eq.~\eqref{eq:mode-dictionary}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cccc}
 & $R^{11}$ & $R^{12}$ & $R^{21}$ & $R^{22}$\\\hline
$>$ & $F_1F_2^*\one$ & $F_1G_2^*\sigma_k$ & $G_1F_2^*\sigma_k$ & $G_1G_2^*\one$\\
$<$ & $-G_1^*G_2\one$ & $G_1^*F_2\sigma_k$ & $F_1^*G_2\sigma_k$ & $-F_1^*F_2\one$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
We abbreviate $R^{rs}$ as $R_{rs}$ when its other two indices explicitly
carry SK labels; $\sigma_k=\bs\cdot\hat k$.
These projections permit reuse of scalar integrals without making
four-component modes the main calculation.

\section{Collapsed expansion and cancellation}
\subsection{Nonanalytic soft branches}
For $x=-k\tau\ll1$, introduce
\begin{equation}
 A=-\frac{\ii2^{-1/2-\ii\mu}}{\sqrt\pi}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii\mu),\qquad
 c=-A^2=\frac{2^{-1-2\ii\mu}}\pi\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii\mu)^2,
 \qquad |c|=\frac1{2\cosh\pi\mu}.
 \label{eq:late-coefficient}
\end{equation}
The radial expansions are
\begin{align}
 F(x)&=e^{\pi\mu/2}Ax^{\ii\mu}
 -\frac{\ii e^{-\pi\mu/2}A^*}{1-2\ii\mu}x^{1-\ii\mu}+\cdots,\nonumber\\
 G(x)&=-e^{-\pi\mu/2}A^*x^{-\ii\mu}
 +\frac{\ii e^{\pi\mu/2}A}{1+2\ii\mu}x^{1+\ii\mu}+\cdots.
 \label{eq:late}
\end{align}
The leading nonanalytic projections are
$R^{12}_{\NL}=c(k^2\tau\tau')^{\ii\mu}\sigma_k$ and
$R^{21}_{\NL}=c^*(k^2\tau\tau')^{-\ii\mu}\sigma_k$;
the zeroth-order diagonal terms depend only on $\tau/\tau'$ and
are analytic in $\bk$. The nonanalytic pieces, including descendants,
agree in $>$ and the \emph{signed} $<$ contraction. This follows
from the two Bessel branches of Eq.~\eqref{eq:modes}, or from the
reversal identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:reverse-line-rule}; keeping the
same momentum on a reversed Majorana line would destroy this identity.
They therefore have no SK labels. Analytic soft terms do not generate
the double-soft nonanalytic coefficient; hard-region contributions
supply contact terms and their matching, excluded here.

The entire $+\ii\mu$ soft branch can be written in $2\times2$ factors:
\begin{align}
 M^{rs}_{\NL,+}(k;\tau,\tau')
 &=c(k^2\tau\tau')^{\ii\mu}L_r(k,\tau)\sigma_k U_s(k,\tau'),\nonumber\\
 L_1=U_2&=f(x)\one,\qquad L_2=U_1=g(x)\sigma_k,\nonumber\\
 f(x)&={}_0F_1(\tfrac12+\ii\mu;-x^2/4),\qquad
 g(x)=-\frac{\ii x}{1+2\ii\mu}
 {}_0F_1(\tfrac32+\ii\mu;-x^2/4).
 \label{eq:soft-factored}
\end{align}
Thus $L_2=\ii\tau\bs\cdot\bk/(1+2\ii\mu)+O(k^3\tau^3)$,
while $L_1=\one+O(k^2\tau^2)$, and likewise for $U$.
The conjugate branch is determined by
$R_{\NL,-}(k;\tau,\tau')=[R_{\NL,+}(k;\tau',\tau)]^\dagger$
in sector-block notation, before the $\eta_r$ vertex weights are applied.
This exact ${}_0F_1$ form also checks that no descendant of the required
order has been omitted.

\subsection{Bose exchange and the first surviving hard blocks}
\label{sec:collapsed}
We now extract the nonanalytic signal from the four $s$-cut cycles.
All formulas from this point include both Majorana copies: the factor
$2N_c$ cancels the $1/2$ assigned to each written cycle in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:each-box}.
For $k_s\ll k_L,k_R$, use symmetric hard momenta:
\begin{align}
 \bk_1&=\bm k_L+\tfrac12\bk_s,&\bk_2&=-\bm k_L+\tfrac12\bk_s,\\
 \bk_3&=\bm k_R-\tfrac12\bk_s,&\bk_4&=-\bm k_R-\tfrac12\bk_s,\\
 \bm\ell&=\bq+\tfrac12\bk_s,&\bm K&=\bq+\bk_s,\qquad
 \bm n_L=\hat{\bm k}_L,\quad\bm n_R=\hat{\bm k}_R,\quad\bm n_s=\hat{\bk}_s.
 \label{eq:central}
\end{align}
Write $k_L=|\bm k_L|$, $k_R=|\bm k_R|$; at the accuracy of the final answer
$k_L=k_{12}/2$, $k_R=k_{34}/2$, where $k_{ij}=k_i+k_j$ is a sum of magnitudes.


For completeness the four routed cycles, cyclically started at a left
hard line, are
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{c|cccc}
cycle & left hard & soft $K$ & right hard & soft $q$\\\hline
$1234$ & $12:\ k_L+\ell$ & $23$ & $34:\ k_R+\ell$ & $41$\\
$1243$ & $12:\ k_L+\ell$ & $24$ & $43:\ -k_R+\ell$ & $31$\\
$1342$ & $21:\ -k_L+\ell$ & $13$ & $34:\ k_R+\ell$ & $42$\\
$1432$ & $21:\ -k_L+\ell$ & $14$ & $43:\ -k_R+\ell$ & $32$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All momenta in this table are vectors. The last two rows follow from
shifting the original routing variable to $q_{\rm old}=K-k_1$.
It follows directly that their sum is the product of the two independent
hard-end ordering sums, after relabelling each pair's dummy times and SK
labels. No equality of unequal external magnitudes is assumed here.

Each hard line is contracted with the endpoint factors of its two soft
neighbors. For the representative cycle these are the $2\times2$ matrices
\begin{align}
 V_L&=\sum_{r,s=1}^2 U_r(q,\tau_1)M^{rs}_{12}(\bm k_L+\bm\ell;\tau_1,\tau_2)L_s(K,\tau_2),\\
 V_R&=\sum_{r,s=1}^2 U_r(K,\tau_3)M^{rs}_{34}(\bm k_R+\bm\ell;\tau_3,\tau_4)L_s(q,\tau_4).
\end{align}
The type and projection sums in the box therefore reduce to
\begin{equation}
 c^2(qK)^{2\ii\mu}\prod_v(-\tau_v)^{\ii\mu}
 \tr_2[V_L\sigma_K V_R\sigma_q].
 \label{eq:spin-cut}
\end{equation}
The remaining spin trace reduces to scalar products through
\begin{equation}
 \tr[(\bs\cdot\bm A)\sigma_K(\bs\cdot\bm B)\sigma_q]
 =2[(\bm A\cdot\hat K)(\bm B\cdot\hat q)
 +(\bm A\cdot\hat q)(\bm B\cdot\hat K)
 -(\bm A\cdot\bm B)(\hat K\cdot\hat q)].
 \label{eq:pauli}
\end{equation}
At zeroth order only $U_2=L_1=\one$ survives. These projections select $V_L^{(0)}=-R_{21}(k_L)\propto\bs\cdot\bm n_L$,
and similarly $V_R^{(0)}\propto\bs\cdot\bm n_R$.

The four cut cycles are generated from $1234$ by the independent
exchanges $1\leftrightarrow2$ and $3\leftrightarrow4$, giving
$1234,1342,1243,1432$ after cyclic relabelling. The left exchange sends
$\bm n_L\to-\bm n_L$ at fixed $\bk_s$; the right sends
$\bm n_R\to-\bm n_R$. At this order $k_1=k_2=k_L$ and
$k_3=k_4=k_R$. After relabelling the dummy vertex times and SK indices,
the scalar time kernels are the same for both orderings, whereas
each corresponding Pauli matrix changes sign. Thus the zeroth-order
four-cycle spin factor is proportional to
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{\varepsilon_L,\varepsilon_R=\pm1}
 \varepsilon_L\varepsilon_R\,
 \tr_2[(\bs\cdot\bm n_L)\sigma_K
       (\bs\cdot\bm n_R)\sigma_q]=0.
\end{equation*}
This is a cancellation between the external-leg orderings, not a
vanishing trace for an individual box. It holds after relabelling the hard time integrals but before the soft
momentum integral is evaluated. Individual projected hard SK sums are
nonzero; this is distinct from the late-time SK cancellation discussed
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sk-endpoint-cancel}. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\left.\G^{\rm box}_{s,\rm clock}\right|_{k_s^{3\pm4\ii\mu}}=0.}
 \label{eq:cancellation}
\end{equation}
Keeping a first-order correction at only one hard end still leaves a
vanishing zeroth-order sum at the other. The first surviving clock
term therefore needs one soft-momentum correction at \emph{each} end:
both the endpoint corrections in Eq.~\eqref{eq:soft-factored} and the
momentum dependence of the hard propagators must be retained. Their
product supplies two extra powers of $k_s$, changing the clock scaling
to $k_s^{5\pm4\ii\mu}$.
The omitted second-order endpoint terms multiply a vanishing
zeroth-order hard block at the opposite end and cannot change this
leading coefficient. The next subsection evaluates the two first-order hard
blocks explicitly. This cancellation concerns the equal soft branches;
the opposite-branch, nonoscillatory contribution is treated separately
in Sec.~\ref{sec:mixed}.

\subsection{Left and right hard subgraphs}
We now calculate the first order of each Bose-symmetrized hard block.
Write $x=-k_L\tau_1$, $y=-k_L\tau_2$ and $r=p/k_L$, where
$p=|\bm p|$ denotes the hard-line momentum magnitude in this section.
Also write $\sigma_p=\bs\cdot\hat{\bm p}$. We need only two
SK-summed scalar seeds, corresponding to the $21$ and $11$ blocks of $R$:
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{mn}(\alpha,\beta;r)={}&\sum_{a,b=\pm}ab\int_0^\infty\dd x\dd y\,
 x^{\alpha-1}y^{\beta-1}(1+\ii ax)(1+\ii by)e^{-\ii ax-\ii by}
 [R_{mn}(r;x,y)]^{\rm radial}_{ab},\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{65mm} mn=21,11.
 \label{eq:hard-def}
\end{align}
``Radial'' means the coefficient of $\sigma_p$ in $R_{21}$ or of $\one$ in
$R_{11}$; their mode arguments are $rx,ry$. Hence these are scalar integrals,
with the spin structure already removed. Their finite evaluation is given
in Sec.~\ref{sec:seed-eval}.

The useful exact reversal identities are
\begin{equation}
 R_{21;ab}(\tau_1,\tau_2)=R_{21;ba}(\tau_2,\tau_1),\qquad
 R_{11;ab}(\tau_1,\tau_2)=-R_{22;ba}(\tau_2,\tau_1).
 \label{eq:reverse}
\end{equation}
For one left ordering, expanding the endpoint projectors gives
\begin{equation}
 V_L=-R_{21}+\frac{\ii\tau_1}{1+2\ii\mu}\bs\cdot\bq\,R_{11}
                  -\frac{\ii\tau_2}{1+2\ii\mu}R_{22}\bs\cdot\bm K+O(q^2,k_s^2).
 \label{eq:vertex-expand}
\end{equation}
All entries here are unapproximated hard SK contractions before expansion of their
momentum $\bm p=\bm k_L+\bm\ell$. The other ordering has
$\bm p=-\bm k_L+\bm\ell$. The derivative of the hard line and the descendants
of both soft endpoints must both be kept. Eq.~\eqref{eq:reverse} shows that the
first-order difference between the two external magnitudes gives zero in the
sum: the integrated $R_{21}$ block is symmetric in those energies. Also $\int\tau_2R_{22}=-\int\tau_1R_{11}$
at equal external magnitudes.

The intermediate momentum expansion makes the two terms in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result} explicit. Write
$h(r)=\mathcal H_{21}(\ii\mu,\ii\mu;r)$ and
$L=\bm n_L\cdot\bm\ell$. For the ordering $\varepsilon=\pm1$,
\begin{align}
 |\varepsilon k_L\bm n_L+\bm\ell|
 &=k_L+\varepsilon L+O(\ell^2/k_L),\\
 \bs\cdot\widehat{\varepsilon k_L\bm n_L+\bm\ell}
 &=\varepsilon\bs\cdot\bm n_L
 +\frac{\bs\cdot(\bm\ell-\bm n_LL)}{k_L}
 +O(\ell^2/k_L^2),\\
 h\!\left(\frac{|\varepsilon k_L\bm n_L+\bm\ell|}{k_L}\right)
 &=h(1)+\frac{\varepsilon L}{k_L}h'(1)
 +O(\ell^2/k_L^2).
 \label{eq:hard-taylor}
\end{align}
The $-R_{21}$ terms of the two orderings therefore add to
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{2}{k_L}\bs\cdot\big[
 h(1)\bm\ell+(h'(1)-h(1))\bm n_LL\big]
 +O(\ell^2/k_L^2).
 \label{eq:hard-derivative-step}
\end{equation}
The $R_{11}$ and $R_{22}$ descendants in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:vertex-expand} contribute
$-4\ii\mathcal H_{11}(1+\ii\mu,\ii\mu;1)
\bs\cdot\bm\ell/[k_L(1+2\ii\mu)]$.
The common factor $k_L^{-2\ii\mu}$ comes from
$(-\tau_1)^{\ii\mu}(-\tau_2)^{\ii\mu}$ after
$x=-k_L\tau_1$, $y=-k_L\tau_2$.
Finally, the external magnitudes are
$|\bm k_L\pm\bk_s/2|=k_L\pm\bm n_L\cdot\bk_s/2+O(k_s^2/k_L)$.
Their first-order shifts cancel under the two orderings by the
time-reversal identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:reverse}; equal external energies
are imposed only after this sum.

Strip $H^2/(4k_1^3k_2^3)$ from the left two external lines and vertices. Its
integrated projected matrix, summed over its two Higgs orderings, is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal V_L&=-2k_L^{-1-2\ii\mu}\bs\cdot
 \big[\mathcal P_1(\mu)\bm\ell+
 \mathcal P_2(\mu)\bm n_L(\bm n_L\cdot\bm\ell)\big],\\
 \mathcal P_1(\mu)&=\mathcal H_{21}(\ii\mu,\ii\mu;1)
 +\frac{2\ii}{1+2\ii\mu}\mathcal H_{11}(1+\ii\mu,\ii\mu;1),\\
 \mathcal P_2(\mu)&=\left.(\partial_r-1)\mathcal H_{21}(\ii\mu,\ii\mu;r)\right|_{r=1}.
 \label{eq:hard-result}
\end{align}
The two coefficients $\mathcal P_1,\mathcal P_2$ multiply the independent
spin structures $\bs\cdot\bm\ell$ and
$(\bs\cdot\bm n_L)(\bm n_L\cdot\bm\ell)$.
The $\mathcal H_{11}$ term comes from the soft endpoint descendants;
the $r$ derivative comes from the hard propagator's momentum dependence.
The right result is the same with $(k_L,\bm n_L)\to(k_R,\bm n_R)$ and external
prefactor $H^2/(4k_3^3k_4^3)$. In particular the four-cycle sum is the product
of these two already symmetrized blocks; no further factor of two or four is
allowed. Formula~\eqref{eq:hard-result} retains all terms at this order.
The absence of the constant term in each block makes the signal a spatial
$P$-wave fermion-pair effect. It adds two powers of $k_s$ to the bubble scaling.

\section{Scalar time integrals and soft convolution}
\subsection{Evaluation of the scalar hard seeds}
\label{sec:seed-eval}
\paragraph{Relation to the published Hankel scalar seed.}
The hard time integrals can also be reduced to the scalar seed of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}, provided its mass index is analytically continued.
This gives a direct route to the literature's single- and double-folded
formulae. Set
\begin{equation}
 \nu_h=\mu-\frac{\ii}{2},\qquad \rho=\ii\nu_h=\frac12+\ii\mu,
 \qquad x=-k_L\tau_1,\quad y=-k_L\tau_2,\quad r=p/k_L.
\end{equation}
Define a dimensionless scalar SK kernel $S_{ab}^{(\nu_h)}$ from the two
Wightman functions
\begin{align}
 S^{>}(r;x,y)&=\frac\pi4 e^{-\pi\nu_h}(xy)^{3/2}
 H^{(1)}_{\rho}(rx)H^{(2)}_{-\rho}(ry),\\
 S^{<}(r;x,y)&=\frac\pi4 e^{-\pi\nu_h}(xy)^{3/2}
 H^{(2)}_{-\rho}(rx)H^{(1)}_{\rho}(ry),
 \label{eq:scalar-seed-kernel}
\end{align}
with the same SK theta-function ordering as Eq.~\eqref{eq:sk}.
Using $H_{-\rho}^{(2)}=e^{-\ii\pi\rho}H_\rho^{(2)}$ and
$N_FN_G=\pi/4$ for the mode prefactors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:modes}, one finds
the \emph{exact} radial identities, for every branch pair,
\begin{equation}
 [R_{21}(r;x,y)]^{\rm radial}_{ab}
 =\frac{r}{xy}S_{ab}^{(\nu_h)}(r;x,y),\qquad
 [R_{11}(r;x,y)]^{\rm radial}_{ab}
 =\frac1r\left(-\ii\partial_x+\frac\mu x\right)
 [R_{21}(r;x,y)]^{\rm radial}_{ab}.
 \label{eq:fermion-scalar-seed-map}
\end{equation}
The $1/r$ in the second identity follows from differentiating a mode
with argument $rx$. Differentiating the SK theta functions introduces
no delta-function term: the two radial $R_{21}$ Wightman functions agree
at $x=y$. Although $\nu_h$ is complex, Eq.~\eqref{eq:scalar-seed-kernel}
is an analytic scalar kernel; no physical scalar with complex mass is
being introduced.

To see precisely which published seeds are needed, let
\begin{equation}
 J^{p,q}_{ab}(r)=\int_0^\infty\dd x\dd y\,
 x^p y^q e^{-\ii ax-\ii by}S_{ab}^{(\nu_h)}(r;x,y).
\end{equation}
In the units $H=1$ of Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}, its branch-resolved Hankel
seed $\mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}$ obeys the exact normalization map
\begin{equation}
 J^{p,q}_{ab}(r)=-ab\,r^{-5-p-q}
 \mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}(u,u),\qquad
 u=\frac{2r}{1+r}.
 \label{eq:seed-normalization}
\end{equation}
The reference includes $-ab$ in the seed definition; neither that sign
nor the power of $r$ may be omitted. For
$p=\alpha-2$, $q=\beta-2$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-def} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{21}(\alpha,\beta;r)
 =r\sum_{a,b=\pm}ab\big[
 J^{p,q}_{ab}+\ii aJ^{p+1,q}_{ab}
 +\ii bJ^{p,q+1}_{ab}-abJ^{p+1,q+1}_{ab}\big].
 \label{eq:published-seed-map}
\end{equation}
The four terms correspond to the integer time-power shifts generated by
$(1+\ii ax)(1+\ii by)$. The SK indices stay explicit until their
$a,b$ weights are combined.

The remaining hard coefficient can also be expressed without a new
time integral. Put $\mathscr S_{ab}^{p,q}=\mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}(1,1)$,
$p_0=-2+\ii\mu+\delta$, and use one common endpoint regulator
$\delta$ in all seed powers. Equation~\eqref{eq:published-seed-map} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{21}(\ii\mu+\delta,\ii\mu+\delta;1)
 =-\sum_{a,b=\pm}\sum_{j,l=0}^1
 (\ii a)^j(\ii b)^l\mathscr S_{ab}^{p_0+j,p_0+l}.
 \label{eq:H21-literature}
\end{equation}
Integrating the second relation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fermion-scalar-seed-map} by parts yields
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{11}(1+\ii\mu+\delta,\ii\mu+\delta;1)
 ={}&\ii\delta\,
 \mathcal H_{21}(\ii\mu+\delta,\ii\mu+\delta;1)\nonumber\\
 &-\ii\sum_{a,b=\pm}\left[
 \mathscr S_{ab}^{\ii\mu+\delta,p_0}
 +\ii b\,\mathscr S_{ab}^{\ii\mu+\delta,p_0+1}\right].
 \label{eq:H11-literature}
\end{align}
The $\ii\delta\mathcal H_{21}$ term is retained at finite regulator;
the complete $\mathcal H_{21}$ will be finite, so this term vanishes
in the final limit. Individual SK terms do have regulator poles.
The integration-by-parts boundary term at
$x=0$ cancels only in the complete SK sum, since for $x<y$ the two
branches with the same $b$ have the same Wightman function.

The hard-momentum derivative in $\mathcal P_2$ follows from the
published scalar bootstrap equation without differentiating a time
integral. At $u_1=u_2=1$, the coefficient of its second derivative
vanishes. Define
\begin{align}
 T_{ab}^{p,q}&=\delta_{ab}\,
 e^{-\ii a\pi(p+q)/2}2^{-5-p-q}\Gamma(5+p+q),\\
 \mathscr D_{ab}^{p,q}&=\frac12\sum_{w=p,q}
 \frac{\nu_h^2+(w+5/2)^2}{w+3}\,\mathscr S_{ab}^{p,q}
 -\frac12\left(\frac1{p+3}+\frac1{q+3}\right)T_{ab}^{p,q}.
 \label{eq:seed-bootstrap-derivative}
\end{align}
Since $\dd u/\dd r|_{r=1}=1/2$, $\mathscr D_{ab}^{p,q}$ is precisely
$\dd\mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}(u(r),u(r))/\dd r|_{r=1}$.
Combining this with the explicit $r^{-5-p-q}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed-normalization} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_2=-\FP_{\delta\to0}
 \sum_{a,b=\pm}\sum_{j,l=0}^1(\ii a)^j(\ii b)^l
 \left[\mathscr D_{ab}^{p_0+j,p_0+l}
 -(5+2p_0+j+l)\mathscr S_{ab}^{p_0+j,p_0+l}\right].
 \label{eq:P2-literature}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:H21-literature}--\eqref{eq:P2-literature}, with
$\mathcal P_1$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}, complete both hard
subgraphs in terms of the scalar seed values of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}; the right subgraph uses the same two functions.
\paragraph{Regularization and a finite evaluation.}
There is an essential normalization in Eq.~(92) of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}: its hypergeometric function is regularized,
\begin{equation}
 {}_3\widetilde F_2(a,b,c;d,e;1)
 =\frac{{}_3F_2(a,b,c;d,e;1)}{\Gamma(d)\Gamma(e)}.
 \label{eq:regularized-hyper}
\end{equation}
A historical implementation omitted these two Gamma factors; the
workspace version preceding this rewrite had already corrected them.
This is not a new correction from the Majorana change of variables. A real-index conjugation
check alone cannot detect this error. As an independent normalization
check, the conformal scalar $\nu_h=-\ii/2$ has
$S^>(1;x,y)=xy\,e^{\ii(x-y)}/2$. Direct elementary time integration gives
$\mathscr S_{++}^{0,0}=\mathscr S_{--}^{0,0}=-3/16$ and
$\mathscr S_{+-}^{0,0}=\mathscr S_{-+}^{0,0}=1/32$; the corrected
implementation reproduces these values and their unequal-power generalization.

The scalar kernel is invariant under $\nu_h\to-\nu_h$, by the
Hankel order-reflection identities. In each equal-SK seed choose the
sign of $\nu_h$ so that $a\ii\nu_h=1/2+\ii\mu$. Its ordinary
${}_3F_2(1)$ then has convergence balance $1+\ii\mu$, whose real
part is positive. This is an exact change of representation and needs
no additional mass-index regulator.

For this subsection only set $z=\ii\mu$, and define the single symmetric
family
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Q_{jl}=\frac{2^{-1-2z-j-l}\Gamma(1+2z+j+l)}
 {(1+2z+j)(1+2z+l)}
 {}_3F_2\!\left(\begin{matrix}1+2z+j+l,\ 1+z,\ 1\\
 2+2z+j,\ 2+2z+l\end{matrix};1\right).
 \label{eq:finite-Q}
\end{equation}
Only $\mathcal Q_{00},\mathcal Q_{01},\mathcal Q_{11},
\mathcal Q_{02},\mathcal Q_{12}$ are needed. Also abbreviate the
finite endpoint contribution by
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E=\pi^2 2^{-1-2z}
 \frac{\Gamma(1+2z)^2}{\Gamma(1+z)^2}.
 \label{eq:endpoint-finite}
\end{equation}
The fully summed hard seeds and momentum derivative are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{21}(z,z;1)
 &=2\cosh(\pi\mu)(\mathcal Q_{00}+2\mathcal Q_{01}+\mathcal Q_{11})
 -\mathcal E,\nonumber\\
 \mathcal H_{11}(1+z,z;1)
 &=-2\ii\cosh(\pi\mu)(\mathcal Q_{02}+\mathcal Q_{12}),
 \label{eq:finite-hard-seeds}\\
 \mathcal P_2
 &=2\cosh(\pi\mu)\sum_{j,l=0}^1
 \left[\frac{j(1+2z+j)}{2(1+z+j)}
 +\frac{l(1+2z+l)}{2(1+z+l)}-(1+2z+j+l)\right]\mathcal Q_{jl}
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad +(1+2z)\mathcal E
 +2^{-1-2z}\cosh(\pi\mu)\Gamma(1+2z)
 \frac{4+5z+2z^2}{1+z}.
 \label{eq:finite-P2}
\end{align}
Together with $\mathcal P_1$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}, these are
finite, convergent special-function expressions for the complete hard
coefficients. No raw mode-power-series truncation or numerical
regulator extrapolation is required for the oscillatory signal.

Here is the endpoint cancellation leading to these expressions.
For $p_j=-2+z+\delta+j$, write the Gamma product in the opposite-SK
seed as
\begin{equation}
 C_{jl}(\delta)=
 \frac{\Gamma(\delta+j)\Gamma(1+2z+\delta+j)
       \Gamma(\delta+l)\Gamma(1+2z+\delta+l)}
 {\Gamma(1+z+\delta+j)\Gamma(1+z+\delta+l)}.
\end{equation}
After the external-mode factors $(\ii a)^j(\ii b)^l$ and the SK
branches are summed, its contribution to $\mathcal H_{21}$ is
$2\,2^{-1-2z-2\delta-j-l}[\cos(\pi\delta)-1]C_{jl}(\delta)$.
Only $j=l=0$ has a double pole; therefore
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\delta\to0}2[\cos(\pi\delta)-1]C_{00}(\delta)
 =-\pi^2\frac{\Gamma(1+2z)^2}{\Gamma(1+z)^2}.
\end{equation}
This is precisely $-\mathcal E$ after restoring its power of two.
Discarding the poles branch by branch would lose this finite term.
The remaining regularized-hypergeometric pieces give
$2\cosh(\pi\mu)\mathcal Q_{jl}$.
The integration-by-parts insertion in Eq.~\eqref{eq:H11-literature}
replaces $j=0,1$ by $j=2$ and contributes $-\ii$ times the same
pieces; it has no double-pole remainder.
Finally, substituting $p_j$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed-bootstrap-derivative} gives the bracket in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-P2}. The last term in that equation is the
summed bootstrap source, and $(1+2z)\mathcal E$ is the derivative's
endpoint remainder. These steps preserve all factors of $r$.

The late-time cancellation can also be seen before integration.
With $E_a(x)=(1+\ii ax)e^{-\ii ax}$, the SK sum for $x<y$ is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{a,b}ab E_a(x)E_b(y)R_{ab}(x,y)
 =[E_+(x)-E_-(x)]
 [E_+(y)R^>(x,y)-E_-(y)R^<(x,y)].
 \label{eq:sk-endpoint-cancel}
\end{equation}
Since $E_+(x)-E_-(x)=-2\ii x^3/3+O(x^5)$, the common late-time
endpoint is integrable in the hard blocks used here. The early-time
BD prescription is still necessary; this identity does not justify
integrating undamped oscillations numerically on a truncated interval.

\subsection{Soft bubble integral}
The spin trace of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pauli} leaves a polynomial numerator divided
by $qK$. To display it, write the two vectors appearing in the hard blocks as
\begin{equation}
 \bm v_L=\mathcal P_1\bm\ell+\mathcal P_2\bm n_L(\bm n_L\cdot\bm\ell),\qquad
 \bm v_R=\mathcal P_1\bm\ell+\mathcal P_2\bm n_R(\bm n_R\cdot\bm\ell).
\end{equation}
Since $\bm K=\bm\ell+\bk_s/2$ and $\bq=\bm\ell-\bk_s/2$, the scalar loop
integral after the spin contraction is
\begin{align}
 &2\int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3}(qK)^{2\ii\mu-1}
 \big[2(\bm v_L\cdot\bm\ell)(\bm v_R\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\tfrac12(\bm v_L\cdot\bk_s)(\bm v_R\cdot\bk_s)\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{55mm}-(\bm v_L\cdot\bm v_R)(\ell^2-k_s^2/4)\big].
 \label{eq:spin-bubble}
\end{align}
Its mass dimension is $5+4\ii\mu$. We now evaluate it by scalar convolutions,
following the same procedure as for the vector box. The only required scalar
formula is
\begin{align}
 F_0(A,B)&=\frac{\Gamma(A+B-\tfrac32)\Gamma(\tfrac32-A)\Gamma(\tfrac32-B)}
 {\Gamma(A)\Gamma(B)\Gamma(3-A-B)},\\
 \int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3}(q^2)^{-A}(K^2)^{-B}
 &=\frac{k_s^{3-2A-2B}}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}F_0(A,B).
 \label{eq:scalar-bubble}
\end{align}
For the scalar numerator this identity follows by Schwinger parameters
and a Gaussian momentum integral in the convergence domain
$\Re A,\Re B<3/2$, $\Re(A+B)>3/2$. The shifted polynomial moments
are defined by the same meromorphic continuation, with common regulator
parameters before recombination. The physical soft-region integrals have
UV power divergences when extended beyond $q\sim k_s$; this continuation
selects their nonanalytic coefficient. UV-dependent polynomial contact
terms belong to matching with the hard region and are excluded.
No physical UV cutoff is sent to infinity term by term.

For the reduction only, measure $q,K,\ell$ in units of $k_s$, put
$u=q^2$, $v=K^2$, and set $\nu=1/2-\ii\mu$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \ell^2=(u+v)/2-1/4,\qquad
 \ell_\parallel\equiv\bm\ell\cdot\bm n_s=(v-u)/2,\qquad
 \ell_\perp^2=\ell^2-\ell_\parallel^2.
\end{equation}
Azimuthal averaging replaces two transverse components by
$\ell_\perp^2\delta^\perp_{ij}/2$ and four by
$\ell_\perp^4(\delta^\perp_{ij}\delta^\perp_{kl}
+\delta^\perp_{ik}\delta^\perp_{jl}+\delta^\perp_{il}\delta^\perp_{jk})/8$,
where $\delta^\perp_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-n_{s i}n_{s j}$.
The numerator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spin-bubble} consequently becomes a polynomial
in $u,v$ and the three external angles. Each monomial is integrated by
\begin{align}
 u^mv^n&\quad\longmapsto\quad
 \frac{F_0(\nu-m,\nu-n)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}},\\
 \frac{F_0(\nu-m,\nu-n)}{F_0(\nu,\nu)}&=
 \frac{(3/2-\nu)_m(3/2-\nu)_n(1-\nu)_m(1-\nu)_n}
 {(5/2-2\nu)_{m+n}(3-2\nu)_{m+n}}.
 \label{eq:bubble-reduction}
\end{align}
Only nonnegative integers $m,n$ with $m+n\leq4$ occur. Thus the bubble is a
finite Gamma sum, and all its terms reduce to the same $F_0(\nu,\nu)$ times
rational functions. For example the coefficient of $\mathcal P_1^2$ in the
square bracket of Eq.~\eqref{eq:spin-bubble} is
$\ell^4+\ell^2/4-\ell_\parallel^2/2$, whose integral is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{F_0(\nu,\nu)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}
 \frac{3(\nu-1)(2\nu-3)}{8(\nu-2)(4\nu-7)(4\nu-5)}.
\end{equation}
The $\mathcal P_1\mathcal P_2$ and $\mathcal P_2^2$ terms are evaluated by
the same two steps. Their fully reduced coefficients are displayed directly
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:compact-coefficients}, without introducing separate symbols
for each tensor moment.
The azimuthal average introduces only the five angle monomials
$1$, $c_1^2+c_3^2$, $c_1^2c_3^2$,
$\sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3$, and
$\sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3)$, where
$c_i=\cos\theta_i$. Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:bubble-reduction} to their
coefficients reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:compact-coefficients}.

\section{Nonlocal signal}
\subsection{Oscillatory signal and angular coefficients}
Choose the angle convention
\begin{align}
 \bm n_L&=(\sin\theta_1,0,\cos\theta_1),&
 \bm n_R&=(\sin\theta_3\cos\phi_3,\sin\theta_3\sin\phi_3,\cos\theta_3),&
 \bm n_s&=(0,0,1).
\end{align}
The leading nonzero oscillatory term, summed over the four $s$-cut boxes, is
\begin{align}
 \left.\langle\delta h_{\bk_1}\delta h_{\bk_2}\delta h_{\bk_3}\delta h_{\bk_4}\rangle'
 \right|_{s,\rm clock}
 ={}&-\frac{N_cg_t^4H^4 k_s^5}{16\,k_1^3k_2^3k_3^3k_4^3 k_Lk_R}\nonumber\\
 &\times\left[\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)^{2\ii\mu}
 \cC(\mu;\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)+\mathrm{c.c.}\right].
 \label{eq:final}
\end{align}
The coefficient is given directly in the angular basis of the vector-box note:
\begin{align}
 \cC={}&A_1+A_2(\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_3)
 +A_3\cos^2\theta_1\cos^2\theta_3\nonumber\\
 &+B_1\sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3
 +D_1\sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3).
 \label{eq:angular-final}
\end{align}
All five coefficients share one soft-bubble normalization, including the two
soft-mode coefficients $c^2$, the factor $4$ from the two Bose-summed hard blocks, the factor $2$ from the Pauli trace, and the phase from the chosen momentum ratio:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B(\mu)=
 \frac{2^{1+4\ii\mu}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii\mu)^4 F_0(\nu,\nu)}
 {32\pi^2(4\pi)^{3/2}(\nu-3)(\nu-2)(4\nu-7)(4\nu-5)},\qquad
 \nu=\tfrac12-\ii\mu.
 \label{eq:bubble-normalization}
\end{equation}
In particular, the natural hard scaling is
$(k_s^2/(k_Lk_R))^{2\ii\mu}$. Since
$4k_{12}k_{34}=16k_Lk_R$ at this order, using the vector note's
ratio transfers $16^{2\ii\mu}$ into the coefficient. Thus
$8c^2\,16^{2\ii\mu}
=2^{1+4\ii\mu}\Gamma(1/2-\ii\mu)^4/\pi^2$, which fixes both
the phase and the overall factor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bubble-normalization}.
Combining the bubble with the two hard coefficients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}
gives the final mass dependence:
\begin{align}
 A_1&=\mathcal B(\nu-1)(2\nu-3)
 \big[4(\nu-3)(3\mathcal P_1^2+2\mathcal P_1\mathcal P_2)
 +(2\nu-5)\mathcal P_2^2\big],\\*
 A_2&=-\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\nu-1)(2\nu-3)^2,\qquad A_3=-3A_2,\\*
 B_1&=-2\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\nu-1)(\nu-2)(2\nu-3),\\*
 D_1&=\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\nu-2)(2\nu-5)(2\nu-3).
 \label{eq:compact-coefficients}
\end{align}
Thus the answer requires only two hard functions $\mathcal P_1,\mathcal P_2$
and one explicit bubble factor $\mathcal B$. The coefficients are complex and
include the clock phase. The full answer is even under either Higgs-pair exchange
and under parity. Its engineering dimension is $-5$, as required for the primed
four-point function. The powers of $H$ follow from four rescaled Weyl Yukawa
vertices and four external Higgs contractions; no extra factor of $H$ or a
second color/orientation multiplicity is implicit. For fixed $\mu>0$ these are the leading double-soft nonanalytic
terms; higher soft powers and analytic contact terms are not displayed.
With the centered hard momenta the next clock terms begin at
$k_s^{7\pm4\ii\mu}$, and the next mixed terms at $k_s^5$.
Thus the generic relative error within each branch is
$O(k_s^2/k_L^2,k_s^2/k_R^2,k_s^2/(k_Lk_R))$; it is not a
relative-error bound at a zero of the displayed angular coefficient.

\subsection{Nonoscillatory contribution}
\label{sec:mixed}
Taking opposite soft branches gives a nonoscillatory $k_s^3$ term. The hard
blocks are $R_{11}$ and $-R_{22}$; Eq.~\eqref{eq:reverse} makes their integrated
values equal to the real seed $\mathcal H_{11}(-\ii\mu,+\ii\mu;1)$.
The leading soft matrices are now opposite triangular blocks, so they
project the diagonal hard blocks. Each hard end therefore has a Bose-even
zeroth-order term. There are two soft assignments and two Higgs orderings
at each end, giving $2\times2\times2$ contributions; the spin trace
adds $2\hat q\cdot\hat K$. The soft coefficients give
$|c|^2=1/(4\cosh^2\pi\mu)$. These factors fix the normalization of
the mixed term independently of the oscillatory branch.
The scalar bubble in this case is
\begin{equation}
 \int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3}(q^2)^{-\ii\mu}(K^2)^{\ii\mu}
 \hat q\cdot\hat K
 =\frac{k_s^3}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\,
 \frac{2\sqrt\pi}{3}\mu(1+\mu^2)\coth(\pi\mu).
\end{equation}
After both soft branches and all four $s$-cut boxes are summed, the result is
\begin{equation}
 \left.\langle\delta h^4\rangle'\right|_{s,\mathrm{mixed}}
 =\frac{N_cg_t^4H^4 k_s^3}{16\prod_i k_i^3}
 \frac{2\mu(1+\mu^2)}{3\pi\sinh(2\pi\mu)}
 \big[\mathcal H_{11}(-\ii\mu,+\ii\mu;1)\big]^2.
 \label{eq:mixed}
\end{equation}
It is independent of angles at this order. Equation~\eqref{eq:mixed} is the
leading nonoscillatory nonanalytic term, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:final} is the
leading mass-dependent clock signal. At $\mu=0$ the separated branches coalesce;
the limit must be taken after combining the relevant terms.
Here $k_s^3=(\bk_s^2)^{3/2}$ is nonanalytic as a function of the
soft momentum components, even though it has no mass-dependent oscillation.
It cannot be discarded as a polynomial contact term.

For evaluation of the remaining mixed seed, the same radial identity
and integration by parts give the general formula
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{11}(\alpha,\beta;1)
 ={}&[\ii(\alpha-1)+\mu]\mathcal H_{21}(\alpha-1,\beta;1)
 \nonumber\\
 &-\ii\sum_{a,b=\pm}\left[
 \mathscr S_{ab}^{\alpha-1,\beta-2}
 +\ii b\,\mathscr S_{ab}^{\alpha-1,\beta-1}\right].
 \label{eq:mixed-seed-eval}
\end{align}
Set $\alpha=-\ii\mu+\delta$, $\beta=\ii\mu+\delta$, perform the
complete sum, and then take $\delta\to0$ using the corrected
regularized seeds. The conjugation relation
$\mathcal H_{11}(\alpha,\beta;1)^*
=\mathcal H_{11}(\beta^*,\alpha^*;1)$ proves the mixed seed is real.
The code evaluates this limit by fourth-order extrapolation, with
step-size and precision checks; the clock coefficients above instead
use the analytic limit.
For example,
$\mathcal H_{11}(-0.73\ii,+0.73\ii;1)=-1.388500280198814$;
halving the extrapolation step changes this value by less than
$2\times10^{-15}$.

The soft-expansion check retains the exact ${}_0F_1$ endpoints,

\section{Dirac comparison as a check}
\label{app:dirac}
This appendix introduces four-component notation only to check the
calculation above. Define
\begin{equation}
 \eta=\binom{\psi_\alpha}{\psi^{\dagger\dot\alpha}},\quad
 \zeta=\binom{\psi_\alpha}{\psi^\dagger_{\dot\alpha}}=T\eta,\quad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}\one&0\\0&E\end{pmatrix},\quad
 X=\begin{pmatrix}0&\one\\\one&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 J=\begin{pmatrix}E&0\\0&-E\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
If $C=\langle T_C\zeta\zeta^T\rangle$, then
$R_W=\langle T_C\eta\eta^\dagger\rangle=T^\dagger CXT$ and
$T^\dagger XJT=\beta_W=X$. Cyclicity of the trace proves, without
any momentum approximation,
\begin{equation}
 \tr_4[JC_{ij}JC_{jk}JC_{kl}JC_{li}]
 =\tr_4[\beta_W R_{W,ij}\beta_W R_{W,jk}
         \beta_W R_{W,kl}\beta_W R_{W,li}].
\end{equation}
The left-hand side is exactly the 16 two-component type contractions
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:weyl-box-trace}. With
\begin{equation}
 U_B=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}\one&-\one\\\one&\one\end{pmatrix},
 \quad R=U_B^\dagger R_WU_B,\quad
 \beta=U_B^\dagger\beta_WU_B=\operatorname{diag}(\one,-\one),
\end{equation}
its entries are precisely the projections of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mass-projection}. The positive and independent negative
frequency mode matrices in this basis may be chosen as
\begin{equation}
 U=\binom{F\one}{G\sigma_k},\qquad
 V=\binom{G^*\one}{-F^*\sigma_k},\qquad
 R^>=U_1U_2^\dagger,\quad R^<=-V_1V_2^\dagger.
\end{equation}
The negative-frequency $V$ is unrelated to the name of Aoki's radial
$v_\lambda$; its relative minus sign is essential. For the charged Dirac
field the particle and antiparticle operators are independent, whereas
in $\eta$ they are related by charge conjugation. The two-point matrix
is nevertheless the same. The distinction in a closed loop is the
Majorana $1/2$ established by Wick counting. Multiplying by two copies
recovers six Dirac cycles with coefficient $-N_cg_t^4$ each. There is
no remaining orientation, spin or color multiplicity to add.
