\section{Top quark propagators and Yukawa vertices}
We calculate the Higgs four-point function generated by a top-quark box
in exact de Sitter space. The top mass is constant, the chemical potential
vanishes, and the external Higgs is a light spectator. We use the
Weyl-spinor conventions of Aoki et al.~\cite{aoki}, consistent with the
corrected free-field formulas of You et al.~\cite{you} (v2).

\subsection{Higgs field and Majorana decomposition}
The metric and scale factor are
\begin{equation}
 ds^2=a^2(\tau)(-d\tau^2+d\bm x^2),\qquad
 a(\tau)=-\frac1{H\tau},\qquad \tau<0.
\end{equation}
We take $\sigma^a=(\one,\bs)$ and $\bar\sigma^a=(\one,-\bs)$.
For $\Phi=(0,(h_0+\delta h)/\sqrt2)^T$, define
\begin{equation}
 g_t=\frac{y_t}{\sqrt2},\qquad m_t=g_th_0,\qquad
 \mu=\frac{m_t}{H},\qquad N_c=3.
\end{equation}
The massless Higgs bulk-to-boundary propagator is
\begin{equation}
 G_a(k;\tau)=\frac{H^2}{2k^3}(1-\ii a k\tau)e^{\ii a k\tau},\qquad a=\pm1.
 \label{eq:higgs}
\end{equation}
This prescription assumes $|\dot h_0|\ll H|h_0|$ and
$|m_{h,\mathrm{eff}}^2|\ll H^2$ over the relevant times~\cite{lu}.
The Higgs-to-curvature conversion is not included.

\paragraph{Spinor indices.}
\label{sec:spinor-indices}
The spinor indices are raised and lowered by
\begin{align}
 \epsilon_{12}=-\epsilon_{21}&=1,\qquad
 \epsilon^{12}=-\epsilon^{21}=-1,\nonumber\\
 \psi^\alpha&=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\psi_\beta,\qquad
 \psi_\alpha=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\psi^\beta,\qquad
 \epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\beta\gamma}=\delta^\alpha{}_{\gamma}.
 \label{eq:epsilon}
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
 \psi\chi\equiv\psi^\alpha\chi_\alpha
 =-\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\psi_\alpha\chi_\beta
 =\chi\psi,\qquad
 (\psi\psi)^\dagger
 =\psi^\dagger_{\dot\alpha}\psi^{\dagger\dot\alpha}
 =\epsilon^{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}
   \psi^\dagger_{\dot\alpha}\psi^\dagger_{\dot\beta}.
 \label{eq:spinor-bilinears}
\end{equation}
The symmetry $\psi\chi=\chi\psi$ uses both the Grassmann sign and the
antisymmetry of epsilon; Hermitian conjugation reverses operator order.

Let $t,t^c$ be the rescaled left-handed top and the left-handed charge
conjugate of the right-handed top, respectively. The latter carries the
conjugate gauge representation.
The rescaled Dirac-field action is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\ii t^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu t
 +\ii t^{c\dagger}\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu t^c
 -a(m_t+g_t\delta h)(tt^c+\mathrm{h.c.}).
 \label{eq:action-dirac}
\end{equation}

With $\widetilde\psi_I=a^{3/2}\psi_I$, Aoki's field redefinition is
\begin{equation}
 t=\frac{\widetilde\psi_1+\ii\widetilde\psi_2}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 t^c=\frac{\widetilde\psi_1-\ii\widetilde\psi_2}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:aoki-change}
\end{equation}
The bilinear $tt^c=t^\alpha(t^c)_\alpha$ is a Lorentz scalar.
Its mixed terms cancel under this substitution, giving the coordinate
Lagrangian density
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathcal L=\sum_{I=1}^2\left[
 \ii\widetilde\psi_I^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\widetilde\psi_I
 -\frac a2(m_t+g_t\delta h)(\widetilde\psi_I\widetilde\psi_I+\mathrm{h.c.})\right].}
 \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
Gauge interactions are omitted, so the two copies are independent for
each color. We calculate one copy and restore $2N_c$ in the box.
The factor $a$ in the mass and Yukawa terms is
$a^4(a^{-3/2})^2$.

We use $\epsilon_{12}=1$, $\epsilon^{12}=-1$ and
$\psi^\alpha=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\psi_\beta$, with the same rules
for dotted indices. In numerical matrix products,
\begin{equation}
 \epsmat=(\epsilon_{\alpha\beta})=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}=\ii\sigma^2,
 \qquad (\epsilon^{\alpha\beta})=-\epsmat.
\end{equation}
Every field is Fourier transformed with $e^{\ii\bk\cdot\bm x}$ and
measure $d^3k/(2\pi)^3$. A prime removes the overall momentum delta
function.

\subsection{Mode functions}
For one Majorana copy the helicity spinors obey
\begin{equation}
 (\bs\cdot\hat k)h^s=s h^s,\qquad
 P_s=h^s h^{s\dagger}
 =\frac{\one+s\bs\cdot\hat k}{2}.
\end{equation}
With one copy
and color suppressed, the operator expansion is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde\psi_\alpha(\tau,\bm x)=\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}
 \sum_{s=\pm}\left[
 h^s_\alpha u_s b^s_{\bk}e^{\ii\bk\cdot\bm x}
 +h^{s\dagger}_{\dot\gamma}\bar\sigma^{0\dot\gamma\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta} v_s^*b^{s\dagger}_{\bk}
 e^{-\ii\bk\cdot\bm x}\right].
 \label{eq:mode-expansion}
\end{equation}
Here $\{b^s_{\bk},b^{s'\dagger}_{\bk'}\}
=(2\pi)^3\delta^{ss'}\delta^3(\bk-\bk')$.
The radial equations are
\begin{align}
 \ii\partial_\tau u_s+s ku_s&=am_tv_s,&
 \ii\partial_\tau v_s-s kv_s&=am_tu_s,\label{eq:weyl-eom}\\
 u_s''-aHu_s'+(k^2+a^2m_t^2+\ii s aHk)u_s&=0,&
 v_s''-aHv_s'+(k^2+a^2m_t^2-\ii s aHk)v_s&=0.
\end{align}

With $x=-k\tau$, the normalized Bunch--Davies radial modes are
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{u_+=v_- =\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{2x}}W_{-1/2,\ii\mu}(-2\ii x),\qquad
 u_-=v_+ =\frac{\ii}{\sqrt{2x}}W_{1/2,\ii\mu}(-2\ii x).}
 \label{eq:aoki-modes}
\end{equation}
Here $u_s,v_s$ are the two radial functions in the
operator expansion~\eqref{eq:mode-expansion}; the creation term contains
$v_s^*$. Their normalization is
$|u_s|^2+|v_s|^2=1$. In particular,
$u_+=v_-$ and $u_-=v_+$, with the relative $\ii$ displayed above.

Take the boundary value from the BD continuation. At large $x$ the
nonvanishing massless-helicity amplitudes tend to
$e^{\ii\pi/4}e^{\ii x}$, and the other amplitudes are $O(\mu/x)$.
The first-order equations conserve $|u_s|^2+|v_s|^2$,
which is one by this boundary condition. Together with
$\epsmat P_s^T(-\hat k)\epsmat^{-1}=P_s(\hat k)$ this gives the canonical
anticommutators, including $\{\widetilde\psi,\widetilde\psi\}=0$.

For the time integrals we use the equivalent Hankel functions
\begin{equation}
 F=\frac{\sqrt{\pi x}}2e^{\pi\mu/2}H^{(1)}_{1/2-\ii\mu}(x),\qquad
 G=\frac{\sqrt{\pi x}}2e^{-\pi\mu/2}H^{(1)}_{1/2+\ii\mu}(x).
 \label{eq:modes}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 u_s=e^{3\ii\pi/4}\frac{F-s G}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 v_s=e^{3\ii\pi/4}\frac{F+s G}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 \label{eq:mode-dictionary}
\end{equation}
These are scalar radial identities; all propagators below still connect
$\widetilde\psi$ and $\widetilde\psi^\dagger$.

From the next subsection onward, we suppress the tildes: $\psi$
denotes the conformally rescaled field $\widetilde\psi$, and $D$ denotes
its propagators. The original covariant spinor is no longer used.
Thus the propagators below contain no external factors of $a^{-3/2}$;
the Yukawa vertices retain the factor $a(\tau)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:action}.

\subsection{Propagators and vertices}
\label{sec:propagators}
We follow the three propagator types and SK ordering of
You et al.~\cite{you} (v2), Eqs.~(2.30), (2.33), and (2.36),
specialized to $\widetilde\lambda=0$ and
$\widetilde M_R=\widetilde\nu=\mu$. Our $\psi$ is their rescaled field
$\widetilde N$; all spinor indices below are lowered using
Eq.~\eqref{eq:epsilon}. We denote the two contour branches by $+,-$,
identified with the numerical values $+1,-1$ in SK vertex weights.
There is no overall factor of $\ii$ in the propagator definitions.

\paragraph{Three types of propagators.}
With $1=(\tau_1,\bk)$ and $2=(\tau_2,-\bk)$, define
\begin{align}
 D_{ab\,\alpha\dot\beta}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\langle T_C\psi_{a,\alpha}(1)\psi^\dagger_{b,\dot\beta}(2)\rangle',
 \nonumber\\
 \widehat D_{ab\,\alpha\beta}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\langle T_C\psi_{a,\alpha}(1)\psi_{b,\beta}(2)\rangle',
 \nonumber\\
 \check D_{ab\,\dot\alpha\dot\beta}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\langle T_C\psi^\dagger_{a,\dot\alpha}(1)
                    \psi^\dagger_{b,\dot\beta}(2)\rangle'.
 \label{eq:aoki-indexed-propagators}
\end{align}
Here $T_C$ orders fermions on the closed time contour. The three
$-+$ components are the unordered contractions
$\langle\psi_1\psi_2^\dagger\rangle'$,
$\langle\psi_1\psi_2\rangle'$, and
$\langle\psi_1^\dagger\psi_2^\dagger\rangle'$, respectively.
Using $u_{sj}=u_s(\tau_j,k)$, $v_{sj}=v_s(\tau_j,k)$ and
$P_s=(\one+s\sigma_k)/2$, $\sigma_k=\bs\cdot\hat{\bk}$, their
explicit helicity sums are
\begin{align}
 D_{-+}
 &=\sum_{s=\pm1}u_{s1}u_{s2}^*P_s,
 \nonumber\\
 \widehat D_{-+}
 &=-\sum_{s=\pm1}u_{s1}v_{s2}^*P_s\epsmat,
 \nonumber\\
 \check D_{-+}
 &=\sum_{s=\pm1}v_{s1}u_{s2}^*\epsmat P_s.
 \label{eq:propagator-mode-sums}
\end{align}
These matrix expressions follow directly from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mode-expansion}: the creation wavefunction is
$v_s^*\epsmat h^{s*}$, and
$h^s(\epsmat h^{s*})^T=-P_s\epsmat$.
This fixes the epsilon signs when translating the helicity-spinor
expressions of Ref.~\cite{you} to our lower-index convention.

For the complete special-function expressions, set $x_j=-k\tau_j>0$ and
\begin{equation}
 W_{\pm j}=W_{\pm\frac12,\ii\mu}(-2\ii x_j),\qquad
 W_{\pm j}^*=W_{\pm\frac12,\ii\mu}(2\ii x_j).
 \label{eq:propagator-whittaker}
\end{equation}
The second equality uses real $\mu,x_j$ and
$W_{\kappa,\nu}=W_{\kappa,-\nu}$ on the BD boundary values.
Substitution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:aoki-modes} gives
\begin{align}
 D_{-+}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{\mu^2W_{-1}W_{-2}^*(\one+\sigma_k)
       +W_{+1}W_{+2}^*(\one-\sigma_k)}{4\sqrt{x_1x_2}},
 \label{eq:normal-whittaker}\\
 \widehat D_{-+}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{\ii\mu}{4\sqrt{x_1x_2}}
 \left[W_{-1}W_{+2}^*(\one+\sigma_k)
       -W_{+1}W_{-2}^*(\one-\sigma_k)\right]\epsmat,
 \label{eq:anomalous-whittaker}\\
 \check D_{-+}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{\ii\mu}{4\sqrt{x_1x_2}}\epsmat
 \left[W_{+1}W_{-2}^*(\one+\sigma_k)
       -W_{-1}W_{+2}^*(\one-\sigma_k)\right].
 \label{eq:conjugate-whittaker}
\end{align}
The rows and columns carry the indices in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:aoki-indexed-propagators}. These are exact propagators
of the rescaled field and contain no $a^{-3/2}$ endpoint factors.

\paragraph{The four SK components.}
Fermionic contour ordering gives
\begin{align}
 D_{+-\,\alpha\dot\beta}
 &=-\langle\psi^\dagger_{\dot\beta}(2)\psi_\alpha(1)\rangle',
 \nonumber\\
 \widehat D_{+-\,\alpha\beta}
 &=-\langle\psi_\beta(2)\psi_\alpha(1)\rangle',
 \nonumber\\
 \check D_{+-\,\dot\alpha\dot\beta}
 &=-\langle\psi^\dagger_{\dot\beta}(2)
                 \psi^\dagger_{\dot\alpha}(1)\rangle'.
 \label{eq:lesser-definitions}
\end{align}
Their explicit forms are
\begin{align}
 D_{+-}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=-\sum_s v_{s2}v_{s1}^*P_{-s}(-\hat{\bk})
 \nonumber\\
 &=-\frac{W_{+1}^*W_{+2}(\one+\sigma_k)
       +\mu^2W_{-1}^*W_{-2}(\one-\sigma_k)}{4\sqrt{x_1x_2}},
 \label{eq:normal-lesser-whittaker}\\
 \widehat D_{+-}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{\ii\mu}{4\sqrt{x_1x_2}}
 \left[W_{+1}^*W_{-2}(\one+\sigma_k)
       -W_{-1}^*W_{+2}(\one-\sigma_k)\right]\epsmat,
 \label{eq:anomalous-lesser-whittaker}\\
 \check D_{+-}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{\ii\mu}{4\sqrt{x_1x_2}}\epsmat
 \left[W_{-1}^*W_{+2}(\one+\sigma_k)
       -W_{+1}^*W_{-2}(\one-\sigma_k)\right].
 \label{eq:conjugate-lesser-whittaker}
\end{align}
Equivalently, the anomalous components obey
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D_{+-}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 =-\mathcal D_{-+}^{T}(-\bk;\tau_2,\tau_1),
 \qquad \mathcal D=\widehat D,\check D.
 \label{eq:anomalous-reversal}
\end{equation}
The reversed momentum is essential;
$P_{-s}(-\hat{\bk})=P_s(\hat{\bk})$, as in Eq.~(D.46) of Ref.~\cite{you}.
For each of $\mathcal D=D,\widehat D,\check D$, all four components are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal D_{-+}
 &=\langle X(1)Y(2)\rangle',&
 \mathcal D_{+-}
 &=-\langle Y(2)X(1)\rangle',
 \nonumber\\
 \mathcal D_{++}
 &=\theta_{12}\mathcal D_{-+}
   +\theta_{21}\mathcal D_{+-},&
 \mathcal D_{--}
 &=\theta_{21}\mathcal D_{-+}
   +\theta_{12}\mathcal D_{+-},
 \label{eq:three-propagator-sk}
\end{align}
where $(X,Y)=(\psi,\psi^\dagger),(\psi,\psi)$, or
$(\psi^\dagger,\psi^\dagger)$, and
$\theta_{ij}=\theta(\tau_i-\tau_j)$. Spinor indices retain the
$X,Y$ order in every component.

\paragraph{Late-time expansion.}
Following Eqs.~(2.25)--(2.29) of Ref.~\cite{you}, let $d=\pm1$
label the powers $(-2k\tau)^{\ii d\mu}$.
At $x=-k\tau\ll1$, define
\begin{equation}
 b_d=e^{-\ii\pi/4+\pi d\mu/2}
       \frac{\mu\Gamma(-2\ii d\mu)}{\Gamma(1-\ii d\mu)}.
 \label{eq:late-mode-coefficient}
\end{equation}
The leading radial modes are
\begin{align}
 u_+^L=v_-^L&=\sum_{d=\pm1}b_d(2x)^{\ii d\mu},&
 u_-^L=v_+^L&=\sum_{d=\pm1}d\,b_d(2x)^{\ii d\mu}.
 \label{eq:late}
\end{align}
Each branch has corrections starting at order $x$.
Products of these modes contain terms proportional to
$(\tau_1/\tau_2)^{\ii d\mu}$ and terms proportional to
$(k^2\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii d\mu}$. We denote the latter nonlocal
branches by $(\NL),d$, as in Ref.~\cite{you}.
A useful zero-chemical-potential form of their coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 c_d=\frac{2^{-1-2\ii d\mu}}{\pi}
           \Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii d\mu)^2,\qquad
 c\equiv c_+,\qquad c_-=c^*,\qquad |c|=\frac1{2\cosh\pi\mu}.
 \label{eq:late-coefficient}
\end{equation}
The Gamma duplication formula gives the direct normalization map
to Eq.~(2.28) of Ref.~\cite{you}:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{c_d}{2}(k^2\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii d\mu}
 =\frac{\Gamma(-2\ii d\mu)^2}{\Gamma(-\ii d\mu)^2}
       (4k^2\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii d\mu}.
 \label{eq:you-soft-coefficient}
\end{equation}
In particular, the three radial products are
\begin{align}
 [u_{s1}u_{s2}^*]_{(\NL),d}
 &=-\frac{s c_d}{2}(k^2\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii d\mu},
 \nonumber\\
 [u_{s1}v_{s2}^*]_{(\NL),d}
 &=\frac{s d c_d}{2}(k^2\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii d\mu},
 \nonumber\\
 [v_{s1}u_{s2}^*]_{(\NL),d}
 &=-\frac{s d c_d}{2}(k^2\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii d\mu}.
 \label{eq:late-radial-products}
\end{align}
For a soft propagator momentum $\bk_s$, with $k_s=|\bk_s|$ and
$|k_s\tau_1|,|k_s\tau_2|\ll1$, the leading and subleading nonanalytic
terms are
\begin{align}
 &\big[D^{\mathrm L\overline{\mathrm L}}_{ab\,\alpha\dot\beta}
      (\bk_s;\tau_1,\tau_2)\big]_{\NL}
 =\big[D_{ab\,\alpha\dot\beta}(\bk_s;\tau_1,\tau_2)\big]_{\NL}
 \nonumber\\
 &=-\sum_{d=\pm1}
 \frac{2^{-2-2\ii d\mu}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii d\mu)^2}{\pi}
 (\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii d\mu}
 \left[
 k_s^{2\ii d\mu}\bs\cdot\hat{\bk}_s
 +\frac{\ii(\tau_1-\tau_2)}{1+2\ii d\mu}
 k_s^{1+2\ii d\mu}\one
 \right]_{\alpha\dot\beta}
 +\cdots,
 \label{eq:weyl-soft-leading}\\
 &\big[\widehat D_{ab\,\alpha\beta}(\bk_s;\tau_1,\tau_2)\big]_{\NL}
 \nonumber\\
 &=-\sum_{d=\pm1}
 \frac{d\,2^{-2-2\ii d\mu}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii d\mu)^2}{\pi}
 (\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii d\mu}
 \left[
 k_s^{2\ii d\mu}(\bs\cdot\hat{\bk}_s)\epsmat
 +\frac{\ii(\tau_1+\tau_2)}{1+2\ii d\mu}
 k_s^{1+2\ii d\mu}\epsmat
 \right]_{\alpha\beta}
 +\cdots,
 \label{eq:hat-soft-expansion}\\
 &\big[\check D_{ab\,\dot\alpha\dot\beta}(\bk_s;\tau_1,\tau_2)\big]_{\NL}
 \nonumber\\
 &=-\sum_{d=\pm1}
 \frac{d\,2^{-2-2\ii d\mu}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii d\mu)^2}{\pi}
 (\tau_1\tau_2)^{\ii d\mu}
 \left[
 k_s^{2\ii d\mu}\epsmat(\bs\cdot\hat{\bk}_s)
 -\frac{\ii(\tau_1+\tau_2)}{1+2\ii d\mu}
 k_s^{1+2\ii d\mu}\epsmat
 \right]_{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}
 +\cdots.
 \label{eq:check-soft-expansion}
\end{align}
The two displayed terms scale as $k_s^{2\ii d\mu}$ and
$k_s^{1+2\ii d\mu}$; the omitted terms start at
$k_s^{2+2\ii d\mu}$ at fixed endpoint times.
Here $\epsmat=\ii\sigma^2$, and the identity and Pauli matrices carry
the displayed spinor indices. These nonlocal terms are the same for
all four SK assignments $ab$, following the cutting prescription of
Ref.~\cite{you}, Eq.~(3.13) and Appendix~D.3.
In the box diagram, these expressions are applied to the two soft-line
momenta $\bq$ and $\bm K=\bq+\bk_s$ separately.

\paragraph{Yukawa vertices.}
There are two Yukawa vertices, $\delta h\psi\psi$
and $\delta h\psi^\dagger\psi^\dagger$.
Their spin tensors, labelled $\mathrm L$ and $\overline{\mathrm L}$ respectively, are
\begin{equation}
 J_{\mathrm L}^{\alpha\beta}=-\epsilon^{\alpha\beta},\qquad
 J_{\overline{\mathrm L}}^{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}
 =+\epsilon^{\dot\alpha\dot\beta},\qquad
 J_{\mathrm L}=\epsmat,\quad J_{\overline{\mathrm L}}=-\epsmat.
 \label{eq:vertex-tensors}
\end{equation}
On SK branch $a=\pm1$ their amputated rules are
$-\ii a g_t a(\tau)J_r$. The two identical fermion slots cancel the
$1/2$ in the interaction. The equivalent calculation with unamputated
bilinears and explicit Wick pairings is given in Section~\ref{sec:wick}.

\section{The four-point box and its soft cut}
\subsection{Vertex types and the connected loop}
We label the two field types by $r,s\in\{\mathrm L,\overline{\mathrm L}\}$,
where $\mathrm L$ denotes $\psi$ and $\overline{\mathrm L}$ denotes
$\psi^\dagger$. The propagator used in the following type sums is
\begin{equation}
 \left(D^{rs}_{ab}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)\right)_{r,s}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \widehat D_{ab}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &D_{ab}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)\\
 -D_{ba}^{T}(-\bk;\tau_2,\tau_1)
 &\check D_{ab}(\bk;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:field-type-dictionary}
\end{equation}
Each entry is a $2\times2$ spin matrix with both indices lowered.
The lower-left entry follows from fermionic exchange; it is not a
fourth independent propagator. Field-type labels $r,s$, SK labels
$a,b=+,-$, and nonlocal branch labels $d=\pm1$ are distinct.

Each vertex can be of either Yukawa type. For a fixed cycle the 16 choices
are grouped as
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|ccccc}
$(\delta h\psi\psi,
\delta h\psi^\dagger\psi^\dagger)$ vertices
 & $(4,0)$ & $(3,1)$ & $(2,2)$ & $(1,3)$ & $(0,4)$\\\hline
number of type assignments & 1 & 4 & 6 & 4 & 1
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
We keep all of them. For a cycle $i\to j\to k\to l\to i$ their sum is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_{ijkl}=\sum_{r_i,r_j,r_k,r_l\in\{\mathrm L,\overline{\mathrm L}\}}
 \tr_2[J_{r_i}D^{r_i r_j}_{ij}J_{r_j}D^{r_j r_k}_{jk}
 J_{r_k}D^{r_k r_l}_{kl}J_{r_l}D^{r_l r_i}_{li}].
 \label{eq:weyl-box-trace}
\end{equation}
The all-$\mathrm L$ term contains four anomalous propagators;
mixed vertex assignments contain both normal and anomalous propagators.
The matrix contraction is explained below Eq.~\eqref{eq:vertex-rule}.

\subsection{Wick contractions and Majorana counting}
\label{sec:wick}
The two unamputated interaction insertions are
\begin{align}
 &-\frac{\ii a g_ta(\tau)}2\delta h\,
 (-\epsilon^{\alpha\beta})
 \psi_\alpha\psi_\beta,
 \nonumber\\
 &-\frac{\ii a g_ta(\tau)}2\delta h\,
 \epsilon^{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}
 \psi^\dagger_{\dot\alpha}\psi^\dagger_{\dot\beta}.
 \label{eq:vertex-rule}
\end{align}
For a fixed choice of vertex types around $i\to j\to k\to l\to i$,
write $J_i=J_{r_i}$ and $D_{ij}=D^{r_i r_j}_{ij}$
in this paragraph. Each matrix product contracts one spinor index:
\[
 (J_iD_{ij})^\alpha{}_{\beta}
 =\sum_{\gamma=1}^2(J_i)^{\alpha\gamma}(D_{ij})_{\gamma\beta}.
\]
The indices are dotted or undotted according to the field at each
endpoint. Multiplying the four segments and closing the last index
with the first gives
\begin{equation}
 \tr_2\!\left[(J_iD_{ij})(J_jD_{jk})(J_kD_{kl})(J_lD_{li})\right].
 \label{eq:weyl-components}
\end{equation}
Here $\tr_2$ sums only the two spinor components. The separate sum
over the 16 vertex-type choices gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:weyl-box-trace},
including both normal and anomalous lines. This contraction assigns
no conserved Dirac arrow to a Majorana line.
At fourth order expand
$\exp[-\ii g_t\sum_a a\int a(\tau)\delta h\,
(\psi\psi+\mathrm{h.c.})/2]$.
The $4!$ attachments of the labelled external Higgs fields cancel
$1/4!$. Retain the four bilinear $1/2$ factors for the following count.
Of the $7!!=105$ pairings of eight fermion slots, precisely 48 are
connected without self-contractions. They form three undirected cycles,
with $2^4$ choices of which slot at each vertex connects to its next
neighbor. Swapping these slots changes the Wick sign and the
antisymmetric $J$ sign together, so all 16 terms for a given cycle
are equal. The remaining closed-cycle Wick permutation gives a minus
sign. Thus $2^4/2^4=1$ for each \emph{undirected} Majorana cycle,
or $1/2$ for each of the six written orientations. This count is made
before referring to a Dirac trace. A Pfaffian gives the same factor
through $\log\operatorname{Pf}=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\log$.


With all external momenta incoming, take the vector routing
\begin{equation}
 p_{ij}=q+k_i,\quad p_{jk}=q+k_i+k_j,\quad
 p_{kl}=q-k_l,\quad p_{li}=q,
 \label{eq:routing}
\end{equation}
Each $D_{uv}$ then carries
$(p_{uv};\tau_u,\tau_v;a_u,a_v)$.
Write $\G=\langle\delta h_{\bk_1}\delta h_{\bk_2}
\delta h_{\bk_3}\delta h_{\bk_4}\rangle'_{\rm conn}$.
For one Majorana copy and one color, and for the full top loop, respectively,
\begin{align}
 \G^{(1)}={}&-\frac{g_t^4}{2}
 \sum_{(1,j,k,l)}\sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_4=\pm}
 \left(\prod_{v=1}^4a_v\right)
 \int_{-\infty}^0\prod_{v=1}^4
 [d\tau_v\,a(\tau_v)G_{a_v}(k_v;\tau_v)]
 \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\mathcal T_{1jkl},\nonumber\\
 \G^{\rm top}&=2N_c\G^{(1)}.
 \label{eq:each-box}
\end{align}
The cycle sum runs over permutations of $(j,k,l)=(2,3,4)$.
The minus sign is the closed-fermion-loop sign. The Majorana factor
$1/2$ follows from 48 connected Wick pairings: three undirected cycles
with 16 slot assignments each. The external Higgs attachments cancel
$1/4!$, as derived above. The 16 vertex types and the six cycle
orientations are separate sums.

\subsection{Left subgraph and the zeroth-order cancellation}
Let $\bm p=\bm k_L+\bm\ell$ and $\sigma_p=\bs\cdot\hat{\bm p}$.
Expanding the endpoint matrices in Eq.~\eqref{eq:soft-endpoints-first},
the four terms in the left type sum are
\begin{align}
 2V_L={}&
 \left(\one+\frac{\ii\tau_1\bs\cdot\bq}{1+2\ii\mu}\right)

 \left(\one+\frac{\ii\tau_2\bs\cdot\bm K}{1+2\ii\mu}\right)
 \nonumber\\
 &+\left(\one+\frac{\ii\tau_1\bs\cdot\bq}{1+2\ii\mu}\right)

 \left(\one-\frac{\ii\tau_2\bs\cdot\bm K}{1+2\ii\mu}\right)
 \nonumber\\
 &+\left(\one-\frac{\ii\tau_1\bs\cdot\bq}{1+2\ii\mu}\right)\epsmat
 D^{\overline{\mathrm L}\mathrm L}\epsmat
 \left(\one+\frac{\ii\tau_2\bs\cdot\bm K}{1+2\ii\mu}\right)

 \left(\one-\frac{\ii\tau_2\bs\cdot\bm K}{1+2\ii\mu}\right)
 +O(q^2,K^2,qK).
 \label{eq:hard-four-types}
\end{align}
This equation is used through first order in the soft momenta;
all four hard propagators carry the arguments in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:weyl-hard-block}.

After this type sum, only scalar products of the radial functions remain.
We denote the three needed scalar time kernels by
$\mathcal W_{GF},\mathcal W_{FF},\mathcal W_{GG}$:
\begin{center}

 & $\mathcal W_{GF}$ & $\mathcal W_{FF}$ & $\mathcal W_{GG}$\\\hline
$-+$ & $G_1F_2^*$ & $F_1F_2^*$ & $G_1G_2^*$\\
$+-$ & $F_1^*G_2$ & $-G_1^*G_2$ & $-F_1^*F_2$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Their diagonal SK components follow Eq.~\eqref{eq:three-propagator-sk}.
In sums such as $\sum_{a,b=\pm}ab$, the signs are the numerical

The labels $GF,FF,GG$ name scalar mode products, not field types.
In particular, these kernels are coefficients obtained by performing
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-four-types}, not propagators in a rotated spinor basis.
For example, at zeroth order its $-+$ component is

 V_L^{(0),-+}
 =\frac12\sum_s P_s
 (u_{s1}-v_{s1})(u_{s2}^*+v_{s2}^*)
 =-G_1F_2^*\sigma_p.
 \label{eq:hard-zeroth-weyl}
\end{equation}
The identity follows by substituting the radial dictionary, with
$\sum_s sP_s=\sigma_p$.
Thus $V_L^{(0)}=-\mathcal W_{GF}\bs\cdot\bm n_L$ and
$V_R^{(0)}=-\mathcal W_{GF}\bs\cdot\bm n_R$.
At this order $k_1=k_2=k_L$, $k_3=k_4=k_R$. After relabelling dummy
times and SK indices, the two orderings at either end have identical

\begin{equation*}
 \sum_{\varepsilon_L,\varepsilon_R=\pm1}
 \varepsilon_L\varepsilon_R\,
 \tr_2[(\bs\cdot\bm n_L)\sigma_K(\bs\cdot\bm n_R)\sigma_q]=0,
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\left.\G^{\rm box}_{s,\rm clock}\right|_{k_s^{3\pm4\ii\mu}}=0.}
 \label{eq:cancellation}
\end{equation}
This is an external-leg exchange cancellation. An individual spin trace
and an individual hard SK sum need not vanish. The late-time cancellation
between SK branches is a separate identity, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sk-endpoint-cancel}.

The first nonzero term requires one soft-momentum correction at each
hard end: both the hard momentum dependence and the first soft descendants
must be retained. A second-order correction at only one end multiplies
the vanishing zeroth-order sum at the other. Thus the leading surviving
clock term has two extra powers of $k_s$.

Set $x=-k_L\tau_1$, $y=-k_L\tau_2$, and $r=p/k_L$ for the hard momentum
$p=|\bm p|$. The scalar time integrals are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{XY}(\alpha,\beta;r)={}&\sum_{a,b=\pm}ab\int_0^\infty\dd x\dd y\,
 x^{\alpha-1}y^{\beta-1}(1+\ii ax)(1+\ii by)e^{-\ii ax-\ii by}
 \mathcal W_{XY;ab}(r;x,y),\nonumber\\*
 &\hspace{65mm}XY=GF,FF.
 \label{eq:hard-def}
\end{align}
The mode arguments are $rx,ry$. We use a common endpoint regulator and
combine all four SK components before taking its finite limit.
The exact hard-kernel reversal identities are
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal W_{GF;ab}(\tau_1,\tau_2)=\mathcal W_{GF;ba}(\tau_2,\tau_1),\qquad
 \mathcal W_{FF;ab}(\tau_1,\tau_2)=-\mathcal W_{GG;ba}(\tau_2,\tau_1).
 \label{eq:reverse}
\end{equation}
Expanding the four field-type contractions gives
\begin{equation}
 V_L=-\mathcal W_{GF}\sigma_p
 +\frac{\ii\tau_1}{1+2\ii\mu}\mathcal W_{FF}\bs\cdot\bq
 -\frac{\ii\tau_2}{1+2\ii\mu}\mathcal W_{GG}\bs\cdot\bm K
 +O(q^2,k_s^2).
 \label{eq:vertex-expand}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Hard momentum and endpoint descendants.}
The first-order hard contraction is Eq.~\eqref{eq:vertex-expand}.
The scalar kernels retain the full hard SK time dependence before expansion of the
momentum $\bm p=\bm k_L+\bm\ell$. The other ordering has
$\bm p=-\bm k_L+\bm\ell$. The derivative of the hard line and the descendants
of both soft endpoints must both be kept. Eq.~\eqref{eq:reverse} shows that the
first-order difference between the two external magnitudes gives zero in the
sum: the integrated $\mathcal W_{GF}$ kernel is symmetric in those energies. Also $\int\tau_2\mathcal W_{GG}=-\int\tau_1\mathcal W_{FF}$
at equal external magnitudes.

To evaluate the hard-momentum correction explicitly, write
$h(r)=\mathcal H_{GF}(\ii\mu,\ii\mu;r)$ and
$L=\bm n_L\cdot\bm\ell$. For the ordering $\varepsilon=\pm1$,
\begin{align}
 |\varepsilon k_L\bm n_L+\bm\ell|
 &=k_L+\varepsilon L+O(\ell^2/k_L),\\
 \bs\cdot\widehat{\varepsilon k_L\bm n_L+\bm\ell}
 &=\varepsilon\bs\cdot\bm n_L
 +\frac{\bs\cdot(\bm\ell-\bm n_LL)}{k_L}
 +O(\ell^2/k_L^2),\\
 h\!\left(\frac{|\varepsilon k_L\bm n_L+\bm\ell|}{k_L}\right)
 &=h(1)+\frac{\varepsilon L}{k_L}h'(1)
 +O(\ell^2/k_L^2).
 \label{eq:hard-taylor}
\end{align}
The $-\mathcal W_{GF}\sigma_p$ terms of the two orderings therefore add to
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{2}{k_L}\bs\cdot\big[
 h(1)\bm\ell+(h'(1)-h(1))\bm n_LL\big]
 +O(\ell^2/k_L^2).
 \label{eq:hard-derivative-step}
\end{equation}
The $\mathcal W_{FF}$ and $\mathcal W_{GG}$ descendants in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:vertex-expand} contribute
$-4\ii\mathcal H_{FF}(1+\ii\mu,\ii\mu;1)
\bs\cdot\bm\ell/[k_L(1+2\ii\mu)]$.
The common factor $k_L^{-2\ii\mu}$ comes from
$(-\tau_1)^{\ii\mu}(-\tau_2)^{\ii\mu}$ after
$x=-k_L\tau_1$, $y=-k_L\tau_2$.
Finally, the external magnitudes are
$|\bm k_L\pm\bk_s/2|=k_L\pm\bm n_L\cdot\bk_s/2+O(k_s^2/k_L)$.
Their first-order shifts cancel under the two orderings by the
time-reversal identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:reverse}; equal external energies
are imposed only after this sum.

 \mathcal V_L&=-2k_L^{-1-2\ii\mu}\bs\cdot
 \big[\mathcal P_1(\mu)\bm\ell+

 \mathcal P_1(\mu)&=\mathcal H_{GF}(\ii\mu,\ii\mu;1)
 +\frac{2\ii}{1+2\ii\mu}\mathcal H_{FF}(1+\ii\mu,\ii\mu;1),\\
 \mathcal P_2(\mu)&=\left.(\partial_r-1)\mathcal H_{GF}(\ii\mu,\ii\mu;r)\right|_{r=1}.
 \label{eq:hard-result}

The $\mathcal H_{FF}$ term comes from the soft descendants; the

\subsection{Scalar time integrals}
\label{sec:seed-eval}
\paragraph{Relation to the published Hankel scalar seed.}
The hard time integrals can also be reduced to the scalar seed of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}, provided its mass index is analytically continued.
This gives a direct route to the literature's single- and double-folded
formulae. Set
\begin{equation}
 \nu_h=\mu-\frac{\ii}{2},\qquad \rho=\ii\nu_h=\frac12+\ii\mu,
 \qquad x=-k_L\tau_1,\quad y=-k_L\tau_2,\quad r=p/k_L.
\end{equation}
Define a dimensionless scalar SK kernel $S_{ab}^{(\nu_h)}$ from the two
Wightman functions
\begin{align}
 S_{-+}(r;x,y)&=\frac\pi4 e^{-\pi\nu_h}(xy)^{3/2}
 H^{(1)}_{\rho}(rx)H^{(2)}_{-\rho}(ry),\\
 S_{+-}(r;x,y)&=\frac\pi4 e^{-\pi\nu_h}(xy)^{3/2}
 H^{(2)}_{-\rho}(rx)H^{(1)}_{\rho}(ry),
 \label{eq:scalar-seed-kernel}
\end{align}
with the standard SK time ordering.
Using $H_{-\rho}^{(2)}=e^{-\ii\pi\rho}H_\rho^{(2)}$ and
the mode prefactors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:modes}, one finds
the \emph{exact} radial identities, for every branch pair,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal W_{GF;ab}(r;x,y)
 =\frac{r}{xy}S_{ab}^{(\nu_h)}(r;x,y),\qquad
 \mathcal W_{FF;ab}(r;x,y)
 =\frac1r\left(-\ii\partial_x+\frac\mu x\right)
 \mathcal W_{GF;ab}(r;x,y).
 \label{eq:fermion-scalar-seed-map}
\end{equation}
The $1/r$ in the second identity follows from differentiating a mode
with argument $rx$. Differentiating the SK theta functions introduces
no delta-function term: the two $\mathcal W_{GF}$ Wightman functions agree
at $x=y$. Although $\nu_h$ is complex, Eq.~\eqref{eq:scalar-seed-kernel}
is an analytic scalar kernel; no physical scalar with complex mass is
being introduced.

To see precisely which published seeds are needed, let
\begin{equation}
 J^{p,q}_{ab}(r)=\int_0^\infty\dd x\dd y\,
 x^p y^q e^{-\ii ax-\ii by}S_{ab}^{(\nu_h)}(r;x,y).
\end{equation}
In the units $H=1$ of Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}, its branch-resolved Hankel
seed $\mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}$ obeys the exact normalization map
\begin{equation}
 J^{p,q}_{ab}(r)=-ab\,r^{-5-p-q}
 \mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}(u,u),\qquad
 u=\frac{2r}{1+r}.
 \label{eq:seed-normalization}
\end{equation}
The reference includes $-ab$ in the seed definition; neither that sign
nor the power of $r$ may be omitted. For
$p=\alpha-2$, $q=\beta-2$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-def} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{GF}(\alpha,\beta;r)
 =r\sum_{a,b=\pm}ab\big[
 J^{p,q}_{ab}+\ii aJ^{p+1,q}_{ab}
 +\ii bJ^{p,q+1}_{ab}-abJ^{p+1,q+1}_{ab}\big].
 \label{eq:published-seed-map}
\end{equation}
The four terms correspond to the integer time-power shifts generated by
$(1+\ii ax)(1+\ii by)$. The SK indices stay explicit until their
$a,b$ weights are combined.

The remaining hard coefficient can also be expressed without a new
time integral. Put $\mathscr S_{ab}^{p,q}=\mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}(1,1)$,
$p_0=-2+\ii\mu+\delta$, and use one common endpoint regulator
$\delta$ in all seed powers. Equation~\eqref{eq:published-seed-map} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{GF}(\ii\mu+\delta,\ii\mu+\delta;1)
 =-\sum_{a,b=\pm}\sum_{j,l=0}^1
 (\ii a)^j(\ii b)^l\mathscr S_{ab}^{p_0+j,p_0+l}.
 \label{eq:H21-literature}
\end{equation}
Integrating the second relation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fermion-scalar-seed-map} by parts yields
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{FF}(1+\ii\mu+\delta,\ii\mu+\delta;1)
 ={}&\ii\delta\,
 \mathcal H_{GF}(\ii\mu+\delta,\ii\mu+\delta;1)\nonumber\\
 &-\ii\sum_{a,b=\pm}\left[
 \mathscr S_{ab}^{\ii\mu+\delta,p_0}
 +\ii b\,\mathscr S_{ab}^{\ii\mu+\delta,p_0+1}\right].
 \label{eq:H11-literature}
\end{align}
The $\ii\delta\mathcal H_{GF}$ term is retained at finite regulator;
the complete $\mathcal H_{GF}$ will be finite, so this term vanishes
in the final limit. Individual SK terms do have regulator poles.
The integration-by-parts boundary term at
$x=0$ cancels only in the complete SK sum, since for $x<y$ the two
branches with the same $b$ have the same Wightman function.

The hard-momentum derivative in $\mathcal P_2$ follows from the
published scalar bootstrap equation without differentiating a time
integral. At $u_1=u_2=1$, the coefficient of its second derivative
vanishes. Define
\begin{align}
 T_{ab}^{p,q}&=\delta_{ab}\,
 e^{-\ii a\pi(p+q)/2}2^{-5-p-q}\Gamma(5+p+q),\\
 \mathscr D_{ab}^{p,q}&=\frac12\sum_{w=p,q}
 \frac{\nu_h^2+(w+5/2)^2}{w+3}\,\mathscr S_{ab}^{p,q}
 -\frac12\left(\frac1{p+3}+\frac1{q+3}\right)T_{ab}^{p,q}.
 \label{eq:seed-bootstrap-derivative}
\end{align}
Since $\dd u/\dd r|_{r=1}=1/2$, $\mathscr D_{ab}^{p,q}$ is precisely
$\dd\mathscr I_{ab}^{p,q}(u(r),u(r))/\dd r|_{r=1}$.
Combining this with the explicit $r^{-5-p-q}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed-normalization} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_2=-\FP_{\delta\to0}
 \sum_{a,b=\pm}\sum_{j,l=0}^1(\ii a)^j(\ii b)^l
 \left[\mathscr D_{ab}^{p_0+j,p_0+l}
 -(5+2p_0+j+l)\mathscr S_{ab}^{p_0+j,p_0+l}\right].
 \label{eq:P2-literature}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:H21-literature}--\eqref{eq:P2-literature}, with
$\mathcal P_1$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}, complete both hard
subgraphs in terms of the scalar seed values of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}; the right subgraph uses the same two functions.
\paragraph{Regularization and a finite evaluation.}
There is an essential normalization in Eq.~(92) of
Ref.~\cite{qin-seed}: its hypergeometric function is regularized,
\begin{equation}
 {}_3F_2^{\mathrm{reg}}(a,b,c;d,e;1)
 =\frac{{}_3F_2(a,b,c;d,e;1)}{\Gamma(d)\Gamma(e)}.
 \label{eq:regularized-hyper}
\end{equation}
As an independent normalization
check, the conformal scalar $\nu_h=-\ii/2$ has
$S_{-+}(1;x,y)=xy\,e^{\ii(x-y)}/2$.
Direct elementary time integration gives
$\mathscr S_{++}^{0,0}=\mathscr S_{--}^{0,0}=-3/16$ and
$\mathscr S_{+-}^{0,0}=\mathscr S_{-+}^{0,0}=1/32$; the implementation reproduces these values and their unequal-power generalization.

The scalar kernel is invariant under $\nu_h\to-\nu_h$, by the
Hankel order-reflection identities. In each equal-SK seed choose the
sign of $\nu_h$ so that $a\ii\nu_h=1/2+\ii\mu$. Its ordinary
${}_3F_2(1)$ then has convergence balance $1+\ii\mu$, whose real
part is positive. This is an exact change of representation and needs
no additional mass-index regulator.

For this subsection only set $z=\ii\mu$, and define the single symmetric
family
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Q_{jl}=\frac{2^{-1-2z-j-l}\Gamma(1+2z+j+l)}
 {(1+2z+j)(1+2z+l)}
 {}_3F_2\!\left(\begin{matrix}1+2z+j+l,\ 1+z,\ 1\\
 2+2z+j,\ 2+2z+l\end{matrix};1\right).
 \label{eq:finite-Q}
\end{equation}
Only $\mathcal Q_{00},\mathcal Q_{01},\mathcal Q_{11},
\mathcal Q_{02},\mathcal Q_{12}$ are needed. Also abbreviate the
finite endpoint contribution by
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{\mathrm{end}}=\pi^2 2^{-1-2z}
 \frac{\Gamma(1+2z)^2}{\Gamma(1+z)^2}.
 \label{eq:endpoint-finite}
\end{equation}
The fully summed hard seeds and momentum derivative are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{GF}(z,z;1)
 &=2\cosh(\pi\mu)(\mathcal Q_{00}+2\mathcal Q_{01}+\mathcal Q_{11})
 -\mathcal H_{\mathrm{end}},\nonumber\\
 \mathcal H_{FF}(1+z,z;1)
 &=-2\ii\cosh(\pi\mu)(\mathcal Q_{02}+\mathcal Q_{12}),
 \label{eq:finite-hard-seeds}\\
 \mathcal P_2
 &=2\cosh(\pi\mu)\sum_{j,l=0}^1
 \left[\frac{j(1+2z+j)}{2(1+z+j)}
 +\frac{l(1+2z+l)}{2(1+z+l)}-(1+2z+j+l)\right]\mathcal Q_{jl}
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad +(1+2z)\mathcal H_{\mathrm{end}}
 +2^{-1-2z}\cosh(\pi\mu)\Gamma(1+2z)
 \frac{4+5z+2z^2}{1+z}.
 \label{eq:finite-P2}
\end{align}
Together with $\mathcal P_1$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}, these are
finite, convergent special-function expressions for the complete hard
coefficients. No raw mode-power-series truncation or numerical
regulator extrapolation is required for the oscillatory signal.

Here is the endpoint cancellation leading to these expressions.
For $p_j=-2+z+\delta+j$, write the Gamma product in the opposite-SK
seed as
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C^{\Gamma}_{jl}(\delta)=
 \frac{\Gamma(\delta+j)\Gamma(1+2z+\delta+j)
       \Gamma(\delta+l)\Gamma(1+2z+\delta+l)}
 {\Gamma(1+z+\delta+j)\Gamma(1+z+\delta+l)}.
\end{equation}
After the external-mode factors $(\ii a)^j(\ii b)^l$ and the SK
branches are summed, its contribution to $\mathcal H_{GF}$ is
$2\,2^{-1-2z-2\delta-j-l}[\cos(\pi\delta)-1]\mathcal C^{\Gamma}_{jl}(\delta)$.
Only $j=l=0$ has a double pole; therefore
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\delta\to0}2[\cos(\pi\delta)-1]\mathcal C^{\Gamma}_{00}(\delta)
 =-\pi^2\frac{\Gamma(1+2z)^2}{\Gamma(1+z)^2}.
\end{equation}
This is precisely $-\mathcal H_{\mathrm{end}}$ after restoring its power of two.
Discarding the poles branch by branch would lose this finite term.
The remaining regularized-hypergeometric pieces give
$2\cosh(\pi\mu)\mathcal Q_{jl}$.
The integration-by-parts insertion in Eq.~\eqref{eq:H11-literature}
replaces $j=0,1$ by $j=2$ and contributes $-\ii$ times the same
pieces; it has no double-pole remainder.
Finally, substituting $p_j$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed-bootstrap-derivative} gives the bracket in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-P2}. The last term in that equation is the
summed bootstrap source, and $(1+2z)\mathcal H_{\mathrm{end}}$ is the derivative's
endpoint remainder. These steps preserve all factors of $r$.

The late-time cancellation can also be seen before integration.
For either scalar kernel $\mathcal W$, with
$\mathcal K_a(x)=(1+\ii ax)e^{-\ii ax}$, the SK sum for $x<y$ is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{a,b}ab \mathcal K_a(x)\mathcal K_b(y)\mathcal W_{ab}(x,y)
 =[\mathcal K_+(x)-\mathcal K_-(x)]
 [\mathcal K_+(y)\mathcal W_{-+}(x,y)-\mathcal K_-(y)\mathcal W_{+-}(x,y)].
 \label{eq:sk-endpoint-cancel}
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal K_+(x)-\mathcal K_-(x)=-2\ii x^3/3+O(x^5)$, the common late-time
endpoint is integrable in the hard blocks used here. The early-time
BD prescription is still necessary; this identity does not justify
integrating undamped oscillations numerically on a truncated interval.

\subsection{Central momentum integral}
Choose the angle convention
\begin{align}
 \bm n_L&=(\sin\theta_1,0,\cos\theta_1),&
 \bm n_R&=(\sin\theta_3\cos\phi_3,\sin\theta_3\sin\phi_3,\cos\theta_3),&
 \bm n_s&=(0,0,1).
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
 \tr[(\bs\cdot\bm A)\sigma_K(\bs\cdot\bm B)\sigma_q]
 =2[(\bm A\cdot\hat K)(\bm B\cdot\hat q)
 +(\bm A\cdot\hat q)(\bm B\cdot\hat K)
 -(\bm A\cdot\bm B)(\hat K\cdot\hat q)].
 \label{eq:pauli}
\end{equation}
The spin trace of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pauli} leaves a polynomial numerator divided
by $qK$. To display it, write the two vectors appearing in the hard blocks as
\begin{equation}
 \bm v_L=\mathcal P_1\bm\ell+\mathcal P_2\bm n_L(\bm n_L\cdot\bm\ell),\qquad
 \bm v_R=\mathcal P_1\bm\ell+\mathcal P_2\bm n_R(\bm n_R\cdot\bm\ell).
\end{equation}
Since $\bm K=\bm\ell+\bk_s/2$ and $\bq=\bm\ell-\bk_s/2$, the scalar loop
integral after the spin contraction is
\begin{align}
 &2\int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3}(qK)^{2\ii\mu-1}
 \big[2(\bm v_L\cdot\bm\ell)(\bm v_R\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\tfrac12(\bm v_L\cdot\bk_s)(\bm v_R\cdot\bk_s)\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{55mm}-(\bm v_L\cdot\bm v_R)(\ell^2-k_s^2/4)\big].
 \label{eq:spin-bubble}
\end{align}
Its mass dimension is $5+4\ii\mu$. We evaluate it using the scalar convolution
\begin{align}
 F_0(A,B)&=\frac{\Gamma(A+B-\tfrac32)\Gamma(\tfrac32-A)\Gamma(\tfrac32-B)}
 {\Gamma(A)\Gamma(B)\Gamma(3-A-B)},\\
 \int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3}(q^2)^{-A}(K^2)^{-B}
 &=\frac{k_s^{3-2A-2B}}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}F_0(A,B).
 \label{eq:scalar-bubble}
\end{align}
For the scalar numerator this identity follows by Schwinger parameters
and a Gaussian momentum integral in the convergence domain
$\Re A,\Re B<3/2$, $\Re(A+B)>3/2$. The shifted polynomial moments
are defined by the same meromorphic continuation, with common regulator
parameters before recombination. The physical soft-region integrals have
UV power divergences when extended beyond $q\sim k_s$; this continuation
selects their nonanalytic coefficient. UV-dependent polynomial contact
terms belong to matching with the hard region and are excluded.
No physical UV cutoff is sent to infinity term by term.
The following reduction expresses the polynomial numerator as a finite
sum of these scalar convolutions. The result depends on the two hard
directions relative to $\bk_s$ as well as on their mutual angle.

\paragraph{Reduction of the polynomial numerator.}
\label{sec:bubble-reduction}
For the reduction only, measure $q,K,\ell$ in units of $k_s$, put
$u=q^2$, $v=K^2$, and set $\nu=1/2-\ii\mu$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \ell^2=(u+v)/2-1/4,\qquad
 \ell_\parallel\equiv\bm\ell\cdot\bm n_s=(v-u)/2,\qquad
 \ell_\perp^2=\ell^2-\ell_\parallel^2.
\end{equation}
Azimuthal averaging replaces two transverse components by
$\ell_\perp^2\delta^\perp_{ij}/2$ and four by
$\ell_\perp^4(\delta^\perp_{ij}\delta^\perp_{kl}
+\delta^\perp_{ik}\delta^\perp_{jl}+\delta^\perp_{il}\delta^\perp_{jk})/8$,
where $\delta^\perp_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-n_{s i}n_{s j}$.
The numerator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spin-bubble} consequently becomes a polynomial
in $u,v$ and the three external angles. Each monomial is integrated by
\begin{align}
 u^mv^n&\quad\longmapsto\quad
 \frac{F_0(\nu-m,\nu-n)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}},\\
 \frac{F_0(\nu-m,\nu-n)}{F_0(\nu,\nu)}&=
 \frac{(3/2-\nu)_m(3/2-\nu)_n(1-\nu)_m(1-\nu)_n}
 {(5/2-2\nu)_{m+n}(3-2\nu)_{m+n}}.
 \label{eq:bubble-reduction}
\end{align}
Only nonnegative integers $m,n$ with $m+n\leq4$ occur. Thus the bubble is a
finite Gamma sum, and all its terms reduce to the same $F_0(\nu,\nu)$ times
rational functions. For example the coefficient of $\mathcal P_1^2$ in the
square bracket of Eq.~\eqref{eq:spin-bubble} is
$\ell^4+\ell^2/4-\ell_\parallel^2/2$, whose integral is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{F_0(\nu,\nu)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}
 \frac{3(\nu-1)(2\nu-3)}{8(\nu-2)(4\nu-7)(4\nu-5)}.
\end{equation}
The $\mathcal P_1\mathcal P_2$ and $\mathcal P_2^2$ terms are evaluated by
the same two steps. Their fully reduced coefficients are displayed directly
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:compact-coefficients}, without introducing separate symbols
for each tensor moment.
The azimuthal average introduces only the five angle monomials
$1$, $c_1^2+c_3^2$, $c_1^2c_3^2$,
$\sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3$, and
$\sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3)$, where
$c_i=\cos\theta_i$. Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:bubble-reduction} to their
coefficients reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:compact-coefficients}.



\subsection{Total oscillatory trispectrum}
The leading nonzero oscillatory term, summed over the four $s$-cut boxes, is
\begin{align}
 \left.\langle\delta h_{\bk_1}\delta h_{\bk_2}\delta h_{\bk_3}\delta h_{\bk_4}\rangle'
 \right|_{s,\rm clock}
 ={}&-\frac{N_cg_t^4H^4 k_s^5}{16\,k_1^3k_2^3k_3^3k_4^3 k_Lk_R}\nonumber\\
 &\times\left[\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)^{2\ii\mu}
 \cC(\mu;\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)+\mathrm{c.c.}\right].
 \label{eq:final}
\end{align}
The angular dependence can be written as
\begin{align}
 \cC={}&A_1+A_2(\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_3)
 +A_3\cos^2\theta_1\cos^2\theta_3\nonumber\\
 &+B_1\sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3
 +D_1\sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3).
 \label{eq:angular-final}
\end{align}
All five coefficients share one soft-bubble normalization, including the two
soft-mode coefficients $c^2$, the factor $4$ from the two Bose-summed hard blocks, the factor $2$ from the Pauli trace, and the phase from the chosen momentum ratio:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B(\mu)=
 \frac{2^{1+4\ii\mu}\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii\mu)^4 F_0(\nu,\nu)}
 {32\pi^2(4\pi)^{3/2}(\nu-3)(\nu-2)(4\nu-7)(4\nu-5)},\qquad
 \nu=\tfrac12-\ii\mu.
 \label{eq:bubble-normalization}
\end{equation}
In particular, the natural hard scaling is
$(k_s^2/(k_Lk_R))^{2\ii\mu}$. Since
$4k_{12}k_{34}=16k_Lk_R$ at this order, using the displayed
ratio transfers $16^{2\ii\mu}$ into the coefficient. Thus
$8c^2\,16^{2\ii\mu}
=2^{1+4\ii\mu}\Gamma(1/2-\ii\mu)^4/\pi^2$, which fixes both
the phase and the overall factor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bubble-normalization}.
Combining the bubble with the two hard coefficients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-result}
gives the final mass dependence:
\begin{align}
 A_1&=\mathcal B(\nu-1)(2\nu-3)
 \big[4(\nu-3)(3\mathcal P_1^2+2\mathcal P_1\mathcal P_2)
 +(2\nu-5)\mathcal P_2^2\big],\\*
 A_2&=-\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\nu-1)(2\nu-3)^2,\qquad A_3=-3A_2,\\*
 B_1&=-2\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\nu-1)(\nu-2)(2\nu-3),\\*
 D_1&=\mathcal B\mathcal P_2^2(\nu-2)(2\nu-5)(2\nu-3).
 \label{eq:compact-coefficients}
\end{align}
Thus the answer requires only two hard functions $\mathcal P_1,\mathcal P_2$
and one explicit bubble factor $\mathcal B$. The coefficients are complex and
include the clock phase. The full answer is even under either Higgs-pair exchange
and under parity. Its engineering dimension is $-5$, as required for the primed
four-point function. The powers of $H$ follow from four rescaled Weyl Yukawa
vertices and four external Higgs contractions; no extra factor of $H$ or a
second color/orientation multiplicity is implicit. For fixed $\mu>0$ these are the leading double-soft nonanalytic
terms; higher soft powers and analytic contact terms are not displayed.
With the centered hard momenta the next clock terms begin at
$k_s^{7\pm4\ii\mu}$, and the next mixed terms at $k_s^5$.
Thus the generic relative error within each branch is
$O(k_s^2/k_L^2,k_s^2/k_R^2,k_s^2/(k_Lk_R))$; it is not a
relative-error bound at a zero of the displayed angular coefficient.
\section{Nonoscillatory contribution}
\label{sec:mixed}
\subsection{Left and right subgraphs}
Taking opposite soft branches gives a nonoscillatory $k_s^3$ term.
For $d=-1$, the leading terms in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:weyl-soft-leading}--\eqref{eq:check-soft-expansion}
are represented by
\[
 L_{\mathrm L}^-=\one,\qquad
 L_{\overline{\mathrm L}}^-=-\epsmat,\qquad
 U_{\mathrm L}^-=\epsmat,\qquad
 U_{\overline{\mathrm L}}^-=-\one,
\]
with scalar prefactor $+c^*/2$, compared with $-c/2$ for $d=+1$.
Use $U^-_r$ at one soft endpoint and $L^+_s$ at the other in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:weyl-hard-block}, and interchange $+,-$ at the right end.
The superscripts $-+$ and $+-$ below label the two nonlocal mass
branches at the endpoints; the SK labels remain on the hard propagator.
At zeroth order their explicit four-type sums give
\begin{align}
 V_L^{-+,(0)}&=-\frac12\sum_{r,s}U_r^-J_rD^{rs}J_sL_s^+
 =\mathcal W_{FF}\one,\nonumber\\
 V_R^{+-,(0)}&=-\frac12\sum_{r,s}U_r^+J_rD^{rs}J_sL_s^-
 =-\mathcal W_{GG}\one.
 \label{eq:mixed-weyl-hard}
\end{align}
The soft prefactor for this assignment is
$-|c|^2(q^2)^{-\ii\mu}(K^2)^{\ii\mu}$, from the opposite signs
of the two branch prefactors displayed above.
Its minus sign cancels the closed-loop sign in Eq.~\eqref{eq:each-box}.
The endpoint time powers are included when integrating these hard
matrices. Equation~\eqref{eq:reverse} makes both integrated hard
coefficients equal to the real seed
$\mathcal H_{FF}(-\ii\mu,+\ii\mu;1)$.
Each hard end therefore has a Bose-even
zeroth-order term. There are two soft assignments and two Higgs orderings
at each end, giving $2\times2\times2$ contributions; the spin trace
adds $2\hat q\cdot\hat K$. The soft coefficients give
$|c|^2=1/(4\cosh^2\pi\mu)$. These factors fix the normalization of
the mixed term independently of the oscillatory branch.
\subsection{The mixed hard coefficient}
For evaluation of the remaining mixed seed, the same radial identity
and integration by parts give the general formula
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{FF}(\alpha,\beta;1)
 ={}&[\ii(\alpha-1)+\mu]\mathcal H_{GF}(\alpha-1,\beta;1)
 \nonumber\\
 &-\ii\sum_{a,b=\pm}\left[
 \mathscr S_{ab}^{\alpha-1,\beta-2}
 +\ii b\,\mathscr S_{ab}^{\alpha-1,\beta-1}\right].
 \label{eq:mixed-seed-eval}
\end{align}
Set $\alpha=-\ii\mu+\delta$, $\beta=\ii\mu+\delta$, perform the
complete sum, and then take $\delta\to0$ using the corrected
regularized seeds. The conjugation relation
$\mathcal H_{FF}(\alpha,\beta;1)^*
=\mathcal H_{FF}(\beta^*,\alpha^*;1)$ proves the mixed seed is real.
The code evaluates this limit by fourth-order extrapolation, with
step-size and precision checks; the clock coefficients above instead
use the analytic limit.
For example,
$\mathcal H_{FF}(-0.73\ii,+0.73\ii;1)=-1.388500280198814$;
halving the extrapolation step changes this value by less than
$2\times10^{-15}$.


\subsection{Central integral and total trispectrum}
The scalar bubble in this case is
\begin{equation}
 \int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3}(q^2)^{-\ii\mu}(K^2)^{\ii\mu}
 \hat q\cdot\hat K
 =\frac{k_s^3}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\,
 \frac{2\sqrt\pi}{3}\mu(1+\mu^2)\coth(\pi\mu).
\end{equation}
After both soft branches and all four $s$-cut boxes are summed, the result is
\begin{equation}
 \left.\langle\delta h^4\rangle'\right|_{s,\mathrm{mixed}}
 =\frac{N_cg_t^4H^4 k_s^3}{16\prod_i k_i^3}
 \frac{2\mu(1+\mu^2)}{3\pi\sinh(2\pi\mu)}
 \big[\mathcal H_{FF}(-\ii\mu,+\ii\mu;1)\big]^2.
 \label{eq:mixed}
\end{equation}
It is independent of angles at this order. Equation~\eqref{eq:mixed} is the
leading nonoscillatory nonanalytic term, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:final} is the
leading mass-dependent clock signal. At $\mu=0$ the separated branches coalesce;
the limit must be taken after combining the relevant terms.
Here $k_s^3=(\bk_s^2)^{3/2}$ is nonanalytic as a function of the
soft momentum components, even though it has no mass-dependent oscillation.
It cannot be discarded as a polynomial contact term.
The remaining real coefficient is evaluated with the same scalar seed
through Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed-seed-eval}.
For example, $\mathcal H_{FF}(-0.73\ii,+0.73\ii;1)=-1.388500280198814$.

The results above include the leading oscillatory and nonoscillatory
nonanalytic terms for a single collapsed channel. Analytic contact terms,
overlapping collapsed channels and Higgs-to-curvature conversion are
outside the calculation. Section~\ref{sec:checks} specifies the numerical
checks and their limits.

\section{Numerical checks and scope}
\label{sec:checks}
The Whittaker implementation constructs all normal and anomalous
contractions directly from Eq.~\eqref{eq:mode-expansion}. It verifies
EOMs, normalization and the Hankel map at $\mu=0.2,0.73,2,5$.
The explicit Whittaker products in Section~1.3 are checked separately
in \texttt{checks/explicit\_}\allowbreak\texttt{propagator\_checks.py}: all three propagators
and all SK assignments agree with the mode contractions at four masses,
three momentum directions and three time pairs. An alternative Hankel
calculation uses explicit helicity spinors; the largest residual is
$3.1\times10^{-16}$. Conjugation and equal-time anticommutators are
checked as well. Raising special-function precision from 30 to 60 digits
leaves the resulting double-precision matrices unchanged. These are
free-propagator consistency checks, not an independent box integration.
The independent eight-slot Wick enumerator gives 48 connected pairings
and agrees with the cycle formula, and with half the Dirac result, for
all 16 SK assignments at $\mu=0.2,0.73,2$. Its test momenta are generic
edge momenta: this is a local contraction test, not a momentum integral.
The original-field implementation in \texttt{checks/aoki\_weyl\_checks.py}
also compares both soft branches with an independent Bessel decomposition
at $\mu=0.2,0.73,2,5$. It checks the four-type hard sums against Hankel
scalar products, both mixed assignments, and the factorized spin cut
for all 16 SK choices at the first three masses. The first-descendant
remainder decreases quadratically with the soft momentum. These tests
use the original $\psi,\psi^\dagger$ contractions.
The soft-expansion check retains the exact ${}_0F_1$ endpoints,
Whittaker hard lines, both external energy shifts and all four
orderings. On a finite time interval, at three masses and two geometries,
its error relative to the leading nonzero coefficient decreases by
approximately four when the soft momenta are halved. The nonzero
single-cycle zeroth-order trace and its exchange cancellation are
checked separately. Time-quadrature refinement is also tested.

A further independent calculation integrates the fermion Hankel kernels
on BD-rotated contours at the physical mass indices: the two same-SK
ordered triangles rotate together, and the two opposite-SK integrals
rotate separately. It checks both $FF$ and $GF$ scalar hard
integrals and the hard-momentum derivative at $\mu=0.2,0.73$, with a common positive endpoint regulator
$\delta=0.8$, including a mixed-branch case. The derivative is obtained directly from
the first-order mode equations, independently of the scalar bootstrap
source; the regulated $\mathcal P_1,\mathcal P_2$ are compared as well. This tests the scalar-seed
normalization beyond the conformal-scalar example. It does not directly
evaluate the zero-regulator limit; that limit still uses the analytically
summed seeds and regulator-path checks.

The retained scalar-seed tests check the elementary conformal-scalar
normalization, Gamma reductions, regulator paths and working precision.
Comparing the regulated and analytically summed versions of the same
seed is a consistency check, not a second derivation of that seed.
Neither finite-interval soft tests nor special-function agreement is
a direct numerical integration of the full unexpanded BD box.
The analytic contact part and its renormalization, overlapping collapsed
channels, evolving masses, nonzero chemical potential and curvature
conversion have not been computed. Reproducible commands and residuals
are recorded in \texttt{checks/top\_quark\_audit.md}.
