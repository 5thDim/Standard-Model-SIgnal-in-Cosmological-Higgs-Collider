\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\paragraph{Notations and conventions}
We work with the inflation patch of the dS spacetime with coordinates $x^\mu=(\tau,\bm x)$ and metric $\di s^2=(-\di\tau^2+\di\bm x^2)/\tau^2$, where $\tau\in(-\infty,0)$ and $\bm x\in\mathbb{R}^3$.
We denote a three-momentum by $\bm k$ and its magnitude by $k\equiv|\bm k|$.
We also use the condensed subscripts to denote summations, such as $k_{12}\equiv k_1+k_2$, $p_{12}\equiv p_1+p_2$, etc.
Our conventions for Euler $\Gamma$ products, the Pochhammer symbol, and hypergeometric functions are collected in App.~\ref{app:useful-functions}.
A prime on a momentum-space correlator, such as $\langle\zeta_{\bm k_1}\cdots\zeta_{\bm k_n}\rangle'$, indicates that the overall factor $(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\sum_{i=1}^n\bm k_i)$ has been removed.

\section{Overview of the Cosmological Higgs Collider}
\label{sec:chc-overview}
We begin with a brief introduction to the cosmological Higgs collider.
Further details can be found in \cite{lu_cosmological_2020}.

The cosmological Higgs collider is based on Higgs-modulated reheating.
We assume a typical standard slow-roll inflation scenario in this paper.
During inflation, the Standard Model Higgs acts as a light spectator field and develops fluctuations around its background value,
\begin{equation}
    h(t,\mb x)=h_0(t)+\delta h(t,\mb x).
    \label{eq:overview-higgs-split}
\end{equation}
After inflation, the inflaton decays and transfers its energy to matter and radiation.
Its decay rate can depend on the Higgs background through the masses of the decay products or through Higgs-dependent interactions.
The Higgs fluctuations then change the decay rate in different regions.
These regions reheat at different times and undergo different amounts of expansion.
In this way, the Higgs fluctuations contribute to the primordial curvature perturbation.

The conversion can be described by the local number of e-folds $N$ from a spatially flat slice at the end of inflation to a uniform-density slice after reheating.
Let $h$ denote the Higgs value on the initial slice.
Using the $\delta N$ formalism~\cite{lyth_inflationary_2005,sasaki_general_1996,starobinsky_dynamics_1982,starobinsky_multicomponent_1985,lyth_general_2005}, we write the Higgs contribution to the curvature perturbation as:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_h=N_h\delta h
    +\frac12N_{hh}\left(\delta h^2-\langle\delta h^2\rangle\right)
    +\cdots,
    \label{eq:overview-delta-n}
\end{equation}
where $N_h=\partial N/\partial h$ and $N_{hh}=\partial^2N/\partial h^2$.
These coefficients depend on the detailed dynamics of post-inflationary evolution and reheating.
At linear order in this conversion, the connected Higgs correlators give
\begin{equation}
    \left\langle\zeta_{\mb k_1}\cdots\zeta_{\mb k_n}\right\rangle_c'
    \supset N_h^n
    \left\langle\delta h_{\mb k_1}\cdots
    \delta h_{\mb k_n}\right\rangle_c'.
    \label{eq:overview-correlator-transfer}
\end{equation}
Thus, interactions with Higgs modes during inflation can leave characteristic signatures in primordial perturbations.
This gives us a direct motivation to compute the inflationary Higgs correlators.

We next summarize the Higgs dynamics during inflation~\cite{chen_mass_spectrum_2017,chen_standard_model_background_2017}.
Given that the inflationary Hubble scale $H$ is much larger than the electroweak scale, the Higgs potential is dominated by $V(h)\simeq\lambda h^4/4$.
We assume that the Higgs potential remains stable over the relevant field range, and take $\lambda$ to be positive and small at the inflation scale.
As Higgs modes leave the horizon, their fluctuations accumulate and the quartic interaction limits their growth.
The resulting variance and dynamical mass are
\begin{equation}
    \langle h^2\rangle\sim\frac{H^2}{\sqrt\lambda},\qquad
    m_H^2\simeq\sqrt{\frac{6\lambda}{\pi^3}}\,H^2,
    \label{eq:overview-higgs-mass}
\end{equation}
where the mass estimate follows from the Euclidean de Sitter zero-mode approximation.
For small $\lambda$, $m_H^2\ll H^2$ and the Higgs fluctuations have a nearly scale-invariant spectrum.
We therefore use massless scalar modes for the external Higgs legs.

Modes with wavelengths larger than the observable patch form an approximately homogeneous background $h_0$.
During inflation, its classical equation of motion is
\begin{equation}
    \ddot h_0+3H\dot h_0+\lambda h_0^3=0.
    \label{eq:overview-higgs-eom}
\end{equation}
The local potential curvature is $V''(h_0)=3\lambda h_0^2$.
For the typical background size $h_0^2\sim\langle h^2\rangle$, it satisfies $V''(h_0)/H^2\sim\sqrt\lambda\ll1$.
The background therefore admits a slow-roll solution in which the force from the quartic potential is balanced by Hubble friction.
We can neglect the second time derivative and obtain
\begin{equation}
    3H\dot h_0\simeq-\lambda h_0^3,\qquad
    \frac{|\dot h_0|}{H|h_0|}\simeq
    \frac{\lambda h_0^2}{3H^2}\sim\sqrt\lambda\ll1.
    \label{eq:overview-higgs-slow-roll}
\end{equation}
Thus $h_0$ changes slowly over a Hubble time.
Over an interval of $\Delta N$ e-folds with $\lambda h_0^2\Delta N/(3H^2)\ll1$, its fractional change remains small.
We treat $h_0$ as constant over this interval at leading order.

For the Standard Model gauge bosons and the top quark, the Higgs background gives the tree-level masses $m_{W,\mathrm{tree}}=g|h_0|/2$, $m_{Z,\mathrm{tree}}=m_{W,\mathrm{tree}}/c_W$, and $m_t=y_t|h_0|/\sqrt2$.
Here $g$ and $y_t$ are Standard Model gauge coupling and Yukawa coupling, respectively.
The infrared part of the Higgs loop also contributes to the $W$ and $Z$ masses,
\begin{equation}
    \Delta m_W^2\simeq\frac{3g^2H^4}{8\pi^2m_H^2},\qquad
    \Delta m_Z^2\simeq\frac{\Delta m_W^2}{c_W^2},
    \label{eq:overview-vector-mass-corrections}
\end{equation}
where $c_W=\cos\theta_W$.
These positive corrections are enhanced by $H^2/m_H^2$ and can dominate the gauge boson masses in the light-Higgs regime.

The Standard Model provides a natural starting point for this calculation.
Signals generated through additional inflaton interactions depend on the unknown couplings between the inflaton and other particles.
Introducing more such interactions adds more free parameters to the signal.
However, the Higgs already couples to the SM particles through known gauge and Yukawa interactions.
After accounting for the renormalization-group running of these couplings, the SM contributions provide a reference for calibrating the cosmological Higgs collider.
The SM particle interactions determine the Higgs correlators, whose signatures are transferred to the curvature perturbation through reheating.
We focus on the $W$ and $Z$ bosons and the top quark, which have the largest gauge and Yukawa couplings to the Higgs.

We first consider the three-point function $\langle\delta h^3\rangle$ following the setup of \cite{lu_cosmological_2020}.
We work in the de Sitter approximation, with constant masses, couplings, and Higgs background.
The $W$ and $Z$ interactions contain both $\delta h A_\mu A^\mu$ and $(\delta h)^2 A_\mu A^\mu$, where $A=W,Z$ schematically.
The top Yukawa interaction contains the $\delta h\bar t t$ vertex.
These interactions preserve all the de Sitter isometries.
For the three-point function, the momentum dependence of the full shape is completely fixed by the dimensions and spins of the operators at the future boundary, when all dS isometries are preserved~\cite{wang_interact_2025}.
In our case, there are three light Higgs modes at the future boundary, so no oscillatory signal will appear in the squeezed limit.

We now turn to the connected Higgs four-point function,
\begin{equation}
    \left\langle\delta h_{\mb k_1}\delta h_{\mb k_2}
    \delta h_{\mb k_3}\delta h_{\mb k_4}\right\rangle_c'.
    \label{eq:overview-four-point}
\end{equation}
Specifically, we focus on the leading nonlocal signal in the collapsed $s$ channel,
\begin{equation}
    \mb k_s=\mb k_1+\mb k_2=-(\mb k_3+\mb k_4),
    \qquad k_s\ll k_i.
    \label{eq:overview-collapsed-channel}
\end{equation}
Here the two pairs of external momenta exchange a small momentum $k_s$.
Given that the gauge bosons and top quark have SM charges, their leading contributions to the trispectrum appear at one loop level.
The nonlocal signal has a nonanalytic dependence on $k_s$.
For massive particles in the oscillatory regime, it contains oscillations in the logarithm of $\frac{k_s^2}{k_{12}k_{34}}$.
The oscillation frequency carries information about the particle mass in units of the inflationary Hubble scale $H$.
And the angular dependence of the signal carries information about the particle spin.
We extract the leading nonlocal contribution using the one-loop factorization theorem of \cite{qin_inflation_2023}.

In the following sections, we calculate the amplitude of this leading nonlocal signal and its dependence on the particle masses and Higgs couplings.
For the gauge bosons, we use the mass parameter $\tilde\nu=\sqrt{m_A^2/H^2-1/4}$ and the couplings $g$ for $W$ and $g/c_W$ for $Z$.
For the top quark, we use $\tilde\nu=m_t/H$ and the Yukawa coupling $y_t$.
We also analyze the characteristic angular partial-wave structures in the nonlocal signal, which depend on the spins and interaction structures of the particles in the loop.
These results provide the SM reference signals for calibrating the cosmological Higgs collider.

\section{Gauge Boson Signal}
\label{sec:gauge-boson}

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:gauge-preliminaries}
In this section, we compute the connected four-point function $\langle\delta h_{\mb k_1}\delta h_{\mb k_2} \delta h_{\mb k_3}\delta h_{\mb k_4}\rangle'$ generated by a massive gauge-boson loop.
We focus on the leading nonlocal signal in the collapsed $s$ channel.
In this limit, $\mb k_1 \simeq - \mb k_2$ and $\mb k_3 \simeq - \mb k_4$ at leading order.
We choose $\mb k_s$ along the $z$ axis and set the azimuth of $\mb k_1$ to zero.
Here $\theta_1$ and $\theta_3$ are the polar angles of $\mb k_1$ and $\mb k_3$, respectively, and $\phi_3$ is their relative azimuth.
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \mb k_1 &= k_1(\sin\theta_1,\,0,\,\cos\theta_1), \\
        \mb k_3 &= k_3(\sin\theta_3\cos\phi_3,\,\sin\theta_3\sin\phi_3,\,\cos\theta_3), \\
        \mb k_s &= k_s(0,\,0,\,1).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:gauge-momenta-cartesian}
\end{equation}

The Higgs--gauge couplings follow from the Higgs kinetic term \cite{lu_cosmological_2020}:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\sqrt{-g}}{2}|\mathrm D_\mu\mb H|^2
    \supset\frac{a^2g^2}{4}(h_0+\delta h)^2
    \left(W^+_\mu W^{-\mu}+\frac{Z_\mu Z^\mu}{2c_W^2}\right),
    \label{eq:higgs-gauge-couplings}
\end{equation}
where $g$ is the weak gauge coupling, $c_W=\cos\theta_W$, and the Lorentz contractions on the right use the flat conformal metric.
The terms linear and quadratic in $\delta h$ generate the cubic and quartic vertices, respectively.
These vertices generate the bubble diagram, the two triangle diagrams, and the box diagram shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:gauge-diagrams}.

For the gauge-boson calculation, we adopt the conventions of~\cite{qin_helical_2023}.
We use a constant vector mass $m_A$ and the parameter $\tilde\nu\equiv\sqrt{m_A^2/H^2-1/4}>0$ in the following.
Throughout this section, the $W^\pm$ contribution is obtained by multiplying the expressions by $2$, and the $Z$ contribution by making the replacement $g\to g/c_W$, with the corresponding mass used in each case.
We use $\lambda,\sigma=L,+,-,T$ for vector polarization-basis labels, and $\eta=\pm1$ for the two nonlocal branches.
The SK branch at vertex $v$ is denoted by $\aa_v=\pm1$, with $v=1,2,\ldots$.
Neglecting the Higgs mass in the external mode functions, we use the massless bulk-to-boundary propagator
\begin{equation}
    G_{\aa_v}(k;\tau)=\frac{H^2}{2k^3}
    (1-\ii\aa_v k\tau)e^{\ii\aa_v k\tau}.
    \label{eq:higgs-propagator}
\end{equation}

The gauge-boson propagator decomposes as
\begin{align}
    D_{\aa_1\aa_2,\mu\nu}(\mb k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    ={}&\sum_{\lambda=T,L,\pm}
    e^{(\lambda)}_{\mb k,\mu}e^{(\lambda)*}_{\mb k,\nu}
    D^{(\lambda)}_{\aa_1\aa_2}(k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    \nonumber\\
    &+e^{(T)}_{\mb k,\mu}e^{(L)*}_{\mb k,\nu}
    D^{(TL)}_{\aa_1\aa_2}(k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    +e^{(L)}_{\mb k,\mu}e^{(T)*}_{\mb k,\nu}
    D^{(LT)}_{\aa_1\aa_2}(k;\tau_1,\tau_2),
    \label{eq:polarization-decomposition}
\end{align}
where $\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3$.
For a comoving momentum $\mb k = k(\sin\theta\cos\phi, \sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)$, the covariant polarization basis is:
\begin{align}
    \begin{gathered}
    e^{(T)}_{\mb k,\mu}=(1,\mb0),\qquad
    e^{(L)}_{\mb k,\mu}=(0,\widehat{\mb k}),\\
    e^{(\pm)}_{\mb k,\mu}
    =\frac1{\sqrt2}(0,\mb e_\theta\pm\ii\mb e_\phi)
    =\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}
        0\\
        \cos\theta\cos\phi\mp\ii\sin\phi\\
        \cos\theta\sin\phi\pm\ii\cos\phi\\
        -\sin\theta
    \end{pmatrix},
    \end{gathered}
\end{align}
with $(\widehat{\mb k},\mb e_\theta,\mb e_\phi)$ the orthonormal spherical basis.
The label $T$ denotes the temporal component of the longitudinal mode.
At zero chemical potential, the vector propagator can be expressed through an auxiliary scalar propagator with the same mass index \cite{qin_helical_2023,qin_closed-form_2023}:
\begin{align}
    D^{(\mathrm{s})}_>(k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    &=\frac{\pi H^2}{4}e^{-\pi\tilde\nu}
    (\tau_1\tau_2)^{3/2}
    \mathrm H^{(1)}_{\ii\tilde\nu}(-k\tau_1)
    \mathrm H^{(2)}_{-\ii\tilde\nu}(-k\tau_2),
    \label{eq:scalar-propagator}\\
    D^{(\pm)}_>&=\frac{D^{(\mathrm{s})}_>}{H^2\tau_1\tau_2},\qquad
    D^{(L)}_>=\frac{(\partial_{\tau_1}-2/\tau_1)
    (\partial_{\tau_2}-2/\tau_2)D^{(\mathrm{s})}_>}
    {H^2(\tilde\nu^2+1/4)},
    \label{eq:vector-scalar-relation}\\
    D^{(T)}_>&=\frac{k^2D^{(\mathrm{s})}_>}
    {H^2(\tilde\nu^2+1/4)},
    \label{eq:temporal-scalar-relation}\\
    D^{(TL)}_>&=\frac{\ii k(\partial_{\tau_2}-2/\tau_2)D^{(\mathrm{s})}_>}
    {H^2(\tilde\nu^2+1/4)},\qquad
    D^{(LT)}_>=-\frac{\ii k(\partial_{\tau_1}-2/\tau_1)D^{(\mathrm{s})}_>}
    {H^2(\tilde\nu^2+1/4)}.
    \label{eq:mixed-scalar-relations}
\end{align}

For the spatial and mixed components, the SK propagators are constructed from the greater and lesser Wightman functions, with $D_{-+}=D_>$, $D_{+-}=D_<$, and the $++$ and $--$ propagators are
\begin{align}
    D_{++}&=\theta(\tau_1-\tau_2)D_>
              +\theta(\tau_2-\tau_1)D_<,\nonumber\\
    D_{--}&=\theta(\tau_2-\tau_1)D_>
              +\theta(\tau_1-\tau_2)D_<.
    \label{eq:sk-prescription}
\end{align}
These relations apply to $D^{(\pm)},D^{(L)},D^{(TL)}$ and $D^{(LT)}$.
But for $D^{(T)}$, the $++$ and $--$ propagators have a delta-function term because the temporal mode is not a physical degree of freedom:
\begin{align}
    D_{++}^{(T)}&=\theta(\tau_1-\tau_2)D_>^{(T)}
              +\theta(\tau_2-\tau_1)D_<^{(T)} + \frac{\ii \delta(\tau_1-\tau_2)}{H^2(\tn^2+1/4) a(\tau_1)^2} ,\nonumber\\
    D_{--}^{(T)}&=\theta(\tau_2-\tau_1)D_>^{(T)}
              +\theta(\tau_1-\tau_2)D_<^{(T)} - \frac{\ii \delta(\tau_1-\tau_2)}{H^2(\tn^2+1/4) a(\tau_1)^2}.
\end{align}
We will show that the propagators $D^{(T)},D^{(TL)}$ and $D^{(LT)}$ do not contribute to the leading nonlocal signal due to the one-loop factorization theorem.

The leading nonlocal parts of the spatial propagators are independent of the SK indices:
\begin{align}
    \left[D^{(\pm)}_{\aa_1\aa_2}(k;\tau_1,\tau_2)\right]_{\mathrm{NonL}}
    &=\frac{(\tau_1\tau_2)^{1/2}}{4\pi}
      \sum_{\eta=\pm1}\Gamma^2(-\ii\eta\tilde\nu)
      \left(\frac{k^2\tau_1\tau_2}{4}\right)^{\ii\eta\tilde\nu},
      \label{eq:transverse-nonlocal}\\
    \left[D^{(L)}_{\aa_1\aa_2}(k;\tau_1,\tau_2)\right]_{\mathrm{NonL}}
    &=\frac{(\tau_1\tau_2)^{1/2}}{4\pi}
      \sum_{\eta=\pm1}
      \frac{(-1/2+\ii\eta\tilde\nu)^2}{\tilde\nu^2+1/4}
      \Gamma^2(-\ii\eta\tilde\nu)
      \left(\frac{k^2\tau_1\tau_2}{4}\right)^{\ii\eta\tilde\nu}.
    \label{eq:longitudinal-nonlocal}
\end{align}
The corresponding nonlocal part of the temporal propagator is
\begin{equation}
    \left[D^{(T)}_{\aa_1\aa_2}(k;\tau_1,\tau_2)\right]_{\mathrm{NonL}}
    =\frac{k^2(\tau_1\tau_2)^{3/2}}
    {4\pi(\tilde\nu^2+1/4)}
    \sum_{\eta=\pm1}\Gamma^2(-\ii\eta\tilde\nu)
    \left(\frac{k^2\tau_1\tau_2}{4}\right)^{\ii\eta\tilde\nu}.
    \label{eq:temporal-nonlocal}
\end{equation}

The one-loop factorization theorem and cutting rule \cite{qin_inflation_2023} isolate the leading nonlocal signal in the collapsed channel.
We first identify the two internal lines, whose momenta can be chosen as $\mb q$ and $\mb K\equiv\mb q+\mb k_s$.
The relevant loop region has $q,K\sim k_s\ll k_i$.
We replace these two propagators by the leading nonlocal terms of their late-time expansions, keeping their polarization tensors, and expand the remaining hard propagators to leading order in the soft momenta.
The hard propagators retain their full time dependence and SK ordering.
The cut propagators are independent of the SK indices, and their time dependence separates between the two endpoints.
The time integrals thus factorize into two tree subgraphs with additional time powers supplied by the cut lines.
Multiplying these subgraphs by the soft momentum integral and summing over polarizations gives the nonlocal signal.
The branches $\eta=\pm1$ combine into the oscillatory signal retained below.
Higher terms in the soft expansion give subleading corrections in the collapsed limit.

Now we will show that the propagators $D^{(T)}$, $D^{(TL)}$, and $D^{(LT)}$ do not contribute to the leading nonlocal signal.
On a soft line, each temporal mode function $B^{(T)}$ carries an additional factor of $k\tau$ relative to the spatial modes $B^{(L)}$ and $B^{(\pm)}$.
Their nonanalytic contributions are therefore suppressed in the collapsed limit.
The contact term in $D^{(T)}$ is analytic in the soft momentum and does not contribute to the nonlocal signal.
On a hard line, a temporal endpoint has a vanishing polarization contraction with the spatial components of the attached soft line.
A nonzero contraction would require a temporal soft component, which is already suppressed.
We therefore omit propagators with temporal components when computing the leading signal below.

All required soft momentum integrals reduce to five dimensionless functions $f_r$, $r=0,1,2,3,4$.
With $z\equiv\widehat{\mb q}\cdot\widehat{\mb k}_s$, we define
\begin{equation}
    \left[ \int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
    q^{-2\alpha}K^{-2\beta}z^r \right]_{\mathrm{NonL}}
    =\frac{k_s^{3-2\alpha-2\beta}}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}
    f_r(\alpha,\beta),\qquad r=0,1,2,3,4.
    \label{eq:fr-definition}
\end{equation}
These expressions are understood by analytic continuation from the convergent domain, with only the nonanalytic term retained.
The first three functions are
\begin{align}
    f_0(\alpha,\beta)
    &=\Gamma\!\left[\begin{matrix}
        \alpha+\beta-\tfrac32,\,\tfrac32-\alpha,\,\tfrac32-\beta\\
        3-\alpha-\beta,\,\alpha,\,\beta
    \end{matrix}\right],
    \label{eq:f0}\\
    f_1(\alpha,\beta)
    &=\frac12\left[
    f_0(\alpha+\tfrac12,\beta-1)
    -f_0(\alpha-\tfrac12,\beta)
    -f_0(\alpha+\tfrac12,\beta)\right],
    \label{eq:f1}\\
    f_2(\alpha,\beta)
    &=\frac14\left[
    f_0(\alpha+1,\beta-2)+f_0(\alpha-1,\beta)
    +f_0(\alpha+1,\beta)\right.\nonumber\\
    &\hspace{13mm}\left.
    -2f_0(\alpha,\beta-1)-2f_0(\alpha+1,\beta-1)
    +2f_0(\alpha,\beta)\right].
    \label{eq:f2}
\end{align}
The functions $f_3$ and $f_4$ are likewise finite sums of $f_0$ and their explicit expressions are collected in App.~\ref{app:loop-integrals}.

For the nonlocal branch $\eta$, it is convenient to set $x=-\ii\eta\tilde\nu$ and define
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal J_r(m,n;x)
    \equiv f_r\left(x-\frac m2,x+\frac n2\right),
    \qquad r=0,1,2,3,4.
    \label{eq:Jr-definition}
\end{equation}
Here $m,n$ are integer shifts of momentum powers.
In particular,
\begin{equation}
    \left[
    \int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
    \left(\frac q2\right)^{-2x}
    \left(\frac K2\right)^{-2x}q^mK^{-n}z^r
    \right]_{\mathrm{NonL}}
    =\frac{4^{2x}k_s^{3-4x+m-n}}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}
    \mathcal J_r(m,n;x).
    \label{eq:Jr-normalization}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Bubble Diagram}
\label{sec:gauge-bubble}
The bubble contains two quartic vertices, each attached to a pair of external Higgs modes.
With the four external momenta fixed, the $s$-channel contribution is
\begin{align}
    \langle\delta h^4\rangle'_{\mathrm{Bub}}
    ={}&\frac{g^4}{8}
    \sum_{\aa_1,\aa_2=\pm1}(-\aa_1\aa_2)
    \int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{\di\tau_1}{|H\tau_1|^2}
    \int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{\di\tau_2}{|H\tau_2|^2}
    G_{\aa_1}(k_1;\tau_1)G_{\aa_1}(k_2;\tau_1)
    G_{\aa_2}(k_3;\tau_2)G_{\aa_2}(k_4;\tau_2)\nonumber\\
    &\times\int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
    D_{\aa_1\aa_2,ij}(\mb q;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    D_{\aa_2\aa_1,ji}(\mb K;\tau_2,\tau_1),
    \qquad \mb K=\mb q+\mb k_s.
    \label{eq:bubble-sk}
\end{align}
The two quartic vertices give the factor $\left(\frac{g^2}{2}\right)^2=\frac{g^4}{4}$.
An additional factor \(1/2\) accounts for the exchange of the two identical internal lines.
For the fixed partition $(12)\mid(34)$, exchanges within either external Higgs pair leave the graph unchanged.
There is therefore no additional external-permutation factor in the $s$-channel signal.

Replacing the two soft propagators by \eqref{eq:transverse-nonlocal}--\eqref{eq:longitudinal-nonlocal} removes their SK-index dependence, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:bubble-cutting}.
The oscillatory signal comes from the two soft propagators with the same branch label $\eta$.
After summing the polarizations, this contribution is
\begin{align}
    &\left[\int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
    D_{\aa_1\aa_2,ij}(\mb q;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    D_{\aa_2\aa_1,ji}(\mb K;\tau_2,\tau_1)\right]_{\mathrm{NonL}}
    \nonumber\\
    &\qquad\simeq\sum_{\eta=\pm1}
    (\tau_1\tau_2)^{1+2\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    \frac{\Gamma^4(-\ii\eta\tilde\nu)}{(4\pi)^{7/2}}
    4^{-2\ii\eta\tilde\nu}k_s^{3+4\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    \mathcal I_{\mathrm{Bub}}(-\ii\eta\tilde\nu).
    \label{eq:bubble-cut-loop}
\end{align}
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figure/Bub NL.pdf}
    \caption{The leading nonlocal part of the bubble in the $k_s\to0$ limit.
    The two soft gauge-boson lines supply the central loop factor, while the left and right subgraphs give the two quartic-vertex time integrals.
    }
    \label{fig:bubble-cutting}
\end{figure}

The dimensionless polarization sum has the compact form
\begin{align}
    \mathcal I_{\mathrm{Bub}}(x)
    ={}&\frac{3+2x}{1-2x}\,\mathcal J_0(0,0;x)
    +\frac{16x^2}{(1-2x)^2}
    \left[\mathcal J_0(2,2;x)+2\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_2(0,2;x)\right],
    \label{eq:bubble-polarization-sum}
\end{align}
with $x=-\ii\eta\tilde\nu$.
The separate polarization contributions are given in App.~\ref{app:bubble-polarizations}.

The remaining time integrals factorize.
For the left subgraph, at $k_1\simeq k_2\simeq k_{12}/2$, we find
\begin{align}
    &\sum_{\aa_1=\pm1}\ii\aa_1
    \int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{\di\tau}{|H\tau|^2}
    G_{\aa_1}(k_1;\tau)G_{\aa_1}(k_2;\tau)
    (-\tau)^{1+2\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    \simeq\frac{H^2k_{12}^{-2\ii\eta\tilde\nu}}
    {4k_1^3k_2^3}\,\mathcal P^{(1)}_{\eta}(\tilde\nu),
    \label{eq:bubble-time-integral}\\
    &\mathcal P^{(1)}_{\eta}(\tilde\nu)
    =\frac{2+\ii\eta\tilde\nu}{2\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    \ii\sinh(\pi\eta\tilde\nu)\Gamma(2+2\ii\eta\tilde\nu).
    \label{eq:bubble-P1}
\end{align}
The right vertex gives the same expression with $(k_1,k_2,k_{12})\to(k_3,k_4,k_{34})$.
Combining the two vertices with \eqref{eq:bubble-cut-loop}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
    \left[\langle\delta h^4\rangle'_{\mathrm{Bub}}\right]_{\mathrm{NS}}
    \simeq\frac{g^4H^4k_s^3}
    {128k_1^3k_2^3k_3^3k_4^3}
    \left[
    \left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)^{2\ii\tilde\nu}
    C^{\mathrm{Bub}}(\tilde\nu)+\text{c.c.}\right],
    \label{eq:bubble-signal}
\end{equation}
where the complex signal coefficient is
\begin{equation}
    C^{\mathrm{Bub}}(\tilde\nu)
    =\frac{\Gamma^4(-\ii\tilde\nu)}{(4\pi)^{7/2}}
    \left[\mathcal P^{(1)}_{+}(\tilde\nu)\right]^2
    \mathcal I_{\mathrm{Bub}}(-\ii\tilde\nu).
    \label{eq:bubble-coefficient}
\end{equation}
This definition separates the loop and time integrals from the couplings and diagram symmetry factor.
The bracket in \eqref{eq:bubble-signal} equals
\begin{equation}
    2|C^{\mathrm{Bub}}(\tilde\nu)|
    \cos\left[
    2\tilde\nu\log\!\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)
    +\arg C^{\mathrm{Bub}}(\tilde\nu)\right].
    \label{eq:bubble-phase}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Triangle Diagram}
\label{sec:gauge-triangle}
The triangle diagram contains two cubic vertices and one quartic vertex.
Its symmetry factor is unity.
We first consider the diagram ``Triangle 1''.
Its integral expression is
\begin{align}
    \langle\delta h^4\rangle'_{\mathrm{Tri1}}
    ={}& -\frac{g^6h_0^2}{8}
    \sum_{\aa_1,\aa_2,\aa_3=\pm1}(-\ii\aa_1\aa_2\aa_3)
    \int_{-\infty}^0\prod_{v=1}^{3}
    \frac{\di\tau_v}{|H\tau_v|^2}\,
    G_{\aa_1}(k_1;\tau_1)G_{\aa_2}(k_2;\tau_2)
    G_{\aa_3}(k_3;\tau_3)G_{\aa_3}(k_4;\tau_3)
    \nonumber\\
    &\times\int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
    D_{\aa_1\aa_2,ij}(\mb q+\mb k_1;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    D_{\aa_2\aa_3,j\ell}(\mb K;\tau_2,\tau_3)
    D_{\aa_3\aa_1,\ell i}(\mb q;\tau_3,\tau_1).
    \label{eq:triangle-sk}
\end{align}
The nonlocal signal comes from $q,K\sim k_s\ll k_1$.
We cut the $\mb q$ and $\mb K$ lines and evaluate the remaining hard propagator at $\mb q+\mb k_1\simeq\mb k_1$.
The resulting factorization is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:triangle-cutting}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figure/Tri NL.pdf}
    \caption{The leading nonlocal part of the first triangle.
    Cutting the two soft lines leaves a hard gauge-boson exchange on the left and a quartic vertex on the right, joined by the central soft loop factor.
    The second triangle follows by exchanging the two external pairs.}
    \label{fig:triangle-cutting}
\end{figure}

After summing the soft polarizations, the cut loop takes the form
\begin{align}
    &\left[\int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
    D_{\aa_1\aa_2,ij}(\mb q+\mb k_1;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    D_{\aa_2\aa_3,j\ell}(\mb K;\tau_2,\tau_3)
    D_{\aa_3\aa_1,\ell i}(\mb q;\tau_3,\tau_1)\right]_{\mathrm{NonL}}
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad\simeq\sum_{\lambda=L,\pm}\sum_{\eta=\pm1}
    D^{(\lambda)}_{\aa_1\aa_2}(k_1;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    (\tau_1\tau_2)^{1/2+\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    (-\tau_3)^{1+2\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    \frac{\Gamma^4(-\ii\eta\tilde\nu)}{(4\pi)^{7/2}}
    4^{-2\ii\eta\tilde\nu}k_s^{3+4\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    \mathcal I^\lambda_{\mathrm{Tri}}(-\ii\eta\tilde\nu,\theta_1).
    \label{eq:triangle-cut-loop}
\end{align}
This equation defines the dimensionless coefficients $\mathcal I^\lambda_{\mathrm{Tri}}(x,\theta)$, with $x=-\ii\eta\tilde\nu$.
After simplification, we find that all three coefficients equal $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Bub}}(x)/3$ and the apparent angular dependence cancels, as shown in \eqref{eq:triangle-I-closed}.
We therefore suppress the angular argument in $\mathcal I^\lambda_{\mathrm{Tri}}$ below.

We next evaluate the left and right subgraphs.
The quartic vertex gives the time integral $\mathcal P^{(1)}_{\eta}$ in \eqref{eq:bubble-P1}.
The two cubic vertices and the hard line instead define
\begin{align}
    &\sum_{\aa_1,\aa_2=\pm1}(-\aa_1\aa_2)
    \int_{-\infty}^0\frac{\di\tau_1\di\tau_2}
    {|H\tau_1|^2|H\tau_2|^2}
    G_{\aa_1}(k_1;\tau_1)G_{\aa_2}(k_2;\tau_2)
    D^{(\lambda)}_{\aa_1\aa_2}(k_1;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    (\tau_1\tau_2)^{1/2+\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    \nonumber\\
    &\qquad\simeq
    \frac{k_{12}^{-2\ii\eta\tilde\nu}}{4k_1^3k_2^3}
    \mathcal P^{(2),\lambda}_{\eta}(\tilde\nu).
    \label{eq:triangle-time-integral}
\end{align}
At leading order, $k_1\simeq k_2\simeq k_{12}/2$.
The integral therefore reduces to the double-folded scalar seeds of Ref.~\cite{qin_closed-form_2023}, which we denote by $\mathcal M_{\aa_1\aa_2}^{p_1p_2}\equiv \widetilde{\mathcal I}_{\aa_1\aa_2}^{p_1p_2}(1,1)$.
For the transverse modes this follows directly from \eqref{eq:vector-scalar-relation}.
For the longitudinal mode, the differential relation also produces a delta-function contribution by integration by parts, and we retain this term together with the scalar-seed contributions.
The required seed exponents include $p_0=-5/2+\ii\eta\tilde\nu$, on the boundary of the convergence domain.
We define the result by analytic continuation from $p_0+\epsilon$, $\epsilon>0$, and take the finite part of the assembled expression at $\epsilon=0$.
The regulator poles cancel after summing all SK branches.
The seed normalization, closed forms, and combinations giving $\mathcal P^{(2),\lambda}_{\eta}$ are collected in App.~\ref{app:triangle-coefficients}.

Finally, the leading signal of the first triangle is
\begin{equation}
    \left[\langle\delta h^4\rangle'_{\mathrm{Tri1}}\right]_{\mathrm{NS}}
    \simeq -\frac{g^6h_0^2H^2k_s^3}
    {128k_1^3k_2^3k_3^3k_4^3}
    \left[
    \left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)^{2\ii\tilde\nu}
    C^{\mathrm{Tri}}(\tilde\nu)+\text{c.c.}\right],
    \label{eq:triangle-single-signal}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    C^{\mathrm{Tri}}(\tilde\nu)
    =\frac{\Gamma^4(-\ii\tilde\nu)}{(4\pi)^{7/2}}
    \mathcal P^{(1)}_{+}(\tilde\nu)
    \sum_{\lambda=L,\pm}
    \mathcal P^{(2),\lambda}_{+}(\tilde\nu)
    \mathcal I^\lambda_{\mathrm{Tri}}(-\ii\tilde\nu).
    \label{eq:triangle-coefficient}
\end{equation}
The simplified loop coefficients make $C^{\mathrm{Tri}}$ independent of $\theta$ at this order and its reduced expression is given in \eqref{eq:triangle-C-reduced}.

In the collapsed $s$ channel, the quartic vertex carries either the pair $(3,4)$, as in Triangle 1, or the pair $(1,2)$, as in Triangle 2.
For either placement, exchanging the two Higgs legs at the cubic vertices reproduces the same undirected triangle after reflection.
Exchanging the two legs at the quartic vertex also leaves the graph unchanged, since both attach to the same vertex.
Each placement is therefore counted once.
Triangle 2 follows from Triangle 1 by $(1,2)\leftrightarrow(3,4)$, and their leading collapsed signals are equal.
Adding these two contributions gives
\begin{align}
    \left[\langle\delta h^4\rangle'_{\mathrm{Tri}}\right]_{\mathrm{NS}}
    \simeq{}&-\frac{g^6h_0^2H^2k_s^3}
    {64k_1^3k_2^3k_3^3k_4^3}
    \left[\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)^{2\ii\tilde\nu}
    C^{\mathrm{Tri}}(\tilde\nu)
    +\text{c.c.}\right].
    \label{eq:triangle-total-signal}
\end{align}
The nonanalytic momentum dependence is the same as in the bubble, while the hard propagator changes the amplitude and phase.

\subsection{Box Diagram}
\label{sec:gauge-box}
The box diagram contains four cubic vertices.
We first consider the box with cyclic ordering $1234$, shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:box-cutting}.
With the external labels fixed, its diagram symmetry factor is unity.
The four cubic vertices give the coupling factor $(g^2h_0/2)^4=g^8h_0^4/16$.
Its $s$-channel contribution is
\begin{align}
    &\langle\delta h^4\rangle'_{\mathrm{Box},1234}
    =\frac{g^8h_0^4}{16}
    \sum_{\aa_1,\aa_2,\aa_3,\aa_4=\pm1}\aa_1\aa_2\aa_3\aa_4
    \int_{-\infty}^0\prod_{v=1}^{4}
    \frac{\di\tau_v}{|H\tau_v|^2}
    \nonumber\\
    &\qquad\times G_{\aa_1}(k_1;\tau_1)G_{\aa_2}(k_2;\tau_2)
    G_{\aa_3}(k_3;\tau_3)G_{\aa_4}(k_4;\tau_4)
    \nonumber\\
    &\qquad\times\int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
    D_{\aa_1\aa_2,ij}(\mb q+\mb k_1;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    D_{\aa_2\aa_3,j\ell}(\mb K;\tau_2,\tau_3)
    \nonumber\\
    &\qquad\times D_{\aa_3\aa_4,\ell m}(\mb q-\mb k_4;\tau_3,\tau_4)
    D_{\aa_4\aa_1,mi}(\mb q;\tau_4,\tau_1),
    \label{eq:box-sk}
\end{align}
where $\mb K=\mb q+\mb k_s$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figure/Box NL.pdf}
    \caption{The leading nonlocal part of the box with cyclic ordering $1234$.
    Cutting the two soft lines separates two tree subgraphs, each containing one hard gauge-boson propagator.
    The central factor contains the nonlocal parts of the two soft propagators and their loop momentum integral.}
    \label{fig:box-cutting}
\end{figure}

In the collapsed limit, the leading nonlocal signal in \eqref{eq:box-sk} comes from the region $q,K\sim k_s$.
Cutting these two soft lines leaves two hard propagators, with momenta approaching $\mb k_1$ and $\mb k_3$.
Their polarizations are denoted by $\lambda_1,\lambda_3=L,\pm$.
The soft loop gives the dimensionless coefficient $\mathcal I^{\lambda_1\lambda_3}_{\mathrm{Box}} (-\ii\eta\tilde\nu,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)$ defined in \eqref{eq:box-I-definition}.
Explicitly, after summing the soft polarizations, the cut loop is
\begin{align}
    &\left[\int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
    D_{\aa_1\aa_2,ij}(\mb q+\mb k_1;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    D_{\aa_2\aa_3,j\ell}(\mb K;\tau_2,\tau_3)\times
    D_{\aa_3\aa_4,\ell m}(\mb q-\mb k_4;\tau_3,\tau_4)
    D_{\aa_4\aa_1,mi}(\mb q;\tau_4,\tau_1)\right]_{\mathrm{NonL}}
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad\simeq\sum_{\lambda_1,\lambda_3=L,\pm}\sum_{\eta=\pm1}
    D^{(\lambda_1)}_{\aa_1\aa_2}(k_1;\tau_1,\tau_2)
    D^{(\lambda_3)}_{\aa_3\aa_4}(k_3;\tau_3,\tau_4)
    (\tau_1\tau_2)^{1/2+\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    (\tau_3\tau_4)^{1/2+\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    \nonumber\\
    &\qquad\times
    \frac{\Gamma^4(-\ii\eta\tilde\nu)}{(4\pi)^{7/2}}
    4^{-2\ii\eta\tilde\nu}k_s^{3+4\ii\eta\tilde\nu}
    \mathcal I^{\lambda_1\lambda_3}_{\mathrm{Box}}
    (-\ii\eta\tilde\nu,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3).
    \label{eq:box-cut-loop}
\end{align}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:box-cutting}, the remaining time integrals factorize into two copies of \eqref{eq:triangle-time-integral}, one for each hard line.
The left and right subgraphs contribute $\mathcal P^{(2),\lambda_1}_{\eta}(\tilde\nu)$ and $\mathcal P^{(2),\lambda_3}_{\eta}(\tilde\nu)$, respectively.

The collapsed $s$ channel contains a second inequivalent box, obtained from the diagram shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:box-cutting} by exchanging the external momenta $\mb k_1$ and $\mb k_2$.
Exchanging $\mb k_3$ and $\mb k_4$ gives the same additional undirected graph, while exchanging both pairs returns the original graph.
At leading collapsed order, $\mb k_2\simeq-\mb k_1$, so the first exchange sends $(\theta_1,\phi_3)$ to $(\pi-\theta_1,\phi_3+\pi)$.
The coefficient $C^{\mathrm{Box}}$ is invariant under this transformation, as follows from its angular decomposition below.
The two boxes therefore contribute equally.

For each box, the vertex factor $1/16$ and the normalization $1/4$ from each hard subgraph give $1/256$.
Summing the two distinct boxes supplies a further factor $2$, giving $1/128$.
Including the complex-conjugate soft branch, the total $s$-channel signal is
\begin{align}
    \left[\langle\delta h^4\rangle'_{\mathrm{Box}}
    \right]_{\mathrm{NS}}
    \simeq{}&\frac{g^8h_0^4k_s^3}
    {128k_1^3k_2^3k_3^3k_4^3}
    \left[
    \left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)^{2\ii\tilde\nu}
    \times
    C^{\mathrm{Box}}(\tilde\nu,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)
    +\text{c.c.}\right].
    \label{eq:box-single-signal}
\end{align}
Here $C^{\mathrm{Box}}$ denotes the coefficient of the representative box, before summing the external orderings:
\begin{align}
    C^{\mathrm{Box}}(\tilde\nu,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)
    ={}&\frac{\Gamma^4(-\ii\tilde\nu)}{(4\pi)^{7/2}}
    \sum_{\lambda_1,\lambda_3=L,\pm}
    \mathcal P^{(2),\lambda_1}_{+}(\tilde\nu)
    \mathcal P^{(2),\lambda_3}_{+}(\tilde\nu)
    \times\mathcal I^{\lambda_1\lambda_3}_{\mathrm{Box}}
    (-\ii\tilde\nu,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3).
    \label{eq:box-coefficient}
\end{align}

Since the two hard transverse helicities have equal time integrals, the polarization sum reduces to the $LL$, $LH$, and $HH$ groups defined in \eqref{eq:box-channel-groups}, where $H$ denotes the sum over transverse helicities.
Their explicit expressions are given in App.~\ref{app:box-coefficients}.

The result contains five angular structures:
\begin{align}
    C^{\mathrm{Box}}(\tilde\nu,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)
    ={}&A_{1,g}(\tilde\nu)
    +A_{2,g}(\tilde\nu)(\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_3)
    +A_{3,g}(\tilde\nu)\cos^2\theta_1\cos^2\theta_3
    \nonumber\\
    &+B_g(\tilde\nu)\sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3
    \nonumber\\
    &+D_g(\tilde\nu)\sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3).
    \label{eq:box-angular-expansion}
\end{align}
The five complex functions depend only on $\tilde\nu$.
The oscillatory momentum dependence of the box signal is the same as in \eqref{eq:bubble-phase}, with an angle-dependent amplitude and phase supplied by $C^{\mathrm{Box}}$.

\section{Top Quark Signal}
\label{sec:dirac-top}

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:dirac-action}
We next compute the Higgs four-point function generated by the top-quark loop.
For simplicity, we define the coupling and mass parameters:
\begin{equation}
 g_t\equiv\frac{y_t}{\sqrt2},\qquad m_t=g_th_0,\qquad
 \tn\equiv\frac{m_t}{H}>0.
 \label{eq:dirac-parameters}
\end{equation}

For the fermion calculation, we adopt the conventions of~\cite{aoki_fermionic_2026,you_neutrino_2026}.
Let $t_L$ and $t_R$ denote the covariant two-component left- and right-handed top quark fields.
With $a=-1/(H\tau)$, $e_A{}^\mu=a^{-1}\delta_A{}^\mu$ and $\eta^{AB}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, their action is
\begin{align}
 S_t=\int\di^4x\sqrt{-g}\,\Big[&
 \ii t_L^\dagger\bar\sigma^Ae_A{}^\mu\nabla_\mu t_L
 +\ii t_R^\dagger\sigma^Ae_A{}^\mu\nabla_\mu t_R-(m_t+g_t\delta h)
 (t_L^\dagger t_R+t_R^\dagger t_L)\Big].
 \label{eq:dirac-chiral-action}
\end{align}
Here $\sigma^A=(\mathbf1_2,\bm\sigma)$ and $\bar\sigma^A=(\mathbf1_2,-\bm\sigma)$, with $\sigma^i$ the Pauli matrices.
The covariant derivative acts in the corresponding chiral representation, and color indices are suppressed.

We choose the Dirac basis, with $\gamma^5=\ii\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$:
\begin{align}
 &\gamma^0=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf1_2&0\\0&-\mathbf1_2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \gamma^i=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^i\\-\sigma^i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \gamma^5=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf1_2\\\mathbf1_2&0\end{pmatrix},
 \nonumber\\
 &\{\gamma^A,\gamma^B\}=-2\eta^{AB}\mathbf1_4,\qquad
 \bar T=T^\dagger\gamma^0,\qquad
 P_{L,R}=\frac{\mathbf1_4\mp\gamma^5}{2}.
 \label{eq:dirac-clifford}
\end{align}
The covariant Dirac field and its chiral projections are
\begin{equation}
 T_{\mathrm{cov}}=\frac1{\sqrt2}
 \begin{pmatrix}t_L+t_R\\t_R-t_L\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 P_LT_{\mathrm{cov}}=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}t_L\\-t_L\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 P_RT_{\mathrm{cov}}=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}t_R\\t_R\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:dirac-field}
\end{equation}
Then $\bar T_{\mathrm{cov}}T_{\mathrm{cov}} =t_L^\dagger t_R+t_R^\dagger t_L$, and the action becomes
\begin{equation}
 S_t=\int\di^4x\sqrt{-g}\,
 \bar T_{\mathrm{cov}}
 \left(\ii\gamma^Ae_A{}^\mu\nabla_\mu-m_t-g_t\delta h\right)
 T_{\mathrm{cov}}.
 \label{eq:dirac-covariant-action}
\end{equation}
Rescaling $T=a^{3/2}T_{\mathrm{cov}}$ removes the spin-connection term and gives
\begin{equation}
 S_t=\int\di\tau\,\di^3\mb x\,
 \left[\bar T(\ii\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-a m_t)T
       -a g_t\delta h\,\bar T T\right],
 \qquad \gamma^\mu\equiv\delta_A{}^\mu\gamma^A.
 \label{eq:dirac-rescaled-action}
\end{equation}

We write $\gamma_k=\bm\gamma\cdot\widehat{\mb k}$, where $\bm\gamma\cdot\mb v=\sum_i\gamma^iv_i$.
The helicity operator and projectors are $\Sigma_k=\gamma^5\gamma^0\gamma_k$ and $P_s=(\mathbf1_4+s\Sigma_k)/2$, with $s=\pm1$.
Choose polarization spinors satisfying
\begin{equation}
 P_Lh^s_{\mb k}=h^s_{\mb k},\qquad
 \Sigma_kh^s_{\mb k}=s h^s_{\mb k},\qquad
 h^{s\dagger}_{\mb k}h^{s'}_{\mb k}=\delta^{ss'},\qquad
 h^s_{\mb k}h^{s\dagger}_{\mb k}=P_LP_s.
 \label{eq:dirac-polarizations}
\end{equation}

The mode expansion is
\begin{equation}
 T(\tau,\mb x)=\int\frac{\di^3\mb k}{(2\pi)^3}\sum_{s=\pm1}
 \left[U_s(\mb k,\tau)b^s_{\mb k}e^{\ii\mb k\cdot\mb x}
       +V_s(\mb k,\tau)d^{s\dagger}_{\mb k}e^{-\ii\mb k\cdot\mb x}\right],
 \label{eq:dirac-mode-expansion}
\end{equation}
where $b^s_{\mb k}$ and $d^s_{\mb k}$ annihilate the top and antitop, with
\begin{equation}
 \{b^s_{\mb k},b^{s'\dagger}_{\mb k'}\}
 =\{d^s_{\mb k},d^{s'\dagger}_{\mb k'}\}
 =(2\pi)^3\delta^{ss'}\delta^3(\mb k-\mb k').
 \label{eq:dirac-anticommutators}
\end{equation}

The positive- and negative-frequency spinors are
\begin{equation}
 U_s=(u_s\mathbf1_4+v_s\gamma^0)h^s_{\mb k},\qquad
 V_s=C\bar U_s^{\,T},\qquad \bar U_s=U_s^\dagger\gamma^0,
 \label{eq:dirac-mode-spinors}
\end{equation}
Here $C(\gamma^A)^TC^{-1}=-\gamma^A$, $C^T=-C$ and $C^\dagger C=\mathbf1_4$.
The label $s$ on $V_s$ refers to $U_s$, so $\Sigma_kV_s=-sV_s$.
The normalized Bunch--Davies mode functions are
\begin{equation}
 u_+=v_- =\frac{\tn}{\sqrt{2x}}
 W_{-1/2,\ii\tn}(-2\ii x),\qquad
 u_-=v_+ =\frac{\ii}{\sqrt{2x}}
 W_{1/2,\ii\tn}(-2\ii x),\qquad x=-k\tau.
 \label{eq:dirac-radial-modes}
\end{equation}
They satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \ii\partial_\tau u_s+sku_s=am_tv_s,\qquad
 \ii\partial_\tau v_s-skv_s=am_tu_s,\qquad
 |u_s|^2+|v_s|^2=1.
 \label{eq:dirac-radial-eom}
\end{equation}

We use the propagator
\begin{equation}
 D_{\aa\bb,\alpha\beta}(\mb k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 =\langle \hat{T}_C T_{\aa,\alpha}(\mb k,\tau_1)
                 \bar T_{\bb,\beta}(-\mb k,\tau_2)\rangle',
 \qquad \alpha,\beta=1,\ldots,4.
 \label{eq:dirac-propagator-definition}
\end{equation}
Here $\hat T_C$ denotes ordering along the Schwinger--Keldysh contour, and $\aa,\bb=\pm1$ are its branch labels.
The Wightman components are
\begin{align}
 D_{-+}(\mb k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\sum_s U_s(\mb k,\tau_1)\bar U_s(\mb k,\tau_2),\nonumber\\
 D_{+-}(\mb k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=-\sum_s V_s(-\mb k,\tau_1)\bar V_s(-\mb k,\tau_2).
 \label{eq:dirac-wightman}
\end{align}

The fermionic reversal rule is
\begin{equation}
 \langle T_C\bar T_{\aa,\alpha}(\mb k,\tau_1)
                 T_{\bb,\beta}(-\mb k,\tau_2)\rangle'
 =-[D_{\bb\aa}(-\mb k;\tau_2,\tau_1)]_{\beta\alpha}.
 \label{eq:dirac-reversal}
\end{equation}

For the seed reduction, it is useful to express these propagators through the scalar propagator with chemical potential \cite{qin_closed-form_2023}.
With $X=-k\tau_1$ and $Y=-k\tau_2$, we define the scalar propagators with chemical potential parameter $\kappa$ as
\begin{align}
 D^{(\kappa)}_{-+}(k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{e^{\ii\pi\kappa}}{2k}
 W_{\kappa,\ii\tn}(-2\ii X)W_{-\kappa,\ii\tn}(2\ii Y),\nonumber\\
 D^{(\kappa)}_{+-}(k;\tau_1,\tau_2)
 &=\frac{e^{\ii\pi\kappa}}{2k}
 W_{-\kappa,\ii\tn}(2\ii X)W_{\kappa,\ii\tn}(-2\ii Y).
 \label{eq:dirac-scalar-kernels}
\end{align}
Here $W_{\kappa,\ii\tn}$ is the Whittaker W function.
The seed parameter is analytically continued to $\kappa=\pm1/2$.
With $\sigma_k=\bm\sigma\cdot\widehat{\mb k}$, the Dirac propagator is
\begin{align}
 D_{\aa\bb}
 =\frac{k}{2\sqrt{XY}}\Bigg[&
 \begin{pmatrix}
 (\tn+X-\ii X\partial_X+\tfrac\ii2)\mathbf1_2
 &-(\tn+X-\ii X\partial_X+\tfrac\ii2)\sigma_k\\
 -(\tn-X+\ii X\partial_X-\tfrac\ii2)\sigma_k
 &(\tn-X+\ii X\partial_X-\tfrac\ii2)\mathbf1_2
 \end{pmatrix}
 D^{(-1/2)}_{\aa\bb}\nonumber\\
 &+\begin{pmatrix}
 (\tn-X-\ii X\partial_X+\tfrac\ii2)\mathbf1_2
 &(\tn-X-\ii X\partial_X+\tfrac\ii2)\sigma_k\\
 (\tn+X+\ii X\partial_X-\tfrac\ii2)\sigma_k
 &(\tn+X+\ii X\partial_X-\tfrac\ii2)\mathbf1_2
 \end{pmatrix}
 D^{(+1/2)}_{\aa\bb}\Bigg].
 \label{eq:dirac-scalar-reconstruction}
\end{align}
The derivatives act on the scalar kernels at fixed $Y$.
The soft limit follows from the small-argument expansion
\begin{equation}
 W_{\kappa,\ii\tn}(z)
 =\sum_{\eta=\pm1}
 \frac{\Gamma(-2\ii\eta\tn)}
      {\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii\eta\tn-\kappa)}
 z^{1/2+\ii\eta\tn}
 \left[1-\frac{\kappa z}{1+2\ii\eta\tn}+O(z^2)\right].
 \label{eq:dirac-whittaker-soft}
\end{equation}
Substituting this expansion gives, at $|k\tau_1|,|k\tau_2|\ll1$, the required nonlocal terms
\begin{align}
 [D_{\aa\bb}(\mb k;\tau_1,\tau_2)]_{\NL}
 ={}&-\frac1{4\pi}\sum_{\eta=\pm1}
 \Gamma^2(\tfrac12-\ii\eta\tn)
 \left(\frac{k^2\tau_1\tau_2}{4}\right)^{\ii\eta\tn}\nonumber\\
 &\times\left[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \dfrac{\ii k[(1-\eta)\tau_1-(1+\eta)\tau_2]}{1+2\ii\eta\tn}\mathbf1_2
 &(1+\eta)\sigma_k\\[2mm]
 -(1-\eta)\sigma_k
 &\dfrac{\ii k[(1-\eta)\tau_2-(1+\eta)\tau_1]}{1+2\ii\eta\tn}\mathbf1_2
 \end{pmatrix}
 +\cdots\right].
 \label{eq:dirac-soft-propagator}
\end{align}
The omitted terms start at second order in soft momentum at fixed times.
The Yukawa interaction gives the amputated vertex $-\ii\aa g_ta(\tau)\mathbf1_4$.

\subsection{The Box Diagram and Soft Expansion}
\label{sec:dirac-box}
We first consider the box shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:dirac-box} with ordering $1234$ for a fixed color.
For the collapsed $s$-channel contribution, we retain the original term together with those obtained by exchanging $\mb k_1\leftrightarrow\mb k_2$, exchanging $\mb k_3\leftrightarrow\mb k_4$, and performing both exchanges.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figure/quark.pdf}
\caption{The top-quark box with cyclic ordering $1234$.
Black lines denote external Higgs modes and blue lines denote Dirac propagators.
The arrows run from $j$ to $i$ for $D^{ij}$.}
\label{fig:dirac-box}
\end{figure}

The Higgs four-point function is
\begin{align}
 \langle\delta h^4\rangle'_{1234}
 ={}&-g_t^4\sum_{\aa_1,\ldots,\aa_4=\pm1}
 \left(\prod_{v=1}^4\aa_v\right)
 \int_{-\infty}^0\prod_{v=1}^4
 [\di\tau_v\,a(\tau_v)G_{\aa_v}(k_v;\tau_v)]\nonumber\\
 &\times\int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
 \tr_4\!\left[D^{12}(\mb q+\mb k_1)
 D^{23}(\mb K)D^{34}(\mb q-\mb k_4)D^{41}(\mb q)\right],
 \qquad \mb K=\mb q+\mb k_s.
 \label{eq:dirac-box-integral}
\end{align}
where $D^{ij}(\mb p)=D_{\aa_i\aa_j}(\mb p;\tau_i,\tau_j)$.

In the double-soft region $q,K\sim k_s$, introduce centered hard momenta
\begin{equation}
 \mb k_L=\frac{\mb k_1-\mb k_2}{2},\qquad
 \mb k_R=\frac{\mb k_3-\mb k_4}{2},\qquad
 \bm\ell=\mb q+\frac{\mb k_s}{2}.
 \label{eq:dirac-centered-momenta}
\end{equation}
The hard lines carry $\mb k_L+\bm\ell$ and $\mb k_R+\bm\ell$.
At leading collapsed order, $k_L\simeq k_{12}/2$ and $k_R\simeq k_{34}/2$.
The cutting rule replaces the two soft propagators by $D^{ij}_{\NL}$, giving the factorization in Fig.~\ref{fig:dirac-cut}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figure/quark NL.pdf}
\caption{The leading nonlocal part of the top-quark box with cyclic ordering $1234$ in the collapsed $s$ channel.
Cutting the two soft lines separates two tree subgraphs, each containing one hard Dirac propagator.
The central factor contains the nonlocal parts of the two soft propagators and their loop momentum integral.
Four-component spinor indices are contracted between the three factors.}
\label{fig:dirac-cut}
\end{figure}

We combine the contributions from diagrams with different orderings at the integrand level.
The four integrand factors are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal T^{}_{1234}
 &=\tr_4[D^{12}(\mb k_L+\bm\ell)D^{23}_{\NL}(\mb K)
          D^{34}(\mb k_R+\bm\ell)D^{41}_{\NL}(\mb q)],\nonumber\\
 \mathcal T^{}_{1243}
 &=\tr_4[D^{12}(\mb k_L+\bm\ell)D^{24}_{\NL}(\mb K)
          D^{43}(-\mb k_R+\bm\ell)D^{31}_{\NL}(\mb q)],\nonumber\\
 \mathcal T^{}_{1342}
 &=\tr_4[D^{21}(-\mb k_L+\bm\ell)D^{13}_{\NL}(\mb K)
          D^{34}(\mb k_R+\bm\ell)D^{42}_{\NL}(\mb q)],\nonumber\\
 \mathcal T^{}_{1432}
 &=\tr_4[D^{21}(-\mb k_L+\bm\ell)D^{14}_{\NL}(\mb K)
          D^{43}(-\mb k_R+\bm\ell)D^{32}_{\NL}(\mb q)].
 \label{eq:dirac-four-cut-traces}
\end{align}
Every time and SK label remains attached to its physical external vertex.
So the total nonlocal signal in the collapsed s channel is:
\begin{align}
 [\langle\delta h^4\rangle'_{t}]_{\mathrm{NS}}
 \simeq{}&-g_t^4\sum_{\aa_1,\ldots,\aa_4=\pm1}
 \left(\prod_{v=1}^4\aa_v\right)
 \int_{-\infty}^0\prod_{v=1}^4
 [\di\tau_v\,a(\tau_v)G_{\aa_v}(k_v;\tau_v)]\nonumber\\
 &\times\int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
 \left(\mathcal T^{\mathrm{}}_{1234}
       +\mathcal T^{\mathrm{}}_{1243}
       +\mathcal T^{\mathrm{}}_{1342}
       +\mathcal T^{\mathrm{}}_{1432}\right).
 \label{eq:dirac-cut-integral}
\end{align}

We take the branch $\eta=+1$ for example and suppress its label.
Attaching the soft endpoints to each hard line gives matrices $V_{12}$ and $V_{34}$, defined in \eqref{eq:dirac-hard-blocks} of App.~\ref{app:dirac-expansion}.
With $\gamma_{\mb n}=\bm\gamma\cdot\mb n$ and $M(\mb n)=(\mathbf1_4+\gamma^0)\gamma_{\mb n}$, the corresponding cut integrand takes the form
\begin{align}
 \mathcal T^{\mathrm{}}_{1234,+}
 ={}&\frac{4^{-2\ii\tn}\Gamma^4(\tfrac12-\ii\tn)}{(4\pi)^2}
 (qK)^{2\ii\tn}\prod_{v=1}^4(-\tau_v)^{\ii\tn}
 \times\tr_4[V_{12}M(\widehat{\mb K})
                  V_{34}M(\widehat{\mb q})].
 \label{eq:dirac-cut-trace}
\end{align}
Summing the four orderings replaces the trace with
\begin{equation}
 \tr_4[(V_{12}+V_{21})M(\widehat{\mb K})
       (V_{34}+V_{43})M(\widehat{\mb q})].
 \label{eq:dirac-four-orderings}
\end{equation}

To isolate the relevant spinor blocks, introduce the projectors
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\Lambda_+=\frac{\mathbf1_4+\gamma^0}{2}
 =\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf1_2&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Lambda_-=\frac{\mathbf1_4-\gamma^0}{2}
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&\mathbf1_2\end{pmatrix},\\[2mm]
 &M(\mb n)=2\Lambda_+\gamma_{\mb n}
          =2\gamma_{\mb n}\Lambda_-
          =\begin{pmatrix}0&2\bm\sigma\cdot\mb n\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:dirac-soft-projectors}
\end{equation}
Consequently, only $\Lambda_-V\Lambda_+$ contributes to the trace.

At zeroth soft order, the projections give the following expressions, with $F_{0;\aa\bb}=F_{0,+;\aa\bb}$ defined in \eqref{eq:dirac-hard-kernel-traces}:
\begin{align}
 \Lambda_-V_{12}^{(0)}\Lambda_+
 &=-\frac12F_{0;\aa_1\aa_2}(k_L;\tau_1,\tau_2)
       \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\\bm\sigma\cdot\mb n_L&0\end{pmatrix},
 \nonumber\\
 \Lambda_-V_{21}^{(0)}\Lambda_+
 &=\frac12F_{0;\aa_1\aa_2}(k_L;\tau_1,\tau_2)
       \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\\bm\sigma\cdot\mb n_L&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mb n_L=\mb k_L/k_L.
 \label{eq:dirac-cancellation}
\end{align}
The right pair obeys the same relation with $(L,1,2)\to(R,3,4)$.
Thus each projected ordering sum vanishes at the integrand level, removing the candidate $k_s^{3\pm4\ii\tn}$ signal.

The cancellation above admits a partial-wave interpretation.
At zeroth order in the soft-momentum expansion, the $J=0$ contribution is absent from the projected integrand, while the remaining $J=1$ contribution cancels upon summing the orderings related by exchanging the two external Higgs legs.
Here $J$ denotes the total angular momentum of the Higgs pair.

To understand this structure, consider the left cut subgraph as the analogue of an $hh\to t\bar t$ amplitude mediated by a hard $t$-channel fermion exchange.
An individual exchange generally contains an infinite tower of partial waves, but higher orbital angular momenta are suppressed by additional powers of the relative momentum.
So the first two leading terms will be $J=0$ and $J=1$ partial waves.
The absence of the $J=0$ term has a simple flat-space interpretation.
In this channel, the incoming Higgs pair has positive parity, whereas the outgoing $L=0$ $t\bar t$ state has negative parity, since a fermion--antifermion pair has parity $P=(-1)^{L+1}$.
The transition is therefore forbidden by the parity-conserving Yukawa interaction.
And the $J=1$ contribution is excluded by Bose symmetry: since the two incoming Higgs particles are identical scalars, their symmetrized state contains only even $J$.
This is the partial-wave counterpart of the cancellation between the two external orderings.
Therefore the zeroth-order soft contribution vanishes.

We therefore expand each hard subgraph to first order in the soft momenta.
Combining the hard-line correction \eqref{eq:dirac-hard-expansion} and the endpoint correction \eqref{eq:dirac-endpoint-correction} gives
\begin{align}
 \Lambda_-(V_{12}^{(1)}+V_{21}^{(1)})\Lambda_+
 &=\frac1{k_L}\Lambda_-
       (\bm\gamma\cdot\mb v_L)\Lambda_+,
 &\mb v_L&=\Pi^L_1\bm\ell
       +\Pi^L_2\mb n_L(\mb n_L\cdot\bm\ell),\nonumber\\
 \Lambda_-(V_{34}^{(1)}+V_{43}^{(1)})\Lambda_+
 &=\frac1{k_R}\Lambda_-
       (\bm\gamma\cdot\mb v_R)\Lambda_+,
 &\mb v_R&=\Pi^R_1\bm\ell
       +\Pi^R_2\mb n_R(\mb n_R\cdot\bm\ell).
 \label{eq:dirac-first-order}
\end{align}
Here $\mb n_R=\widehat{\mb k}_R$ and $\Pi_i^L=\Pi_{i;\aa_1\aa_2}(k_L;\tau_1,\tau_2)$, with analogous definitions for the right subgraph.
The scalar functions $\Pi_i$ are given in \eqref{eq:dirac-Pi}.
Using
\begin{align}
 &\tr_4[\Lambda_-(\bm\gamma\cdot\mb a)
 (\bm\gamma\cdot\mb b)(\bm\gamma\cdot\mb c)
 (\bm\gamma\cdot\mb d)]\nonumber\\
 &\qquad=2[(\mb a\cdot\mb b)(\mb c\cdot\mb d)
 -(\mb a\cdot\mb c)(\mb b\cdot\mb d)
 +(\mb a\cdot\mb d)(\mb b\cdot\mb c)],
 \label{eq:dirac-four-gamma-trace}
\end{align}
the non-vanishing leading term of the sum of four traces becomes
\begin{align}
 &\tr_4[(V_{12}^{(1)}+V_{21}^{(1)})M(\widehat{\mb K})
        (V_{34}^{(1)}+V_{43}^{(1)})M(\widehat{\mb q})]
 \nonumber\\
 &=\frac{8}{k_Lk_RqK}\left[
 2(\mb v_L\cdot\bm\ell)(\mb v_R\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\frac12(\mb v_L\cdot\mb k_s)(\mb v_R\cdot\mb k_s)
 -(\mb v_L\cdot\mb v_R)(\ell^2-k_s^2/4)\right].
 \label{eq:dirac-scalar-trace}
\end{align}

The factor $8$ consists of $2$ from the projected Dirac trace and $4$ from the two central matrices.
Consequently the first nonzero oscillatory signal scales as $k_s^{5\pm4\ii\tn}$.

\subsection{Factorized Loop and Time Integrals}
\label{sec:dirac-factorized}
The spinor trace in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dirac-scalar-trace} reduces the loop momentum integration to scalar convolutions.
For each common soft branch $\eta=\pm1$, put $\mb v_{A,\eta}=\Pi^A_{1,\eta}\bm\ell +\Pi^A_{2,\eta}\mb n_A(\mb n_A\cdot\bm\ell)$, with $A=L,R$.
The dimensionless loop coefficients are defined by
\begin{align}
 &\int\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}(qK)^{2\ii\eta\tn-1}
 \left[2(\mb v_{L,\eta}\cdot\bm\ell)(\mb v_{R,\eta}\cdot\bm\ell)
 -\frac12(\mb v_{L,\eta}\cdot\mb k_s)(\mb v_{R,\eta}\cdot\mb k_s)
 -(\mb v_{L,\eta}\cdot\mb v_{R,\eta})(\ell^2-k_s^2/4)\right]\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{15mm}=\frac{k_s^{5+4\ii\eta\tn}}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}
 \sum_{i,j=1}^2\mathcal I^{ij}_t(-\ii\eta\tn,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)
 \Pi^L_{i,\eta}\Pi^R_{j,\eta}.
 \label{eq:dirac-loop-definition}
\end{align}

The resulting coefficients satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I^{11}_t=3\mathcal I^{12}_t=3\mathcal I^{21}_t.
 \label{eq:dirac-loop-relations}
\end{equation}
These three coefficients are independent of angles, and all angular dependence is carried by $\mathcal I_t^{22}$.
All explicit expressions for these coefficients are given in App.~\ref{app:dirac-expansion}.
The loop integral summed over the four orderings is
\begin{align}
 & \left[ \int_{}\frac{\di^3\mb q}{(2\pi)^3}
 \sum_{\mathrm{ord}}\mathcal T^{\mathrm{}}_{\mathrm{ord}} \right]_{\mathrm{NS}}
 \simeq \sum_{\eta=\pm} \frac{8\cdot4^{-2\ii\eta\tn}\Gamma^4(\tfrac12-\ii\eta\tn)
 k_s^{5+4\ii\eta\tn}}{(4\pi)^{7/2}k_Lk_R}
 \prod_{v=1}^4(-\tau_v)^{\ii\eta\tn}\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{30mm}\times
 \sum_{i,j=1}^2\mathcal I^{ij}_t(-\ii\eta\tn,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)
 \Pi^L_{i,\eta}\Pi^R_{j,\eta}.
 \label{eq:dirac-cut-loop}
\end{align}

The two time integrations on each side are now independent.
For the left subgraph, the leading collapsed term at $k_1\simeq k_2\simeq k_L\simeq k_{12}/2$ defines
\begin{align}
 &\sum_{\aa,\bb=\pm1}\aa\bb
 \int_{-\infty}^0\frac{\di\tau_1}{H\tau_1}\frac{\di\tau_2}{H\tau_2}
 (-\tau_1)^{\ii\eta\tn}(-\tau_2)^{\ii\eta\tn}
 G_\aa(k_1;\tau_1)G_\bb(k_2;\tau_2)
 \Pi_{i,\eta;\aa\bb}(k_{12}/2;\tau_1,\tau_2)\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{15mm}\simeq\frac{H^2}{4k_1^3k_2^3}
 k_{12}^{-2\ii\eta\tn}\mathcal P_{t,i,\eta}(\tn),
 \qquad i=1,2.
 \label{eq:dirac-time-definition}
\end{align}

Below, $\mathcal P_{t,i}\equiv\mathcal P_{t,i,+}$ denotes the positive-branch coefficient.
The right subgraph follows by $(1,2,L)\to(3,4,R)$ and $k_{12}\to k_{34}$.
The seed normalization, its closed form and the combinations giving $\mathcal P_{t,i}$ are given in App.~\ref{app:dirac-seed}.

\subsection{Oscillatory Signal}
\label{sec:dirac-clock}
We now restore color multiplicity in the final signal.
Combining the soft loop and the two hard subgraphs, setting $N_c=3$, and adding the complex-conjugate branch gives
\begin{align}
 \left[\langle\delta h^4\rangle'_t\right]_{\mathrm{NS}}
 \simeq-\frac{3g_t^4H^4k_s^5}
 {4k_1^3k_2^3k_3^3k_4^3k_{12}k_{34}}
 \left[\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)^{2\ii\tn}
 C_t(\tn,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)+\text{c.c.}\right],
 \label{eq:dirac-clock-signal}
\end{align}

where
\begin{equation}
 C_t=\frac{8\Gamma^4(\tfrac12-\ii\tn)}{(4\pi)^{7/2}}
 \sum_{i,j=1}^2\mathcal P_{t,i}\mathcal P_{t,j}
 \mathcal I^{ij}_t(-\ii\tn,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3).
 \label{eq:dirac-clock-coefficient}
\end{equation}

The angular decomposition is
\begin{align}
 C_t={}&A_{1,t}+A_{2,t}(\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_3)
       +A_{3,t}\cos^2\theta_1\cos^2\theta_3\nonumber\\
 &+B_t\sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3
  +D_t\sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3).
 \label{eq:dirac-angular}
\end{align}

The five coefficients depend only on $\tn$ and their analytic expressions are given in App.~\ref{app:dirac-coefficients}.
All nonconstant angular terms are proportional to $\mathcal P_{t,2}^2$.
At fixed angles the oscillatory bracket in \eqref{eq:dirac-clock-signal} is
\begin{equation}
 2|C_t|\cos\!\left[2\tn\log\!\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)
                   +\arg C_t\right].
 \label{eq:dirac-clock-phase}
\end{equation}
This is the leading same-branch oscillatory contribution in the collapsed $s$ channel.
Compared with the gauge-boson signals, the top-quark signal carries an additional soft suppression $k_s^2/(k_{12} k_{34})$ from \eqref{eq:dirac-cancellation}.

\label{sec:phenomenology}

Now we discuss the phenomenology of the Standard Model signal in the cosmological Higgs collider.
We illustrate the mass dependence of the nonlocal signal coefficients in Figs.~\ref{fig:gauge-coefficient-mass-scan} and~\ref{fig:top-coefficient-mass-scan}.
The left and right panels show the logarithmic magnitude and the principal phase in radians, respectively.
The horizontal variable $\nu$ in each plot denotes the mass parameter $\tilde\nu$ defined above.

    \caption{Mass dependence of the gauge-boson coefficients $C^{\mathrm{Bub}}$, $C^{\mathrm{Tri}}$ and $A_{1,g}$, $A_{2,g}$, $A_{3,g}$, $B_g$, $D_g$, with $\tilde\nu=\sqrt{m_A^2/H^2-1/4}$.

To compare with observations, we relate the Higgs fluctuations to the curvature perturbation.
At leading order, the Higgs contribution is $\zeta_h=N_h\delta h$, as in \eqref{eq:overview-delta-n}.
Assuming that the contributions from different fields are uncorrelated, the Higgs contribution to the dimensionless curvature power spectrum is
\begin{equation}
    P_\zeta^{(h)}=N_h^2P_{\delta h}
    \simeq N_h^2\left(\frac{H}{2\pi}\right)^2,
    \label{eq:phenomenology-higgs-power}
\end{equation}
where we use $P_{\delta h}\simeq(H/2\pi)^2$ in the light-Higgs approximation.
Following \cite{lu_cosmological_2020}, we define
\begin{equation}
    R_h\equiv\left(\frac{P_\zeta^{(h)}}{P_\zeta}\right)^{1/2},
    \qquad 0\leq R_h\leq1,
    \label{eq:phenomenology-higgs-fraction}
\end{equation}
where $P_\zeta$ is the total dimensionless curvature power spectrum.
The conversion coefficient then satisfies
\begin{equation}
    |N_h|\simeq\frac{2\pi}{H}P_\zeta^{1/2}R_h.
    \label{eq:phenomenology-Nh}
\end{equation}

At leading order, the contribution of each particle species to the curvature trispectrum is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        T_\zeta^{(I)}(\mb k_i)
        &\equiv\left\langle\zeta_{\mb k_1}\zeta_{\mb k_2}
        \zeta_{\mb k_3}\zeta_{\mb k_4}\right\rangle_{c,I}'\\
        &\simeq\left(\frac{2\pi}{H}\right)^4R_h^4P_\zeta^2
        \left\langle\delta h_{\mb k_1}\delta h_{\mb k_2}
        \delta h_{\mb k_3}\delta h_{\mb k_4}\right\rangle_{c,I}'.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:phenomenology-species-trispectrum}
\end{equation}
Here $I=W,Z,t$ labels the particle species, and the correlator on the right-hand side is the connected Higgs four-point function generated by species $I$.

We define the dimensionless trispectrum by
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal T(\mb k_i)
    \equiv\frac{(k_1k_2k_3k_4)^3}{(2\pi)^6P_\zeta^3k_t^3}
    \sum_{I=W,Z,t} T_\zeta^{(I)}(\mb k_i),
    \qquad k_t\equiv\sum_{i=1}^4k_i.
    \label{eq:phenomenology-dimensionless-trispectrum}
\end{equation}
In the collapsed $s$ channel, the leading oscillatory nonlocal contribution from the $W$, $Z$, and top loops takes the form
\begin{align}
    \left[\mathcal T(\mb k_i)\right]_{\mathrm{NS}}
    \underset{k_s\ll k_i}{\simeq}{}&
    t_{\mathrm{NL}}^W\left(\frac{k_s}{k_t}\right)^3
    \cos\!\left[2\tilde\nu_W\log\!\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)
    +\varphi_W\right]\nonumber\\
    &+t_{\mathrm{NL}}^Z\left(\frac{k_s}{k_t}\right)^3
    \cos\!\left[2\tilde\nu_Z\log\!\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)
    +\varphi_Z\right]\nonumber\\
    &+t_{\mathrm{NL}}^t\frac{k_t^2}{k_{12}k_{34}}
    \left(\frac{k_s}{k_t}\right)^5
    \cos\!\left[2\tilde\nu_t\log\!\left(\frac{k_s^2}{4k_{12}k_{34}}\right)
    +\varphi_t\right].
    \label{eq:phenomenology-clock-shape}
\end{align}
Both $t_{\mathrm{NL}}^I$ and $\varphi_I$ depend on the external momentum directions.
Here $\tilde\nu_{W,Z}=\sqrt{m_{W,Z}^2/H^2-1/4}$, and $\tilde\nu_t=m_t/H$.
The nonnegative coefficients $t_{\mathrm{NL}}^I$ define dimensionless clock amplitudes, in analogy with the bispectrum clock amplitude in \cite{lu_cosmological_2020}, while $\varphi_I$ are the corresponding phases.

To make this relation explicit, we define the complex combinations
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathcal C_W\equiv{}&C^{\mathrm{Bub}}(\tilde\nu_W)
        -2g^2\frac{h_0^2}{H^2}C^{\mathrm{Tri}}(\tilde\nu_W)
        +g^4\frac{h_0^4}{H^4}C^{\mathrm{Box}}(\tilde\nu_W),\\
        \mathcal C_Z\equiv{}&C^{\mathrm{Bub}}(\tilde\nu_Z)
        -\frac{2g^2}{c_W^2}\frac{h_0^2}{H^2}C^{\mathrm{Tri}}(\tilde\nu_Z)
        +\frac{g^4}{c_W^4}\frac{h_0^4}{H^4}C^{\mathrm{Box}}(\tilde\nu_Z).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:phenomenology-gauge-clock-combinations}
\end{equation}

With the normalization in \eqref{eq:phenomenology-clock-shape}, the clock amplitudes and phases are
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        t_{\mathrm{NL}}^W
        &=\frac{g^4R_h^4}{128\pi^2P_\zeta}|\mathcal C_W|,
        &\qquad \varphi_W&=\arg\mathcal C_W,\\
        t_{\mathrm{NL}}^Z
        &=\frac{g^4R_h^4}{256\pi^2c_W^4P_\zeta}|\mathcal C_Z|,
        &\varphi_Z&=\arg\mathcal C_Z,\\
        t_{\mathrm{NL}}^t
        &=\frac{3y_t^4R_h^4}{32\pi^2P_\zeta}|C_t|,
        &\varphi_t&=\arg(-C_t).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:phenomenology-clock-amplitudes-phases}
\end{equation}

Since the clock amplitudes $t_{\mathrm{NL}}^I$ depend on the external momentum configuration, we first examine their angular distributions.
For observational purposes, we are particularly interested in the configurations that maximize these amplitudes.
We fix the mass parameter to $\tilde\nu=1$ and consider three azimuthal angles, $\phi_3=0,\pi/2,\pi$.

\section{Useful Functions}
\label{app:useful-functions}
We summarize the special-function conventions used in this work.

\paragraph{Gamma functions}
Products and ratios of Euler $\Gamma$ functions are written in the compact forms
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma[a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n]
    \equiv\Gamma(a_1)\Gamma(a_2)\cdots\Gamma(a_n),
    \label{eq:useful-gamma-product}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma\!\left[\begin{matrix}
        a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n\\
        b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_m
    \end{matrix}\right]
    \equiv\frac{\Gamma(a_1)\Gamma(a_2)\cdots\Gamma(a_n)}
    {\Gamma(b_1)\Gamma(b_2)\cdots\Gamma(b_m)}.
    \label{eq:useful-gamma-ratio}
\end{equation}
The Pochhammer symbol is defined by
\begin{equation}
    (a)_n\equiv\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(a)}.
    \label{eq:useful-pochhammer}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Hypergeometric functions}
The generalized hypergeometric function is specified by the series
\begin{equation}
    {}_p\mathrm{F}_q\!\left[\begin{matrix}
        a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_p\\
        b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_q
    \end{matrix}\middle|z\right]
    \equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
    \frac{(a_1)_n(a_2)_n\cdots(a_p)_n}
    {(b_1)_n(b_2)_n\cdots(b_q)_n}\frac{z^n}{n!},
    \label{eq:useful-hypergeometric}
\end{equation}
within its region of convergence, and by analytic continuation elsewhere.
The regularized hypergeometric function is denoted by
\begin{equation}
    {}_p\widetilde{\mathrm{F}}_q\!\left[\begin{matrix}
        a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_p\\
        b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_q
    \end{matrix}\middle|z\right]
    \equiv\frac{1}{\Gamma(b_1)\Gamma(b_2)\cdots\Gamma(b_q)}
    {}_p\mathrm{F}_q\!\left[\begin{matrix}
        a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_p\\
        b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_q
    \end{matrix}\middle|z\right].
    \label{eq:useful-regularized-hypergeometric}
\end{equation}
Differentiation of ${}_p\widetilde{\mathrm{F}}_q$ with respect to a lower parameter $b_j$ is understood to follow the Leibniz rule, acting on the $b_j$ dependence of both the reciprocal Gamma prefactor and the ordinary hypergeometric function ${}_p\mathrm{F}_q$.
We also use the dressed hypergeometric function
\begin{equation}
    {}_p\mathcal{F}_q\!\left[\begin{matrix}
        a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_p\\
        b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_q
    \end{matrix}\middle|z\right]
    \equiv\Gamma\!\left[\begin{matrix}
        a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_p\\
        b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_q
    \end{matrix}\right]
    {}_p\mathrm{F}_q\!\left[\begin{matrix}
        a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_p\\
        b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_q
    \end{matrix}\middle|z\right].
    \label{eq:useful-dressed-hypergeometric}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Loop Integral Formulae}
\label{app:appendix}
\label{app:loop-integrals}
The identity $z=(K^2-q^2-k_s^2)/(2qk_s)$ reduces all angular moments in \eqref{eq:fr-definition} to the scalar integral $f_0$:
\begin{equation}
    f_r(\alpha,\beta)=\frac1{2^r}
    \sum_{u+v+w=r}\frac{r!}{u!v!w!}(-1)^{v+w}
    f_0\left(\alpha+\frac r2-v,\beta-u\right),
    \label{eq:fr-finite-sum}
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over nonnegative integers.
For the third and fourth moments this gives
\begin{align}
    f_3(\alpha,\beta)=\frac18\Big[&
    f_0(\alpha+\tfrac32,\beta-3)
    -3f_0(\alpha+\tfrac32,\beta-2)
    -3f_0(\alpha+\tfrac12,\beta-2)\nonumber\\
    &+3f_0(\alpha+\tfrac32,\beta-1)
    +6f_0(\alpha+\tfrac12,\beta-1)
    +3f_0(\alpha-\tfrac12,\beta-1)\nonumber\\
    &-f_0(\alpha+\tfrac32,\beta)
    -3f_0(\alpha+\tfrac12,\beta)
    -3f_0(\alpha-\tfrac12,\beta)
    -f_0(\alpha-\tfrac32,\beta)\Big],
    \label{eq:f3}\\[1mm]
    f_4(\alpha,\beta)=\frac1{16}\Big[&
    f_0(\alpha+2,\beta-4)
    -4f_0(\alpha+2,\beta-3)-4f_0(\alpha+1,\beta-3)\nonumber\\
    &+6f_0(\alpha+2,\beta-2)+12f_0(\alpha+1,\beta-2)
    +6f_0(\alpha,\beta-2)\nonumber\\
    &-4f_0(\alpha+2,\beta-1)-12f_0(\alpha+1,\beta-1)
    -12f_0(\alpha,\beta-1)\nonumber\\
    &-4f_0(\alpha-1,\beta-1)
    +f_0(\alpha+2,\beta)+4f_0(\alpha+1,\beta)\nonumber\\
    &+6f_0(\alpha,\beta)+4f_0(\alpha-1,\beta)
    +f_0(\alpha-2,\beta)\Big].
    \label{eq:f4}
\end{align}

\subsection{Bubble Coefficients}
\label{app:bubble-polarizations}
For $x=-\ii\eta\tilde\nu$, the relative weight of a longitudinal nonlocal propagator is
\begin{equation}
    \beta(x)\equiv\frac{(x+1/2)^2}{1/4-x^2}
    =\frac{1+2x}{1-2x}.
    \label{eq:bubble-longitudinal-weight}
\end{equation}
Define $\mathcal I_{\sigma_1\sigma_2}(x)$ by restricting the loop in \eqref{eq:bubble-cut-loop} to the soft line polarizations $(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)$ and replacing $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Bub}}$ on its right-hand side by $\mathcal I_{\sigma_1\sigma_2}$.
The independent coefficients are
\begin{align}
    \mathcal I_{LL}(x)
    &=[\beta(x)]^2
    \left[\mathcal J_0(2,2;x)+2\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_2(0,2;x)\right],
    \label{eq:bubble-ILL}\\
    \mathcal I_{L+}(x)
    &=\frac{\beta(x)}2
    \left[\mathcal J_0(0,2;x)-\mathcal J_2(0,2;x)\right],
    \label{eq:bubble-ILplus}\\
    \mathcal I_{++}(x)
    &=\frac14\left[
    \mathcal J_0(0,0;x)+\mathcal J_0(2,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_2(0,2;x)\right.\nonumber\\
    &\hspace{12mm}\left.
    +2\mathcal J_0(1,1;x)+2\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)
    +2\mathcal J_1(0,1;x)\right],
    \label{eq:bubble-Iplusplus}\\
    \mathcal I_{+-}(x)
    &=\frac14\left[
    \mathcal J_0(0,0;x)+\mathcal J_0(2,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_2(0,2;x)\right.\nonumber\\
    &\hspace{12mm}\left.
    -2\mathcal J_0(1,1;x)+2\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)
    -2\mathcal J_1(0,1;x)\right].
    \label{eq:bubble-Iplusminus}
\end{align}
The remaining entries satisfy $\mathcal I_{--}=\mathcal I_{++}$, $\mathcal I_{-+}=\mathcal I_{+-}$, and $\mathcal I_{L-}=\mathcal I_{+L}=\mathcal I_{-L}=\mathcal I_{L+}$.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal I_{\mathrm{Bub}}
    =\mathcal I_{LL}+4\mathcal I_{L+}
    +2\mathcal I_{++}+2\mathcal I_{+-},
    \label{eq:bubble-channel-sum}
\end{equation}
which reduces to \eqref{eq:bubble-polarization-sum}.

For the $\eta=+1$ branch, the fully reduced sum is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal I_{\mathrm{Bub}}(-\ii\tilde\nu)
    =\frac{3\cdot2^{-4-4\ii\tilde\nu}(3\ii-2\tilde\nu)^2
    \Gamma[-\tfrac32-2\ii\tilde\nu,\,3+2\ii\tilde\nu]
    \sinh^2(\pi\tilde\nu)}
    {\pi[1+(3\ii-2\tilde\nu)\tilde\nu]^2}.
    \label{eq:bubble-I-closed}
\end{equation}
Substituting this into \eqref{eq:bubble-coefficient} gives
\begin{align}
    C^{\mathrm{Bub}}(\tilde\nu)
    ={}&\frac{3(\tilde\nu-\ii)(\tilde\nu-2\ii)^4\sinh^2(\pi\tilde\nu)}
    {256\pi^{7/2}\tilde\nu^2(2\tilde\nu-\ii)}
    \nonumber\\
    &\times\Gamma\!\bigl[
    -2-\ii\tilde\nu,\,-2-\ii\tilde\nu,\,
    \tfrac52+\ii\tilde\nu,\,\tfrac52+\ii\tilde\nu,\,
    -\tfrac32-2\ii\tilde\nu,\,1+2\ii\tilde\nu\bigr].
    \label{eq:bubble-C-closed}
\end{align}
For real $\tilde\nu>0$, the opposite-branch expression is its complex conjugate.

\subsection{Triangle Coefficients}
\label{app:triangle-coefficients}
\subsubsection{Polarization sums}
Let $x=-\ii\eta\tilde\nu$ and let $\beta(x)$ be the longitudinal weight in \eqref{eq:bubble-longitudinal-weight}.
For a hard line with longitudinal mode, summing the nine soft-polarization combinations gives
\begin{align}
    \mathcal I^L_{\mathrm{Tri}}(x,\theta)
    ={}&[\beta(x)]^2\left\{
    \frac{\sin^2\theta}{2}\left[
    \mathcal J_0(2,2;x)+\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)
    -\mathcal J_2(2,2;x)-\mathcal J_3(1,2;x)\right]\right.
    \nonumber\\
    &\hspace{15mm}\left.+\cos^2\theta\left[
    \mathcal J_2(2,2;x)+\mathcal J_3(1,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)+\mathcal J_2(0,2;x)\right]\right\}
    \nonumber\\
    &+\frac{\beta(x)(1+\cos^2\theta)}2
    \left[\mathcal J_0(0,2;x)-\mathcal J_2(0,2;x)\right]
    \nonumber\\
    &+\frac{\sin^2\theta}{2}\left[
    \mathcal J_2(2,2;x)+\mathcal J_3(1,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)+\mathcal J_2(0,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_0(0,0;x)\right]
    \nonumber\\
    &+\cos^2\theta\left[
    \mathcal J_0(2,2;x)-\mathcal J_2(2,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)-\mathcal J_3(1,2;x)\right].
    \label{eq:triangle-IL-explicit}
\end{align}
The terms proportional to $\beta^2$, $\beta$, and $1$ arise from two longitudinal, mixed, and two transverse soft lines, respectively.

For a hard line with helicity $+$, the corresponding expression is
\begin{align}
    \mathcal I^+_{\mathrm{Tri}}(x,\theta)
    ={}&[\beta(x)]^2\left\{
    \frac{1+\cos^2\theta}{4}\left[
    \mathcal J_0(2,2;x)-\mathcal J_2(2,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)-\mathcal J_3(1,2;x)\right]\right.
    \nonumber\\
    &\hspace{15mm}\left.+\frac{\sin^2\theta}{2}\left[
    \mathcal J_2(2,2;x)+\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_3(1,2;x)+\mathcal J_2(0,2;x)\right]\right\}
    \nonumber\\
    &+\frac{\beta(x)(2+\sin^2\theta)}4
    \left[\mathcal J_0(0,2;x)-\mathcal J_2(0,2;x)\right]
    \nonumber\\
    &+\frac{1+\cos^2\theta}{4}\left[
    \mathcal J_2(2,2;x)+\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)

    \nonumber\\

    \mathcal J_0(2,2;x)-\mathcal J_2(2,2;x)
    +\mathcal J_1(1,2;x)-\mathcal J_3(1,2;x)\right].
    \label{eq:triangle-IP-explicit}
\end{align}
Reducing the Gamma functions gives the same result for all three hard-line polarizations:

    {\pi[1+(3\ii-2\tilde\nu)\tilde\nu]^2}.

Summing over the hard-line polarizations reproduces $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Bub}}$ by polarization completeness.

\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Time integral coefficients}
For the scalar propagator of \eqref{eq:scalar-propagator}, the double-folded seed is defined by

 \label{eq:gauge-finite-masters}

 w&=e^{\pi\tilde\nu},\qquad

 \label{eq:gauge-finite-T}
\end{align}

 \mathcal D_g={}&\frac{4R}{(1-2\ii\tilde\nu)(3+2\ii\tilde\nu)}

 \label{eq:gauge-finite-D}
\end{align}

 \label{eq:gauge-P2-finite}
\end{align}

    \mathcal I^{\lambda_1\lambda_3}_{\mathrm{Box}},
    \label{eq:box-channel-groups}
\end{align}
together with $\mathcal I^{LL}_{\mathrm{Box}}$.
Here $H$ in a polarization superscript denotes the sum over transverse helicities.

    \nonumber\\
    &\hspace{24mm}

    \mathcal I^{LL}_{\mathrm{Box}}
    +\frac13\mathcal P^{(2),+}_{+}
    [2\mathcal P^{(2),L}_{+}+\mathcal P^{(2),+}_{+}]

\end{align}

 F_{0,\eta;\aa\bb}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)

 \label{eq:dirac-hard-kernel-traces}

 \label{eq:dirac-hard-kernels}

 -\frac{\ii k}{1+2\ii\eta\tn}

  +\tau_2F_{1,\eta;\bb\aa}(k;\tau_2,\tau_1)],\nonumber\\

 &=\left.(p\partial_p-1)F_{0,\eta;\aa\bb}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2)\right|_{p=k}.
 \label{eq:dirac-Pi}
\end{align}

For the loop coefficients defined in \eqref{eq:dirac-loop-definition}, set $x=-\ii\tn$.
The common argument $x$ of $\mathcal I^{ij}_{t}$ and $\mathcal J_r$, and the angular arguments of $\mathcal I^{ij}_{t}$, are suppressed below.
Azimuthal averaging and the scalar-convolution identities give
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I^{11}_{t}
 &=3\mathcal I^{12}_{t}=3\mathcal I^{21}_{t},
 \nonumber\\
 \mathcal I^{12}_{t}
 &=\frac12[\mathcal J_0(3,1)-\mathcal J_2(3,1)
 +\mathcal J_1(2,1)-\mathcal J_3(2,1)]
 +\frac14[\mathcal J_0(1,1)-\mathcal J_2(1,1)],
 \nonumber\\
 \mathcal I^{22}_{t}
 &=\frac14[2\mathcal J_4(3,1)-3\mathcal J_2(3,1)+\mathcal J_0(3,1)
 +\mathcal J_3(2,1)-\mathcal J_1(2,1)]\nonumber\\
 &\quad+\frac14\Big[-6\mathcal J_4(3,1)+7\mathcal J_2(3,1)-\mathcal J_0(3,1)
 +5\mathcal J_1(2,1)-5\mathcal J_3(2,1)\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{22mm}+\mathcal J_0(1,1)-\mathcal J_2(1,1)\Big]
 (\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_3-3\cos^2\theta_1\cos^2\theta_3)\nonumber\\
 &\quad+\frac1{16}\Big[-16\mathcal J_4(3,1)+14\mathcal J_2(3,1)-2\mathcal J_0(3,1)
 +10\mathcal J_1(2,1)-18\mathcal J_3(2,1)\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{22mm}+2\mathcal J_0(1,1)-7\mathcal J_2(1,1)-\mathcal J_1(0,1)\Big]
 \sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3\nonumber\\
 &\quad+\frac14[\mathcal J_4(3,1)-\mathcal J_2(3,1)
 +\mathcal J_3(2,1)-\mathcal J_1(2,1)]
 \sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3).
 \label{eq:dirac-loop-reduction}
\end{align}
The finite sum \eqref{eq:fr-finite-sum} and the Gamma recurrence give the closed form below, with the common factor
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal G_{t}(\tn)
 =\Gamma\!\left[\begin{matrix}
 -\tfrac12-2\ii\tn,\,1+\ii\tn,\,1+\ii\tn\\
 2+2\ii\tn,\,\tfrac12-\ii\tn,\,\tfrac12-\ii\tn
 \end{matrix}\right].
 \label{eq:dirac-loop-Gamma}
\end{equation}
For $x=-\ii\tn$, the fully reduced coefficients are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I^{12}_{t}(-\ii\tn)
 &=-\mathcal G_{t}\,
 \frac{(1+\ii\tn)(1+2\ii\tn)}
 {4(3+2\ii\tn)(5+4\ii\tn)(3+4\ii\tn)},
 \nonumber\\
 \mathcal I^{22}_{t}(-\ii\tn,\theta_1,\theta_3,\phi_3)
 &=-\frac{\mathcal G_{t}(1+\ii\tn)}
 {4(5+2\ii\tn)(3+2\ii\tn)(5+4\ii\tn)(3+4\ii\tn)}
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\times\Big\{(2+\ii\tn)(1+2\ii\tn)\nonumber\\
 &\qquad-(1+\ii\tn)(1+2\ii\tn)
 [\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_3-3\cos^2\theta_1\cos^2\theta_3]\nonumber\\
 &\qquad-\frac12(1+2\ii\tn)(3+2\ii\tn)
 \sin(2\theta_1)\sin(2\theta_3)\cos\phi_3\nonumber\\
 &\qquad+(2+\ii\tn)(3+2\ii\tn)
 \sin^2\theta_1\sin^2\theta_3\cos(2\phi_3)\Big\}.
 \label{eq:dirac-loop-closed}
\end{align}
The $\eta=-1$ branch follows by complex conjugation.

\subsection{Whittaker Seed and Time Integrals}
\label{app:dirac-seed}
We denote the Whittaker seed $\mathcal I^{(h)}$ of Ref.~\cite{qin_closed-form_2023} by $\mathcal K$.
For equal external energies $k$, internal magnitude $p=rk$, and $u=2r/(1+r)$, using the scalar kernels \eqref{eq:dirac-scalar-kernels}, the integral seed is defined by
\begin{align}
 \mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;p_1p_2}(u,u)
 \equiv{}&(-\aa\bb)p^{3+p_1+p_2}
 \int_{-\infty}^0\di\tau_1\di\tau_2\,
 (-\tau_1)^{p_1}(-\tau_2)^{p_2}
 e^{\ii\aa k\tau_1+\ii\bb k\tau_2}
 D^{(\kappa)}_{\aa\bb}(p;\tau_1,\tau_2).
 \label{eq:dirac-K-definition}
\end{align}

We define the dimensionless variables $x_1=-k\tau_1$, $x_2=-k\tau_2$, and the integral seed becomes:
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^\infty\di x_1\di x_2\,x_1^{p_1}x_2^{p_2}e^{-\ii\aa x_1-\ii\bb x_2}
 kD^{(\kappa)}_{\aa\bb}(rk;-x_1/k,-x_2/k)
 =-\aa\bb\,r^{-3-p_1-p_2}\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;p_1p_2}(u,u).
 \label{eq:dirac-K-scaling}
\end{equation}

At $r=1$, write $\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;p_1p_2} \equiv\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;p_1p_2}(1,1)$.
The closed expressions are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal K_{\pm\mp}^{\kappa;p_1p_2}
 &=\frac{e^{\mp\ii\pi(p_1-p_2)/2+\ii\pi\kappa}}{2^{3+p_1+p_2}}
 \Gamma\!\left[\begin{matrix}
 \tfrac32+p_1-\ii\tn,\,\tfrac32+p_1+\ii\tn,\,
 \tfrac32+p_2-\ii\tn,\,\tfrac32+p_2+\ii\tn\\
 2+p_1\pm\kappa,\,2+p_2\mp\kappa
 \end{matrix}\right],
 \label{eq:dirac-K-opposite}\\
 \mathcal K_{\pm\pm}^{\kappa;p_1p_2}
 &=\frac{\mp\ii e^{\mp\ii\pi(p_1+p_2)/2}}{2^{3+p_1+p_2}}
 \Gamma[\tfrac32+p_1\pm\ii\tn,\tfrac32+p_2\pm\ii\tn]
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\times\left\{
 \frac{e^{2\ii\pi\kappa}+e^{-2\pi\tn}}{2\pi}
 \Gamma\!\left[\begin{matrix}
 \tfrac32+p_1\mp\ii\tn,\,\tfrac32+p_2\mp\ii\tn,\,
 \tfrac12\pm\kappa-\ii\tn,\,\tfrac12\pm\kappa+\ii\tn\\
 2+p_1\pm\kappa,\,2+p_2\pm\kappa
 \end{matrix}\right]
 \right.\nonumber\\
 &\qquad\left.
 \pm\ii\Gamma(3+p_1+p_2)
 {}_3\widetilde F_2\!\left[
 \begin{matrix}3+p_1+p_2,\ \tfrac12\mp\kappa\pm\ii\tn,\ 1\\
 \tfrac52+p_1\pm\ii\tn,\ \tfrac52+p_2\pm\ii\tn
 \end{matrix}\middle|1\right]\right\}.
 \label{eq:dirac-K-same}
\end{align}

Using $F_j=F_{j,+}$ from \eqref{eq:dirac-hard-kernels}, define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_j(q_1,q_2;r)
 ={}&\sum_{\aa,\bb=\pm1}\aa\bb
 \int_0^\infty\di x_1\di x_2\,
 x_1^{q_1-1}x_2^{q_2-1}
 (1+\ii\aa x_1)(1+\ii\bb x_2)e^{-\ii\aa x_1-\ii\bb x_2}
 \nonumber\\
 &\times F_{j;\aa\bb}(rk;-x_1/k,-x_2/k),\qquad j=0,1.
 \label{eq:dirac-H-definition}
\end{align}
Scale invariance makes the right-hand side independent of $k$.
Expanding the external polynomials with indices $m,n=0,1$, put $p_1=q_1-3/2+m$, $p_2=q_2-3/2+n$.
Integration by parts sends $x_1\partial_{x_1}$ to $-(p_1+1)+\ii\aa x_1$.
Using the definition \eqref{eq:dirac-K-definition} therefore gives directly
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_0(q_1,q_2;r)
 ={}&\ii
 \sum_{\aa,\bb=\pm1}\sum_{m,n=0}^1
 (\ii\aa)^m(\ii\bb)^n r^{-3-p_1-p_2}
 \Big\{(p_1+\tfrac32+\ii\tn)
 [\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{-;p_1p_2}-\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{+;p_1p_2}]
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{23mm}
 +\frac{\ii(\aa-r)}r\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{+;p_1+1p_2}
 -\frac{\ii(\aa+r)}r\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{-;p_1+1p_2}\Big\},
 \label{eq:dirac-H0-K}\\
 \mathcal H_1(q_1,q_2;r)
 ={}&\ii
 \sum_{\aa,\bb=\pm1}\sum_{m,n=0}^1
 (\ii\aa)^m(\ii\bb)^n r^{-3-p_1-p_2}
 \Big\{(p_1+\tfrac32-\ii\tn)
 [\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{-;p_1p_2}+\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{+;p_1p_2}]
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{23mm}
 -\frac{\ii(\aa-r)}r\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{+;p_1+1p_2}
 -\frac{\ii(\aa+r)}r\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{-;p_1+1p_2}\Big\}.
 \label{eq:dirac-H1-K}
\end{align}
Every seed here has arguments $(u,u)$, and its superscript $\pm$ means $\kappa=\pm1/2$.

Differentiating the seed definition and integrating the resulting time derivatives by parts gives
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\di}{\di r}
 \mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;p_1p_2}(u(r),u(r))\right|_{r=1}
 =\ii\aa\,\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;p_1+1p_2}(1,1)
 +\ii\bb\,\mathcal K_{\aa\bb}^{\kappa;p_1p_2+1}(1,1).
 \label{eq:dirac-K-derivative}
\end{equation}
Both the explicit powers of $r$ and the factors $(\aa\pm r)/r$ in \eqref{eq:dirac-H0-K} must also be differentiated.

Then the coefficients used in the main text are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal P_{t,1}
 &=2^{2\ii\tn}\underset{\delta=0}{\operatorname{FP}}\left[
 \mathcal H_0(\ii\tn+\delta,\ii\tn+\delta;1)
 +\frac{2\ii}{1+2\ii\tn}
 \mathcal H_1(1+\ii\tn+\delta,\ii\tn+\delta;1)\right],
 \nonumber\\
 \mathcal P_{t,2}
 &=2^{2\ii\tn}\underset{\delta=0}{\operatorname{FP}}
 \left.(\partial_r-1)
 \mathcal H_0(\ii\tn+\delta,\ii\tn+\delta;r)\right|_{r=1},
 \label{eq:dirac-P-from-seed}
\end{align}
The factor $2^{2\ii\tn}$ converts the seed normalization at $k_L$ to the $k_{12}\simeq2k_L$ convention in \eqref{eq:dirac-time-definition}.

After combining the SK branches, the finite coefficients can be expressed using a single ordinary hypergeometric function,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal F_t(\tn)&={}_3F_2\!\left[
 \begin{matrix}1,\ 1+\ii\tn,\ 1+2\ii\tn\\
 2+2\ii\tn,\ 2+2\ii\tn\end{matrix}\middle|1\right],
 \nonumber\\
 \mathcal E_t(\tn)&=
 \frac{\pi^2}{\cosh(\pi\tn)}
 \Gamma\!\left[\begin{matrix}
 1+2\ii\tn\\
 1+\ii\tn,\,1+\ii\tn
 \end{matrix}\right].
 \label{eq:dirac-finite-master}
\end{align}
The resulting expressions are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal P_{t,1}={}&\Gamma(1+2\ii\tn)\cosh(\pi\tn)
 \left[\mathcal E_t-\frac{2\mathcal F_t}{(1+2\ii\tn)^2}
 -\frac{(5+2\ii\tn)(4+9\ii\tn-4\tn^2)}
 {2(1+\ii\tn)(2+\ii\tn)(1+2\ii\tn)}\right],
 \nonumber\\
 \mathcal P_{t,2}={}&\Gamma(1+2\ii\tn)\cosh(\pi\tn)
 \left[\frac{2\mathcal F_t}{1+2\ii\tn}
 -(1+2\ii\tn)\mathcal E_t
 +\frac{\ii\tn(5+2\ii\tn)}
 {2(1+\ii\tn)(2+\ii\tn)}\right].
 \label{eq:dirac-P-explicit-finite}
\end{align}

\subsection{Angular Coefficients}
\label{app:dirac-coefficients}
Using $\mathcal G_t$ defined in \eqref{eq:dirac-loop-Gamma}, define the common soft factor
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_{t}(\tn)=
 \frac{\Gamma(\tfrac12-\ii\tn)^4
 \mathcal G_t(\tn)}
 {(4\pi)^{7/2}(5+2\ii\tn)(3+2\ii\tn)
 (5+4\ii\tn)(3+4\ii\tn)}.
 \label{eq:dirac-common-factor}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:dirac-loop-closed} into \eqref{eq:dirac-clock-coefficient} and using the angular decomposition \eqref{eq:dirac-angular} gives
\begin{align}
 A_{1,t}&=-2\mathcal B_{t}(1+\ii\tn)(1+2\ii\tn)
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\times\left[(5+2\ii\tn)(3\mathcal P_{t,1}^2
 +2\mathcal P_{t,1}\mathcal P_{t,2})
 +(2+\ii\tn)\mathcal P_{t,2}^2\right],\nonumber\\
 A_{2,t}&=2\mathcal B_{t}\mathcal P_{t,2}^2
 (1+\ii\tn)^2(1+2\ii\tn),
 \qquad A_{3,t}=-3A_{2,t},\nonumber\\
 B_{t}&=\mathcal B_{t}\mathcal P_{t,2}^2
       (1+\ii\tn)(1+2\ii\tn)(3+2\ii\tn),\nonumber\\
 D_{t}&=-2\mathcal B_{t}\mathcal P_{t,2}^2
       (1+\ii\tn)(2+\ii\tn)(3+2\ii\tn).
 \label{eq:dirac-angular-closed}
\end{align}

The remaining time coefficients can also be substituted explicitly.
Using \eqref{eq:dirac-finite-master}, set
\begin{align}
 \mathcal Z_t&=\frac{2\mathcal F_t}{1+2\ii\tilde\nu}
 -(1+2\ii\tilde\nu)\mathcal E_t
 +\frac{\ii\tilde\nu(5+2\ii\tilde\nu)}
 {2(1+\ii\tilde\nu)(2+\ii\tilde\nu)},\nonumber\\
 \mathcal N_t&=\mathcal B_t\Gamma^2(1+2\ii\tilde\nu)
 \cosh^2(\pi\tilde\nu)\nonumber\\
 &=\frac{2^{4\ii\tilde\nu}\pi}
 {(4\pi)^{7/2}
 (5+2\ii\tilde\nu)(3+2\ii\tilde\nu)
 (5+4\ii\tilde\nu)(3+4\ii\tilde\nu)}
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\times\Gamma\!\left[\begin{matrix}
 -\tfrac12-2\ii\tilde\nu,\,1+\ii\tilde\nu,\,1+\ii\tilde\nu,\,
 1+\ii\tilde\nu,\,1+\ii\tilde\nu\\
 2+2\ii\tilde\nu
 \end{matrix}\right].
 \label{eq:top-angular-explicit-common}
\end{align}
Gamma duplication and reflection give the second form of $\mathcal N_t$.
The five coefficients then become
\begin{align}
 A_{1,t}={}&-\frac{2(1+\ii\tilde\nu)}{1+2\ii\tilde\nu}\mathcal N_t
 \Bigg[(7-9\ii\tilde\nu-4\tilde\nu^2-4\ii\tilde\nu^3)\mathcal Z_t^2
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{18mm}
 +\frac{8(1+\tilde\nu^2)(5+2\ii\tilde\nu)^2}{2+\ii\tilde\nu}\mathcal Z_t
 +\frac{12(1+\ii\tilde\nu)^2(5+2\ii\tilde\nu)^3}{(2+\ii\tilde\nu)^2}
 \Bigg],\nonumber\\
 A_{2,t}={}&2(1+\ii\tilde\nu)^2(1+2\ii\tilde\nu)\mathcal N_t\mathcal Z_t^2,
 \qquad A_{3,t}=-3A_{2,t},\nonumber\\
 B_t={}&(1+\ii\tilde\nu)(1+2\ii\tilde\nu)(3+2\ii\tilde\nu)
 \mathcal N_t\mathcal Z_t^2,\nonumber\\
 D_t={}&-2(1+\ii\tilde\nu)(2+\ii\tilde\nu)(3+2\ii\tilde\nu)
 \mathcal N_t\mathcal Z_t^2.
 \label{eq:top-angular-explicit-finite}
\end{align}
Thus all five functions depend on the single hypergeometric function $\mathcal F_t$, together with Gamma and elementary functions.
